# Messages and templates

Muster shows every Alert Group as one **Root message** in each Destination, and edits it as the Alert Group changes.
Without any setup the message is complete and compact, so that a phone shows what a person needs to act: the number
and title with the status as a word, a few fields, the summary and the description, one line of key labels, the
Alerts by the label that tells them apart, the notices, who has it, the buttons and the links. This page is for the
Admin who wants to change what it says: [the labels it shows](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/messages-and-templates.md#labels-in-the-message), the template of a Route, whom
a message mentions, and the links it carries.

## The default message

From 0.2.0 every Root message has this layout, here as Telegram shows it; each messenger lays it out in its own form:

```text
#1287 KubernetesContainerOOMKilled — 🟠 Acknowledged
Severity: warning
Route: Infrastructure
Teams: payments
Started: 2026-10-04 03:12 UTC
Container killed for memory
The container exceeded its limit.
system=kubernetes · team=payments · namespace=checkout
pod: checkout-api-5b2c1, checkout-api-7d9f4, checkout-worker-8e6a0
Acknowledged by Olga
Open in Muster · Runbook · Source
[Unack] [Resolve] [Snooze 1 h] …
```

1. **The title**: `#N` and the Alert Group's title, linked to its page, and the status as a word — Firing,
   Acknowledged, Snoozed or Resolved. The status colour stays: as the colour of the message in Mattermost and Slack,
   and as an emoji beside the word in Telegram.
2. **The fields**: Severity, the Alert Group's Severity level; Route; Teams, left out when the Alert Group has none;
   and Started, absolute in the Organization's time zone.
3. **The summary and the description**: the `summary` and `description` annotations of the first Alert that has each,
   as plain text, each left out when no Alert has it.
4. **The key labels**: the labels of the Route's [Labels in the message](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/messages-and-templates.md#labels-in-the-message), on one line.
5. **The Alerts**, when the Alert Group has more than one: named by the label that tells them apart — the first of
   the Organization's Instance labels whose values differ (`pod`, `instance`, `container`, `endpoint` by default),
   otherwise the first label, by name, whose values differ — with up to five values (`message.compact_alerts_listed`),
   firing Alerts first and resolved ones struck through, then "…and K more".
6. **The notices** that apply, such as "Nobody has taken this" or the Reopen count.
7. **The footer**: while the Alert Group is firing and [Escalation chains](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/escalation-chains.md#assignees)
   have Paged people, its Assignees, each with the Team of their chain — "Assignees: @ivan (devops), @petr (payments)",
   as Mentions where their account is linked in the Destination's Identity space and by name otherwise; after an
   acknowledgement, who acknowledged it, the Owner, named without a Team and followed by "· Escalation chains stopped"
   when the acknowledgement stopped running chains — "Acknowledged by @ivan · Escalation chains stopped"; otherwise
   who snoozed or resolved the Alert Group, or why Muster resolved it. An edit that shows the Assignees notifies nobody;
   a new message that carries them may.
8. **The buttons** of the Destination's set.
9. **The links**: "Open in Muster" first, then the runbook, the source and the links of Link rules.

Every other label and annotation is on the Alert Group page, one click away through "Open in Muster".

**A bridge**
:   The envelope of the Bridge protocol: the status with its colour and emoji, the title, the link, the buttons of the

    and, beside it, `layout`, the same content as structured data, so that a bridge can lay it out natively without
    parsing markup. The Bridge tab shows the `layout` as JSON beside the `body`. A bridge renders either of the two;
    how, is in [Render the structured layout](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/write-a-bridge.md#render-the-structured-layout), and what

A message longer than its messenger allows — 4,096 characters in Telegram, 3,000 in a Slack section, 16,383 in
Mattermost — loses its description first, then its Alerts, then its summary, then its key labels; the title, the
status, the fields, the footer, the buttons and the link to Muster always stay. Through a bridge, the limit is the
longest `body` that the bridge declares, and the same parts go first. The `layout` that a bridge receives loses the same
parts as its `body`.

A Route's own templates replace only part of this. A **Root message** template replaces items 3 to 5, the summary and
the description, the key labels and the Alerts; an **Alert line** template replaces the value of the label that tells
the Alerts apart on each line of item 5; the Fallback template, which shows when a template fails, lists every label
instead of items 3 to 5. The title, the fields, the notices, the footer, the buttons and the links stay Muster's in
every case, and so does the length rule above. A message with a template of its own, or with the Fallback template, has
no `layout` through a bridge, since part of its content exists only in the `body`: the preview's Bridge tab says so.

## Labels in the message

"Labels in the message" (`route.message_labels`) chooses the labels of item 4. Choose them in **Routes → a Route →
Message → Labels in the message**: the labels are chips that you remove with their ×, and **+ label** offers the labels
**From recent Alert Groups of the Route**, each with an example value, and takes any other name you type. **Reset to
default** goes back to the computed list. Through the API, give the Route `own_message_labels: true` and its list as
`message_labels` in `updateRoute`, or `own_message_labels: false` to go back to the default. By default the list is the
Route's Group key without `alertname` and the Organization's severity label, which the field Severity already shows,
then the Team label and `namespace`; it follows the Group key and the Organization's settings until the Route has a
list of its own. The preview under the Root message shows the labels saved on the Route.

A label shows as `name=value` when every firing Alert has the same value, as `name: N values` when their values
differ, and not at all when no Alert has it. The Route suggests labels from its recent Alert Groups, each with an
example value (`listRouteLabelSuggestions`). A change of the list, or of the Group key while the list is the default,
edits the open Root messages of the Route, Quietly, to show the new labels.

The open Root messages also follow the other settings that they show: a change of the Route's language, its Snooze
durations, or its Root message or line template edits the open Root messages of the Route once, Quietly; a change of the
[severity label, the Team label, the Instance labels, the time zone, the start of the working day or the first day of
the week](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/organization.md), or of a [Link rule or a Lookup table](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/messages-and-templates.md#links), edits every open Root message once, Quietly;
a rename of a Team edits those of the open Alert Groups that have the Team; and a new display name of a User or of a
Service account, or a new, changed or removed [Account link](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/account-links.md) of a User, edits those
whose footer names them, as the Owner or as who snoozed the Alert Group, so that the footer shows the new name or
mentions them by the new link. Moving a Mattermost, Slack or Bridge Destination to another Connection edits, once and
Quietly, those of its open Root messages whose footer names a user with an Account link in either Connection, so that
the footer names them as the new Connection's messenger knows them; a move between Telegram Connections edits none. A
message a change does not alter is not edited, and none of these changes sends a new message or a Mention, with one
exception after a move: an edit goes through the new Connection to a message the old one published, so one the new
Connection cannot find is published again, Quietly, where a Mention in its footer may notify, and one it may not edit,
such as a message another Connection published, makes the Destination [Broken](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/broken-destinations.md). Both
happen at an edit after any move; a move only brings them forward to its own edit, and only for the Root messages whose
footer reads otherwise.

## What you can change, and what you cannot

A Route has three templates, under **Routes → a Route → Message**. Each is **Built-in** or **Custom**.

**Root message**
:   The body of the message: items 3 to 5 of [the default message](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/messages-and-templates.md#the-default-message), the summary, the
    description, the key labels and the Alerts. The title with the status, the fields, the notices, the footer, the
    buttons and the links always come from Muster, so no template can lose the buttons or the link to the Alert Group.
    When a template fails, the Fallback template shows every label of the Alert Group and of each Alert instead.

**Alert line**
:   The line of each listed Alert, which then stands on a line of its own instead of the value of the label that tells
    the Alerts apart. It is also called the **Line template**.

**Ack timeout notice**
:   The reply Muster sends when nobody acknowledges an Alert Group in time.

**Language** of the same section sets the language of the built-in texts of the Route, English or Russian
(`route.language`, English by default). Times in messages are absolute and use the Organization's time zone, never
relative: see [Organization settings](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/organization.md#general).

Choosing **Custom** fills the editor with the source of the built-in template in the Route's language, so you start
from what people see today and edit it. Choosing **Built-in** again keeps your text until you leave the page.

Each editor has a **Preview** under it. Choose the **Sample** it renders against: a **Built-in example**, the **Recent
snapshots of this route**, or the Stored Snapshots of one Integration; and, for the other templates, the **Markup of the preview**, Mattermost,
Slack or Telegram. The preview of the Root message has the tabs **Mattermost**, **Slack**, **Telegram** and **Bridge**:
each lays out [the default message](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/messages-and-templates.md#the-default-message) as its messenger does and shows its length against the
messenger's limit, in red when it is over; the **Bridge** tab shows the `layout` a bridge receives as JSON beside the
CommonMark body, and says so when a Route with a template of its own sends the body alone. When you save, Muster parses every template and runs it against the most recent Stored Snapshots of
the Route (up to 20, `template.dry_run_sample`, or the built-in example when there are none). A template that does not
parse, or that calls a function Muster does not have, is **not saved**: the editor marks the error at its line and
column.

The Outgoing webhook Destinations have templates of their own, written the same way: see
[template mode](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/templates.md).

## Edit a Route's message

1. Open **Routes** and the Route, and scroll to **Message**. Choose the **Language** of its built-in texts. Each
   template shows **Built-in** or **Custom**, with its **Preview** under it.

    ![The Message section of a Route: the language English and the Root message built in, with its preview](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/messages-and-templates/01-message.png){ .screenshot }

2. Choose **Custom** for the template you change, such as **Alert line**, and edit the source in the editor; it starts
   from the built-in template. The **Preview** renders it as you type, against the **Sample** you choose, in the markup
   of **Mattermost**, **Telegram** or **Slack**. A Route without recent Stored Snapshots renders against the built-in
   example. [The template language](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/messages-and-templates.md#the-template-language) lists what a template reads.

    ![The Alert line made Custom with the template pod: summary, and its preview of the built-in example](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/messages-and-templates/02-alert-line.png){ .screenshot }

3. Select **Save**. A template with an error is not saved, and the editor marks the error at its line and column.

## The template language

Templates are Go templates, the language of Alertmanager's own templates, and run in a sandbox:

- The functions are those of Alertmanager's templates under their own names (`toUpper`, `toLower`, `title`, `join`,
  `match`, `reReplaceAll`, `safeHtml`, `stringSlice`, `date`, `tz`, `humanizeDuration`, `toJson` and more), so an
  Alertmanager template usually carries over. On top of them come the string, list, map, conversion and regular
  expression (RE2) functions of the [sprout](https://github.com/go-sprout/sprout) library, `now` and `since` from
  Muster's clock, and two Muster functions: `lookup` (see [Links](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/messages-and-templates.md#links)) and `mention`.
- There is no function that reads the environment, files or the network; `env` does not exist. Muster has no list of
  functions in the web UI: sprout's documentation and Alertmanager's template reference are the function reference,
  and the editor names an unknown function when you save.
- Loops are capped, a template may run for 2 seconds, and the output is cut at about 50,000 characters
  (`template.output_cap`).
- A label or annotation that is not there is empty text, as in Alertmanager: `{{ .Labels.cluster }}` prints nothing
  and passes `""` to a function, so `toJson` gives `""`, not `null`. Tell a missing value apart with `if` or `or`,
  as in `{{ or .CommonLabels.team "none" }}`.

What the Root message template reads:

`.AlertGroup`
:   `Number`, `Title`, `Summary`, `Status`, `Route`, `SeverityLevel`, `Urgent`, `StartedAt`, `URL`, `ReopenCount`, `Owner`.

`.Alerts`, `.Status`, `.GroupLabels`, `.CommonLabels`, `.CommonAnnotations`, `.ExternalURL`
:   As in Alertmanager's templates. `.Alerts.Firing` and `.Alerts.Resolved` select by status.

The line template reads **one Alert** directly, as `.Labels`, `.Annotations`, `.Status`, `.StartsAt`, `.EndsAt`,
`.GeneratorURL` and `.Fingerprint`, and `.AlertGroup` as above.

Alert data is safe by construction. Label and annotation values arrive already escaped for the Destination's markup,
cut at 4 KB each (`message.value_cap`), and an `@` in them is neutralised with a zero-width space, so `@channel` in a
label notifies nobody. Links in messages are `http` or `https` only.

!!! tip "Two small examples"
    A line template that shows the pod and its summary:

    ```
    {{ .Labels.pod }}: {{ .Annotations.summary }}
    ```

    A Root message template that shows the summary and the pods that fire:

    ```
    {{ .AlertGroup.Summary }}
    {{ range .Alerts.Firing }}- {{ .Labels.pod }}
    {{ end }}
    ```

    Check both in the preview with a sample that has Alerts before you save.

If a template that was saved fails later, on data it did not expect, Muster does not drop the message. It renders the
**Fallback template** instead, which shows every label under Muster's heading, footer and buttons. The Route shows
**Template error** with the time and the error, the Timeline records that the fallback was used, and the Internal alert
`MusterTemplateError` fires until the template renders again; see its
[runbook](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/operations/runbooks/MusterTemplateError.md). **Organization → System status** lists the template errors.

A long message is shortened to the limit of the messenger by cutting the list of Alerts first, then the label sections.
It always keeps the title, status, footer, buttons and the link. The list shows up to 10 lines of Alerts
(`message.alerts_listed`); above that it shows the distinct values of each label that differs, and "Full list in
Muster".

## Whom a message mentions

Each Destination has a list of Mention entries, each a target and the Loud events it applies to; its **Mentions**
section edits them. [Whom a message mentions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md) describes every entry, what each Destination
type takes, Team mentions, and why a person needs an Account link to be mentioned.

In a template, `{{ mention "all" }}`, `{{ mention "channel" }}`, `{{ mention "here" }}`, `{{ mention "owner" }}` and
`{{ mention "group" "oncall" }}` write a trusted Mention that Muster renders for the messenger. A literal `@all` written
in a template is escaped and mentions nobody.

## Links

Links to other tools come from data, not from code in a message: Link rules read Lookup tables. They appear as one line
in the message and as the **Links** block on the Alert Group page. Creating, changing or deleting a Link rule or a
Lookup table edits, Quietly, the open Root messages whose links it changes, and no other.

### Add a Lookup table

A **Lookup table** is a table of values: a key and one value per named column. Limits: 50 columns and 10,000 rows,
names and column names up to 200 characters, a description up to 2,000, a key or value up to 4,096
(`lookup_table.size_max`).

1. Open **Organization → Lookup tables** and select **Create table**.
2. Enter the **Name** that Link rules read the table by, and a **Description** if you like. Under **Rows**, name the
   first column, add more with **Add column**, and add each row with **Add row**: its **Key** and a value per column.

    ![The form of a new Lookup table: the name dashboards, the column url and the rows prod-eu and prod](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/messages-and-templates/03-new-lookup-table.png){ .screenshot }

3. Select **Create**. The list of Lookup tables shows the table with its number of rows.

    ![The Lookup tables: dashboards with 2 rows](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/messages-and-templates/04-lookup-tables.png){ .screenshot }

### Add a Link rule

A **Link rule** turns an Alert Group, or each value of a label, into a link. Its fields:

**Name**
:   Shown as the text of the link.

**Matchers**
:   Conditions on the Alert Group's common labels. A label that only some Alerts carry counts as missing. Without
    Matchers the rule applies to every Alert Group.

**Scope**
:   **Alert Group** gives one link. **Each value of label** gives one link per distinct value of a label among the
    Alerts.

**URL template**
:   A Go template. It reads `.Labels` (the common labels for the scope Alert Group, the labels of the first Alert with
    the value otherwise), `.Value` (the label's value) and the data of the Root message template. `lookup "table" key
    "column"` reads one cell and gives an empty text when the table, the key or the column does not exist. A template
    that writes nothing, or something that is not an `http` or `https` URL, gives no link.

1. Open **Organization → Link rules** and select **Create rule**.
2. Fill in the fields above. The **Preview** under the URL template shows the link that the rule makes for the
   **Sample** you choose, a built-in example or a recent Alert Group.

    ![The form of a new Link rule: the name Dashboard, no Matchers, the scope Alert Group, the URL template with lookup and its preview link](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/messages-and-templates/05-new-link-rule.png){ .screenshot }

3. Select **Create**. The list of Link rules shows the rule beside the built-in **Explore**.

    ![The Link rules: Dashboard and the built-in Explore](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/messages-and-templates/06-link-rules.png){ .screenshot }

All the Link rules of one message together read at most 100 rows with `lookup` in one render
(`template.lookups_per_render`); the 101st is a template error, and a Link rule that fails is left out of the message
and counted.

Example: a dashboard per cluster.

1. Create the Lookup table `dashboards` with the column `url` and one row for each cluster: the key `prod-eu`, the
   value `https://grafana.example.org/d/abc123/prod-eu`.
2. Create the Link rule `Dashboard`, scope **Alert Group**, with the URL template
   `{{ lookup "dashboards" .Labels.cluster "url" }}`. An Alert Group without a common `cluster` looks up an empty key
   and gets no link: a missing label is [empty text](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/messages-and-templates.md#the-template-language).
3. The preview of the rule shows the link for a sample. Alert Groups with `cluster="prod-eu"` now carry **Dashboard**.

A rule with the scope **Each value of label** and the label `pod` can build one log link per pod:
`https://logs.example.org/search?pod={{ .Value | urlquery }}`.

Muster adds links without a rule as well: the annotations `runbook_url` and `dashboard_url`, shown as **Runbook** and
**Dashboard**, and the `generatorURL` of the first firing Alert, shown as **Source**.

The built-in Link rule **Explore** opens the query of the Alert in Grafana's Explore. It reads the Lookup table
`grafana` with the columns `address` (the address of your Grafana) and `datasource_uid`, and the key is the value of the
`environment` label, else of the `cluster` label. It cannot be deleted, renamed or given another scope; its Matchers and
URL template can be changed. A Lookup table that a Link rule reads cannot be deleted or renamed.

The API has `lookup-tables` and `link-rules` for the same: see the [API reference](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/api.md). A list of
Lookup tables comes at most 50 to a page (`lookup_table.page_max`), since each carries its rows.

## Thread replies

Lifecycle events that are Thread replies, such as a Takeover or a Reopen, are sent at once. Only new Alerts are
batched, by the Route's **Thread batching window** (**Routes → a Route → Delivery**, `route.thread_batching_window`, 60
seconds by default): the first new Alert is sent at once and opens the window, and the new Alerts that arrive within it
are sent as one reply when it closes. After a quiet period longer than the window, the next one is again immediate. A
reply lists the first 10 new Alerts and then "…and K more" (`delivery.thread_alerts_listed`).

# Organization settings

The **Organization** is the owner of all Users, Routes and data in Muster; there is one. Its settings change behaviour
for everyone, so only Admins open them: choose **Organization** in the main navigation, then a page of its
sub-navigation. Every change is recorded in the Audit log (**Organization → Audit log**) with a before and after, and two Admins
editing the same page do not overwrite each other: the second save is refused with "Someone else changed these settings.
Reload to see them.", and you
reload and apply your change again.

The defaults below are what a new installation has.

## General

**Organization → General**

**Name** (`organization.name`, `Muster`)
:   The name of the Organization.

**Time zone of messages** (`organization.time_zone`, `UTC`)
:   The time zone of every time in a message in a messenger. It is `UTC` until you change it, so a new installation
    shows times such as "started 07:12" in UTC. Times in messages are absolute, never "5 minutes ago". Each User sees the
    web UI in their own time zone, which they set in their profile. A change edits every open Root message once, as an
    edit that notifies nobody, so that its start time is in the new time zone: within seconds on a small installation,
    over minutes on one with many open Alert Groups, as the messengers' rate limits allow.

**Start of the working day** (`organization.workday_start`, `09:00`) and **First day of the week** (`organization.week_start`, `monday`)
:   The Snooze presets "Until tomorrow 09:00" and "Until Monday 09:00" end at this time of day and on this day, in the
    time zone of the person who snoozes. A change applies to Snoozes set afterwards; a Snooze already set does not move.
    The open Root messages are edited once, as edits that notify nobody, so that their preset buttons show the new
    labels. See [statuses and commands](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md).

**Team label** (`organization.team_label`, `team`)
:   The label whose value names the Team of an Alert, by the Team's slug. It cannot be a label that an Internal alert
    sets itself, such as `alertname` or `team_id`. A new Team label evaluates the firing Alerts of open Alert Groups
    again and only adds Teams to them. See [Which Alerts belong to a
    Team](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#which-alerts-belong-to-a-team).

**Working hours** (`organization.working_hours`: Monday to Friday, from the start of the working day to `18:00`)
:   The days (`organization.working_days`) and the end of the working day (`organization.workday_end`) of the
    Organization's Working hours, which every Team without its own follows; the start is the **Start of the working
    day** above. An end that is not later than the start is refused. See
    [Working hours](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#working-hours).

1. Open **Organization**; **General** opens first.
2. Change the **Name**, the **Time zone of messages** — type in **Search time zones** to narrow the list —, the **Start

## Severity levels

**Organization → Severity levels**

Alerts say how bad they are in a label. Muster turns its values into three **Severity levels**: critical, warning and
info.

**Severity label** (`organization.severity_label`, `severity`)
:   The label that carries the severity.

**Mapping** (`organization.severity_mapping`)
:   Values of that label and their level. By default `critical` is critical, `warning` is warning, and `info` and `none`
    are info. A label that is missing counts as info; any other value counts as warning and is shown as received.

**Emoji and colours** (`organization.severity_styles`)
:   The emoji and the colour of each level: critical 🟥 `#d32f2f`, warning 🟧 `#f57c00`, info 🟦 `#1976d2`.

**Values seen** (`organization.severity_values_window`, the last 14 days)
:   The values of the severity label that arrived, with their counts. A value with no mapping is marked "No mapping —
    counts as warning" and has a **Map** action. If your alerts use `P1` and `page`, map them to critical here.

1. Open **Organization → Severity levels**.
2. Under **Mapping**, select **Add value**, enter the value, such as `P1`, and choose its level; or select **Map** next
   to a value under **Values seen**. Select **Save**.

    ![The Mapping of the Severity levels with the value P1 mapped to critical](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/organization/02-severity-levels.png){ .screenshot }

## Alert handling

**Organization → Alert handling**

**Instance labels** (`organization.instance_labels`, `pod`, `instance`, `container`, `endpoint`)
:   A new Alert that differs from a firing one only in these labels is a **Replacement**: it stays in the same Alert
    Group, and the Timeline names the label that changed.

**Buttons of Root messages** (`organization.buttons`, all)
:   Which buttons the Root messages of every Destination show, unless a Destination has a set of its own: **All buttons**,
    the default, or **Only the buttons I tick**, with a tick box for **Acknowledge** (which brings **Unack** with it),
    **Resolve**, each Snooze duration of your Routes and both Snooze presets. **What a Root message shows** lists the
    buttons of a firing and of an acknowledged Root message and follows each tick box. Hidden actions stay in Muster
    and in the Text commands of Bridges. See [Button sets](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#button-sets).

From 0.2.0 the page has no **Critical is Urgent** switch: an Alert Group is
[Urgent](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#urgent) exactly when its Severity level is critical.

Changes to Severity levels and Instance labels apply to Alerts evaluated from then on. Open Alert Groups keep their
status. A change of **Buttons of Root messages** updates the open Root messages of the Destinations that follow the
Organization, as edits that notify nobody. So does a change of the severity label, the Team label or the Instance labels
for every open Root message, which shows them in its
[key labels and Alerts](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/messages-and-templates.md#the-default-message): each is edited once, and one that the change
does not alter is left as it is. The edits arrive within seconds on a small installation and over minutes on one with
many open Alert Groups, as the messengers' rate limits allow. A Destination sends the edits of Urgent Alert Groups right
after their Root messages and the edits that Alerts and Commands make to them, and before anything about the other Alert
Groups, so the Root message of an Urgent Alert Group waits for work that is not Urgent at most one batch of it, about
ten calls. Thread replies, Urgent or not, come after the Root messages and the edits that Alerts and Commands make of
both: a reply waits for that work due before it in the Destinations whose rate limit has room. Once a Destination's rate
limit is used up, Muster leaves the rest of its messages for a fraction of a second, so a backlog in one Destination
holds up the others by about one batch of ten calls, not by its size. The edits of the other Alert Groups come last:
they never hold up the Root messages of new Alert Groups, the edits of Root messages that Alerts and Commands make, the
Thread replies, such as the one about new Alerts, or the events of outgoing webhooks. A Destination sends those first,
for as long as they use up its rate limit, and these edits with the rest; none is dropped. Alert Groups that keep
flapping can hold them up for a long time: a Telegram chat takes 10 calls a minute, and an Alert Group that resolves and
opens again takes up to four of them, a Root message edit and a Thread reply each time, so about three such Alert Groups
a minute leave nothing for these edits until they calm down.

1. Open **Organization → Alert handling**.
2. Change the **Instance labels**: **Add label** adds one, the × next to a label removes it.
3. Under **Buttons of Root messages**, choose **Only the buttons I tick** and untick what the Root messages should not
   show, such as **Resolve**. Select **Save**.

    ![Organization → Alert handling: Buttons of Root messages with Only the buttons I tick chosen and Resolve unticked](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/organization/03-alert-handling.png){ .screenshot }

## Security

**Organization → Security**

**TOTP required** (`organization.totp_required`, **Nobody**)
:   **Nobody**, **Local users** (Users who sign in with a password) or **Everyone** (OIDC users too). A person the
    policy covers who has no TOTP sets it up right after their next sign-in. See [TOTP](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/sign-in/totp.md).

**Token grace for OIDC users without an offline token** (`auth.oidc_token_grace`, 7 days)
:   Applies to Users who sign in through OpenID Connect when the identity provider granted no offline token. Their
    Personal access tokens work while their last OIDC sign-in is no older than this; after that they get `401` with the
    code `oidc_recheck_required` until the person signs in through OIDC again. Service account tokens are not affected.
    See [API tokens and automation](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/api-tokens.md).

1. Open **Organization → Security**.
2. Choose who must have TOTP under **TOTP required**, and change the **Token grace for OIDC users without an offline
   token (days)**. Select **Save**.

    ![Organization → Security: TOTP required for nobody and a token grace of 14 days, saved](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/organization/04-security.png){ .screenshot }

## Outbound addresses

**Organization → Outbound addresses**

The **Outbound address policy** (`organization.outbound_policy`) says which network addresses Muster may call:
Destinations, Connections, the identity provider, proxies and the outgoing heartbeat. The check runs after the name is
resolved, so a name that points at a blocked address is blocked.

- **Standard** (the default) allows private addresses and blocks loopback.
- **Strict** allows only public addresses and the entries you allow.

**Allowed** and **Denied** (`organization.outbound_allowed`, `organization.outbound_denied`, both empty) take a network
such as `10.0.0.0/8`, an address, a host name or a domain such as `*.example.org`. A denied entry always wins. Some
addresses are always blocked and no allowed entry opens them: link-local, cloud metadata, unspecified and multicast
addresses. A chat server on the loopback address, for one, needs `127.0.0.0/8` in **Allowed** under Standard.

1. Open **Organization → Outbound addresses**.
2. Choose the **Outbound address policy**. Add entries with **Add allowed entry** and **Add denied entry**, and remove
   one with the × next to it. Select **Save**.

    ![Organization → Outbound addresses: the policy Standard, with 127.0.0.0/8 and 10.20.0.0/16 allowed](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/organization/05-outbound-addresses.png){ .screenshot }

The screenshot is of a development installation, whose test chat servers listen on the loopback address.

## Retention

**Organization → Retention**

**Stored Snapshots (days)** (`retention.stored_snapshots`, 14)
:   Snapshots kept as received, for debugging, previews and replay.

**Alert details (days)** (`retention.alert_details`, 90)
:   Alerts inside Alert Groups, Timeline entries, delivery events, the steps of the runs of
    [Escalation chains](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/escalation-chains.md#when-chains-run) and the delivery state of each Destination.
    Never Notes.

**Alert Group summaries (days)** (`retention.alert_group_summaries`, 730)
:   The summary row of an Alert Group, with its Notes, and the calls through Notification gateways about it.

**Audit log (days)** (`retention.audit_log`, 365)
:   Who changed or did what.

Each period is 1 to 36,500 days. The summaries must be kept at least as long as the details, because details go first;
a save that breaks this is refused (`retention_order`).

1. Open **Organization → Retention**.
2. Enter the number of days of each period and select **Save**.

## The API

`GET /api/v1/organization` reads the Organization and its `ETag`; `PUT /api/v1/organization` replaces the fields above
with `If-Match` set to that ETag, as [API tokens and automation](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/api-tokens.md#conventions) shows. The fields are
`name`, `time_zone`, `workday_start`, `week_start`, `severity_label`, `severity_mapping`, `severity_styles`,
`critical_is_urgent` (ignored from 0.2.0, and always read as `true`), `instance_labels`, `outgoing_heartbeat`
(required, with `proxy`, whose `enabled` is
required; `url` is write-only and kept when omitted), `retention` (`stored_snapshots_days`, `alert_details_days`,
`alert_group_summaries_days`, `audit_log_days`), `totp_required` (`nobody`, `local_users` or `everyone`) and
`oidc_token_grace_seconds`. The outbound policy is `GET` and `PUT /api/v1/organization/outbound-policy`, and the values
seen are `GET /api/v1/organization/severity-values`. Reading the Organization needs any sign-in; reading the outbound
policy and the values seen needs `organization:read`, and changing anything needs `organization:write`; only the Admin
Role holds both. `workday_start` and `week_start` are optional on an update.

# API tokens and automation

Everything the web UI does, a script can do through the API, as a person or as a non-person identity. This page shows
how to get a token, how to call the API, and what to expect from it. The [API reference](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/api.md) lists
every operation, its Permission and its answers; the binary serves the same specification at `/api/v1/openapi.yaml`.

## Two kinds of token

A **Personal access token** acts as a User. It has the Permissions you choose, never more than you hold, and it loses
what your Role loses. Use it for your own scripts. Its prefix is `mstr_pat_`.

A **Service account** is an identity that is not a person, such as Terraform or a pipeline. It has a name, a Role and
any number of tokens, whose prefix is `mstr_sat_`. It cannot sign in to the web UI and has no Account links.

!!! note "A Service account cannot become an Owner"
    The Owner of an Alert Group is always a User. Acknowledge from a Service account is refused with `409` and the code
    `owner_must_be_user`. It may Resolve, Snooze and add Notes, and the Timeline shows the Service account as the actor.
    A Personal access token acts as its User, so acknowledging with one makes that User the Owner.

An **Integration token** (`mstr_int_`) is a third kind and is not for the API: it sends alerts and Heartbeats only, and
the API does not accept it. See [Connect Alertmanager](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/alertmanager.md).

The prefix `mstr_` and the kind in it let secret scanners find a token that leaked into a repository. If one does,
revoke it.

## Create a Personal access token

Tokens are created in the web UI. A token cannot create or revoke another token, so there is no API call to start from
nothing.

1. Open **Profile**, **Personal access tokens**, and select **Create token**.
2. Give it a **Name** that says where it is used, such as `laptop-scripts`.
3. Under **Permissions** tick what the script needs. You see only the Permissions you hold. A script that reads Alert
   Groups needs `alert-groups:read`; one that also acknowledges needs `alert-groups:acknowledge`.
4. Choose **Expires on**: **30 days**, **90 days**, **365 days** or a date of your own. A token may have **No expiry**
   (`token.expiry` is none by default), but the page warns about a token that never expires: choose a date.

    ![The dialog Create token: the name laptop-scripts, 90 days, and alert-groups:read and alert-groups:acknowledge ticked](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/api-tokens/01-create-token.png){ .screenshot }

5. Select **Create**. The dialog shows the token once. Copy it with **Copy** before you select **Done**: only a hash is
   kept.

    ![The dialog Token created with the token mstr_pat_… and the warning that it is shown once](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/api-tokens/02-token-created.png){ .screenshot }

The list shows when each token was created, when and from which address it was last used, and when it expires.
**Revoke** stops a script that uses it at once. The Audit log records a request made with a token as "alex via token
laptop-scripts".

## Create a Service account

1. Open **Access → Service accounts** and select **Create service account**.
2. Give it a **Name** and a **Role**: Admin, Responder or Viewer. You can give only a Role whose Permissions you hold.
   Select **Create**.

    ![The dialog Create service account: the name terraform and the Role Admin](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/api-tokens/03-create-service-account.png){ .screenshot }

3. On its page, under **Tokens**, select **Create token**, give it a name and an expiry, select **Create**, and copy the
   value once.

    ![The dialog Token created with the token mstr_sat_… of the Service account](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/api-tokens/04-service-account-token.png){ .screenshot }

4. The page of the Service account shows its Role, its status and its tokens, with **Disable** and **Delete**.

    ![The Service account terraform: its details, its Role, its actions and the token pipeline](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/configure/api-tokens/05-service-account.png){ .screenshot }

Its tokens act with the Role of the account, and changing the Role changes every token at once. To change a token
without a gap, create the second token, switch the pipeline to it and revoke the first. **Disable** stops all its tokens
until you **Enable** it; **Delete** revokes them for good.

## Call the API

Send the token as a bearer token. The API is under `/api/v1` of the address people open, `MUSTER_PUBLIC_URL`:

```sh
MUSTER=https://muster.example.org
TOKEN=mstr_pat_...
curl -sS -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" "$MUSTER/api/v1/alert-groups?status=firing&limit=20"
```

The answer is JSON: `items` and `next_cursor`. A request with no token, or a wrong one, gets `401`; one with a token
that lacks the Permission gets `403`. Every operation in the reference names the Permission it needs.

Snooze the Alert Group `AGK7M3QX9P2RTA` until a time (the token needs `alert-groups:snooze`):

```sh
curl -sS -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"until": "2026-10-20T08:00:00Z"}' "$MUSTER/api/v1/alert-groups/AGK7M3QX9P2RTA/snooze"
```

A call on the interactive path, such as a check of a Connection, answers `503` with `Retry-After` when Muster's limit
toward the messenger leaves no room within 5 seconds (`delivery.interactive_budget`); retry after the pause.

A few operations need the web session and refuse a token with `403` (`session_required`): changing your own password,
TOTP, Account links and tokens.

### Conventions

**Identifiers**
:   URLs carry the `public_id` of a resource, such as `AGK7M3QX9P2RTA`, never the `#N` of an Alert Group.

**Pagination**
:   `limit` is 50 by default and at most 500 (`api.page_size`). A page carries `next_cursor`; send it back as `cursor`
    for the next page. There are no offsets.

**Changing configuration**
:   Routes, Destinations, the Organization and other configuration carry an `ETag`, in a header and in the body. Read
    it, then update with `PUT` and `If-Match` set to it. A stale value gets `412`, a missing header `428`. This stops
    two writers from overwriting each other.

    ```sh
    curl -sS -D - -o /dev/null -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" "$MUSTER/api/v1/organization" | grep -i '^etag'
    ```

    Then send the changed Organization with `-X PUT -H 'If-Match: <that value>'`. An omitted optional field keeps its
    stored value, and an explicit `null` clears one that can be cleared.

**Errors**
:   RFC 9457 problems (`application/problem+json`) with a stable `type` and `code`, and `errors[]` with a JSON pointer to
    each invalid field.

**Times and units**
:   RFC 3339 in UTC; durations in whole seconds in `*_seconds`; retention in `*_days`.

**Secrets**
:   Write-only. A read shows whether a secret is set and when it changed, never its value.

### Rate limit

The API is limited per token: 20 requests per second with bursts of 100 (`api.rate_limit`), counted on each Muster
replica. A request over the limit gets `429` with `Retry-After` in seconds; wait that long and retry. Another token is
not affected. Web sessions and ingestion are not limited.

## Tokens of OIDC users

A Personal access token of a User who signs in through OpenID Connect follows the identity provider. If the provider
refuses the person at a background re-check, the token gets `401` with the code `oidc_recheck_required` and the text
"Sign in through OIDC to make your tokens work again", and works again after the person's next OIDC sign-in, without a
new token. When the provider granted no offline token, the tokens also stop once the last OIDC sign-in is older than the
Organization's grace period (`auth.oidc_token_grace`, 7 days; **Organization → Security**, see
[Organization settings](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/organization.md#security)). Your profile shows the date until which your tokens work. Service
account tokens are not affected, which is why automation belongs on a Service account.

A web session has its own lifetimes: see [Sign-in](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/sign-in/index.md#ways-to-sign-in).
