# Alert Groups

An **Alert Group** is a set of Alerts that Muster treats as one problem. It is the thing people act on: it has a number
like `#412`, a status, and at most one **Owner**, and each Destination that receives it shows it as a single message
that Muster keeps up to date. Acknowledge it, snooze it or resolve it, and everyone looking at it sees the result.

This page explains what an Alert Group is and how it relates to the other things Muster handles. The pages that follow
go deeper:

- [Routes and Group keys](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md): how an Alert finds its Alert Group.
- [Snapshot semantics](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/snapshot-semantics.md): how Alertmanager's notifications become Alerts, and when an Alert counts
  as gone.
- [Statuses and commands](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md): the four statuses, the commands, ack timeouts and Reminders.
- [Loud and Quiet](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/loud-and-quiet.md): which changes ring a phone, and whom they Mention.
- [Broken Destinations](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/broken-destinations.md): what happens when a messenger stops answering.

## The things Muster handles

Four words sound alike and are not the same. Muster keeps them apart:

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| Snapshot | One notification from Alertmanager. It lists the current Alerts of one **Alertmanager group**. It is input, not an Alert. |
| Alertmanager group | The alerts Alertmanager notifies about together under one `groupKey`. It is Alertmanager's idea of "together", decided by the `group_by` of its route. |
| Alert | One firing problem reported by a source, identified by its full set of labels, such as one pod, one node or one domain. |
| Alert Group | The problem Muster shows people. It contains Alerts, and which ones go together is decided by a [Route and its Group key](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md), not by Alertmanager. |

Muster does not copy Alertmanager's grouping. Alertmanager's groups exist to batch notifications. An Alert Group exists
so that a person can say "I have this one". The two often look alike, but you can change either without the other.

## What an Alert Group has

Number, `#N`
:   Assigned per Organization, without gaps, when the Alert Group is created. It is what people say aloud: "I took
    #412".

Status
:   `firing`, `acknowledged`, `snoozed` or `resolved`. See [Statuses and commands](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md).

Owner
:   The user who acknowledged it. Only a user can be an Owner; a Service account cannot.

Title
:   The `alertname` that its Alerts share. When Alerts with different `alertname` values join it, the Group key values
    instead, such as `cluster=prod, namespace=payments`. It is set when the Alert Group is created and changes at most
    once, at that switch.

Summary
:   The `summary` annotation of the first of its Alerts that has one. It is set once.

Route
:   The [Route](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md) that took its Alerts. It decides the Destinations and the timers.

Severity level
:   The highest Severity level of its firing Alerts — critical, warning or info — raised to the
    [Minimum severity](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#minimum-severity) of its Route.

Urgent
:   Set exactly when its Severity level is critical. See [Urgent](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#urgent) and [What critical
    changes](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/what-critical-changes.md).

Reopen count
:   How many times Muster reopened it.

Teams
:   The [Teams](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#which-alerts-belong-to-a-team) of every Alert that ever joined it, each with the rule that
    brought it: the Team label or a Team matcher. They only grow, and they do not change where the Alert Group is
    delivered, unless its Route delivers to the Teams
    ([Routing to Teams](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#also-to-the-teams-from-the-label)). The list filters by Team, and **My
    Teams** shows those of the Teams you are in.

Alerts
:   One entry per fingerprint, firing first, resolved ones struck through with their reason.

Timeline
:   The ordered record of everything that happened to it.

The Alert Group page shows all of this, the Alerts with their labels and annotations, the labels they share, and the
previous Alert Groups with the same Route and Group key values. The page and the list work at phone width.

## Live updates

The list and the Alert Group page change as soon as something happens, without a reload. A mark in the header of every
page says whether these live updates reach your browser, so that on a phone at night you know whether the list in front
of you is current:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Green filled dot, "Live" | Live updates are on. |
| Grey ring, "Connecting…" | The page has loaded and is connecting. The text shows once this has taken 5 seconds. |
| Amber half-filled dot, pulsing, "Reconnecting…" | The connection has been lost for 5 seconds, and the browser keeps trying again. |
| Red ring with a slash, "Offline" | The connection has been lost for 30 seconds, or the page has not connected within 30 seconds of loading. |

On a screen narrower than 640 pixels, such as a phone, only the dot shows, whatever the state, so that the navigation
keeps its room; each state has its own shape as well as its colour, so you can tell them apart without the colour. A
drop shorter than 5 seconds, such as a Muster replica restarting, leaves the mark as it was. Press the mark to read its
state and what it means: in every state but "Live" it says that what you see may be out of date, while reconnecting or
offline it names the time the connection was lost, and when offline it offers to reload the page. As soon as the
connection is back, the list and the page are read again, so they show everything that happened meanwhile. The mark
sends nothing to Muster.

## How Alerts join an Alert Group

When an Alert starts firing, Muster picks its [Route](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md) and reads the values of the Route's
**Group key** from the Alert's labels. Then, in this order:

1. If an **open** Alert Group of that Route has the same Group key values, the Alert joins it.
2. Otherwise, if the system resolved an Alert Group of that Route with the same values within the
   [Reopen window](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/alert-groups.md#reopening), that Alert Group reopens.
3. Otherwise a new Alert Group starts, with the next number.

An Alert that is already in an open Alert Group is not routed again: it stays there until the Alert Group is resolved,
even if you change the Route's Matchers or Group key meanwhile.

What a new Alert does to an Alert Group depends on its status, because the status says whether anybody is paying
attention:

| The Alert Group is | A new Alert joining it is |
|---|---|
| firing | a Loud event, added to the Thread in batches |
| acknowledged | a Quiet event; the acknowledgement stays, always |
| snoozed | a Quiet update of the Root message only; what accumulated is listed when the Snooze ends |

### Replacements

An exporter restarts and reports the same problem with a new `pod` label. That is a different Alert, since its labels
differ, but it is the same problem. When a new Alert differs from a firing Alert of the same Alert Group only in
[Instance labels](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/alert-groups.md#instance-labels), Muster records a **Replacement**: a Quiet event in the Timeline that names the label
that differs. The old Alert resolving is Quiet too. The Alert Group page suggests the real fix, which is to aggregate the
label away in the rule, for example with `without(pod, instance)`.

### Instance labels

Instance labels are the labels that change when the process reporting an Alert restarts. The Organization lists them in
`organization.instance_labels`; by default `pod`, `instance`, `container` and `endpoint`. See [Instance labels in
informational alerts](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/alertmanager.md#instance-labels-in-informational-alerts) for when to keep them
out of your rules.

## Reopening

An Alert Group is **resolved by the system** when its last Alert resolves, whether Alertmanager said so, or the Alert
went [Gone or Stale](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/snapshot-semantics.md), or its Integration was deleted. A person can resolve it too.

The two ways to resolve differ in what happens when the problem returns:

| Resolved by | When an Alert with the same Route and Group key values fires again |
|---|---|
| The system | Within the **Reopen window** (`route.reopen_window`, 15 minutes by default) the same Alert Group **reopens**: same number, Reopen count plus one, and it returns to the status it had before, with the same Owner if it was acknowledged. After the window, a new Alert Group starts. |
| A person | The Reopen window does not apply. If the Alerts that were still firing are still reported when the **Grace period** (`route.grace_period`, 15 minutes by default) ends, they are grouped again as if they had just fired. Within the Grace period, a new firing starts a new Alert Group at once. |

A reopen is keyed by the Route and the Group key values, not by one Alert: any Alert with the same values brings the
Alert Group back, whether it is one of its own Alerts or a new one. How loud a reopen is depends on where it returns:
Loud into firing, Loud into acknowledged but Mentioning only the Owner, Quiet into snoozed.

A person who resolved the wrong Alert Group presses **Unresolve** in the web UI or the API. Unresolve is not offered in
messengers, and it works only on an Alert Group that a person resolved, not on one that reopens by itself.

The reason is always visible. A resolution by the system carries the reason, such as "Alertmanager no longer reports this
alert", and so does every other automatic change; the page, the Timeline and the message show it.

## Where an Alert Group shows up

A Route lists the Destinations that receive its Alert Groups. For each Alert Group and each of its Destinations, Muster
keeps one **Root message** and brings it to the latest state each time something changes. Replies, such as new Alerts
joining or a Reminder, go to the message's **Thread**. See [Loud and Quiet](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/loud-and-quiet.md) for which changes are new
messages and which are edits.

An Alert Group whose Route has no Destination, as with the Default route in the [Quick start](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/quick-start.md), exists
only in Muster. Its page, the list, the statistics and the Timeline still work.

## The Timeline

The Timeline of an Alert Group shows, in order: Alerts added, resolved, replaced and continued; status changes with the
actor and the Transport; Reopens; Notes; Takeovers; ack timeout notices, Reminders and their answers; use of the
Fallback template; delivery events such as a Publication, a possible duplicate or a delivery that was Not delivered;
moves to the Default route; and the periods when Muster itself was down. Each entry that records something Muster might
tell people also shows whether it was Loud and whom it Mentioned.

You can filter the Timeline by kind and read it oldest first or newest first. Notes belong to it: Responders and Admins
can add one, up to 4,000 characters (`alert_group.note_max_length`).

## Changing a Route does not change an Alert Group

Edits of a Route's settings apply at once, also to its open Alert Groups, whose messages are updated. They never change
an Alert Group's status. A new Group key or new Matchers apply only to Alerts that start firing afterwards.

A Route that still has open Alert Groups cannot be deleted. Muster refuses and offers to move them to the Default
route. A moved Alert Group keeps its Group key values, takes no new Alerts, and never reopens; it stays open until its
Alerts resolve or a person resolves it.

## Statistics

**Statistics** counts the Alert Groups that started in a period and shows how fast people took and closed them. It has
four views, chosen under **Show**: **By route**, **By integration**, **By team** and **By people**. The first three
give, for each Route, Integration or Team, the number of Alert Groups and the median and the 95th percentile of the
time to acknowledge and of the time to resolve. **By people** shows who did the acknowledging and the resolving. Days
are split in your time zone. Everyone who can read Alert Groups sees every view and filter; Teams limit nothing.

The period counts the Alert Groups that **started** in it, not those that were open during it. Choose the **Period** —
**Last 7 days**, **Last 30 days**, **Last 90 days** or **Custom**.

1. Open **Statistics**. Choose the **Period** and the view, and add filters to narrow it.

    ![Statistics of the last 7 days by route: the Alert Groups and the times to acknowledge and to resolve of the Default route and of payments](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/alert-groups/06-statistics.png){ .screenshot }

2. Select a Route, an Integration or a Team to see its Alert Groups per day of start, with the median times of each
   day (**Per day**).

    ![The Alert Groups per day of start of the Route payments](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/alert-groups/07-statistics-days.png){ .screenshot }

### Filters

The page takes these filters of the Alert Group list, and they apply to every view and stay when you change the view:
**Routes**, **Integrations**, **Severity**, **Urgent**, **Team** (a Team or **My Teams**), **Reopened** and **Label
filters**. It adds one of its own, **Acknowledged first by**: **Anyone**, **Me** or a User; a Service account cannot be
chosen, because it cannot acknowledge. The page has no status tabs and no search. The view, the period and the filters
are in the address of the page, so a link shares exactly what you see.

In **By route**, **By integration** and **By team**, the Routes, Integrations or Teams you choose in their own filter
are also the rows of the table.

### By team

**By team** shows what **By route** shows, for each Team. An Alert Group with several Teams counts for each of them, as
one with Alerts from several Integrations counts for each Integration; an Alert Group with no Team counts for none. So
the Teams do not add up to the total. For example, with the Teams payments and search, an Alert Group of both counts
once for payments and once for search.

The name of a Team opens **By people** for that Team, with the period and the other filters kept.

![Statistics by team: the Teams payments and search, with the Alert Group that both share counted for each](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/alert-groups/08-statistics-by-team.png){ .screenshot }

### By people

**By people** is a table with one row for each person who acted on the Alert Groups of the period, with totals over the
period and no split by day. Click a column to sort by it; by default the rows go by **Acknowledged first**, most first.

**Acknowledged first**
:   the number of Alert Groups the person was the first to acknowledge.

**Resolved**
:   the number of Alert Groups the person resolved.

**Median** and **95th percentile** of the **Time to acknowledge**
:   over the Alert Groups the person acknowledged first.

An Alert Group gives one **Acknowledged first** and one **Resolved**, each to one row; they may be two different people.
One that nobody acknowledged counts in no **Acknowledged first**, and one the system resolved counts in no
**Resolved**. Takeovers, Snoozes and Notes are not counted.

A Service account appears with what it resolved, marked **Service account**, and with no times: it cannot acknowledge.

![Statistics by people: alice, bob and the Service account terraform, with what each acknowledged first and resolved and the times to acknowledge](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/alert-groups/09-statistics-by-people.png){ .screenshot }

#### Who gets the credit

An Alert Group gives its acknowledgement to whoever acknowledged it **first**. A Takeover, an Unacknowledge, a later
Acknowledge or a Reopen does not move it. So if alice acknowledges an Alert Group five minutes after it starts and bob
takes it over an hour later, the Alert Group stays with alice, with five minutes; bob, who took over late, gets no
credit for it. The acknowledgement counts even when it came after the end of the period, as long as the Alert Group
started in it.

#### The row Unknown

The row **Unknown** holds the Alert Groups that were acknowledged but whose first acknowledger Muster does not know:
those acknowledged before 0.2.0 whose Timeline was already removed, wholly or in part (`retention.alert_details`), and
those first acknowledged while Muster was being updated by a rolling update. It is always the last row, whatever the
sorting, and it has no **Resolved**. It is there so that **Acknowledged first** adds up over the rows to the number of
Alert Groups that were acknowledged, which is the **Acknowledged** column of **By route**. The row is not shown
when it has no Alert Groups, nor when **Acknowledged first by** is set.

## How long Alert Groups are kept

`retention.alert_details`
:   kept for: 90 days

    Alerts in Alert Groups, Timeline entries, delivery events, the steps of the runs of Escalation chains.

`retention.alert_group_summaries`
:   kept for: 2 years

    The summary row of an Alert Group: number, title, summary, times, Route, status, and its Notes; and the calls
    through a [Notification gateway](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/notification-gateways.md#how-calls-are-made) about it, once every Alert Group
    a call was about is removed. The text of a call, which names its Alert Groups, is cleared when the call ends.

After the details are removed, search still finds the Alert Group by its title and number, and its page shows the
summary with the notice that the Alerts and the Timeline were removed. The list shows Alert Groups whose lifetime
overlaps the chosen time range, 7 days by default (`alert_group.list_range`), so an Alert Group that has been firing for
weeks is always in the default view. Searching by number ignores the range.

# Routes and Group keys

A **Route** decides what happens to an Alert after Muster has read it: which Alert Group it joins, who is told, and how
the Alert Group is handled afterwards. A **Group key** decides which Alerts share an Alert Group. Together they are the
two settings that shape what your team sees.

Do not confuse a Route with an **Alertmanager route**. An Alertmanager route is a rule inside Alertmanager that picks a
receiver and a repeat interval. A Route of Muster is evaluated after the Alert has arrived, on the labels of the Alert.

## How an Alert is routed

1. The Alert arrives in a [Snapshot](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/snapshot-semantics.md) of an Integration.
2. The Integration's **Static labels** are added to it. If the Alert already has a label of that name, its own value
   wins, and Muster warns about the label.
3. Muster walks the Routes in list order. The first Route whose Matchers all match takes the Alert.
4. The Route's Group key says which Alert Group the Alert goes to. See [Alert Groups](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/alert-groups.md#how-alerts-join-an-alert-group).

Only an Alert that starts firing is routed. An Alert that already lives in an open Alert Group stays in it.

## Routes

A Route has:

- a **name**, unique in the Organization, and a description;
- **Matchers**, which pick the Alerts;
- a **position** in the list;
- a [**Minimum severity**](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#minimum-severity);
- a **Group key**;
- the **Destinations** that receive its Alert Groups;
- policy fields for how its Alert Groups are handled: the Reopen window and the Grace period, whether a rise to Urgent
  removes an acknowledgement, the Snooze durations, the Thread batching window and the Storm threshold, the templates
  and the language, and the ack timeout, the Reminders and auto-unacknowledge.

### Matchers

A Matcher is a condition on one label, written as in Alertmanager: `=`, `!=`, `=~` and `!~`, where the last two take an
RE2 regular expression. The Matchers of a Route are all required: they combine with AND. The Route editor builds them
from fields; the API takes the same structure; an invalid expression is refused with a pointer to the field.

`team="payments"` and `severity="critical"`
:   Takes Alerts of the payments team that are critical.

`namespace=~"prod-.*"`
:   Takes Alerts in any namespace that starts with `prod-`.

`alertname!="Watchdog"`
:   Takes everything except the Watchdog.

A missing label counts as an empty value. So `team!="payments"` also matches an Alert that has no `team` label.

In the Route editor, under **Matchers**:

1. Select **Add matcher**. A row appears with the label, the operator and the value.
2. Enter the label, such as `team`, choose the operator, `=` by default, and enter the value, such as `payments`. The ×
   at the end of a row removes it.

![The Matchers of a Route: one row, team = payments, and the button Add matcher](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/routes-and-group-keys/01-matchers.png){ .screenshot }

### Order, and the Default route

Routes are evaluated in list order, and the first Route that matches takes the Alert. Put the narrow Routes first and
the broad ones after them. Drag the Routes on the Routes page to reorder them.

The **Default route** is always last. It has no Matchers, cannot be deleted or moved, and takes every Alert that no other
Route took. A small installation may keep only the Default route. A Route with no Destinations is fine too: its Alert
Groups exist in Muster and in no messenger.

Edits to Routes use optimistic locking: if somebody changed the Routes while you were editing, your save is refused and
you reload.

### Route profiles

When you create a Route, you choose a **Route profile**. A profile only fills in the settings; it is not stored, and you
can change every field afterwards. There are two:

Ack timeout (`route.ack_timeout`)
:   Paging: on; first notice after 15 minutes · Informational: off

Reminders (`route.reminders`)
:   Paging: on; first after 4 hours · Informational: off

Snooze durations (`route.snooze_durations`)
:   Paging: 1 h, 4 h, 24 h · Informational: 1 d, 3 d, 7 d

Group key (`route.group_key`)
:   Paging: `alertname`, `severity`, `cluster` · Informational: the same

Reopen window and Grace period
:   Paging: 15 minutes each · Informational: the same

Choose **Informational** for alerts nobody needs to act on at once, such as a certificate that expires in a month: no
notices, no Reminders, and long Snooze durations. Choose **Paging** for the rest. See [Statuses and
commands](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#ack-timeouts-reminders-and-auto-unacknowledge) for what the timers do.

### Minimum severity

The **Minimum severity** of a Route (`route.minimum_severity`: none, `warning` or `critical`; none by default) is the
lowest Severity level of its Alert Groups. An Alert Group's Severity level is the highest Severity level of its firing
Alerts, raised to the Minimum severity of its Route. The Minimum severity only raises: it never lowers an Alert Group,
and it never changes the level of an Alert, which the Alerts of the Alert Group page, the Alerts view and the `alerts[]`
of outgoing webhook events still show as received.

Use it for a Route whose Alerts all matter as much as a critical one, whatever their `severity` label says: with the
Minimum severity critical, a warning Alert of the Route starts a critical, and so [Urgent](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#urgent), Alert Group. The
Route that Muster suggests for `MusterHeartbeatLost` has the Minimum severity critical. [What critical
changes](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/what-critical-changes.md#what-the-minimum-severity-adds) lists what a Minimum severity does, and what it
leaves alone.

A changed Minimum severity applies at once to the open Alert Groups of the Route, Quietly. A raised one records a Quiet
`severity_raised`, or the Quiet `urgency_raised` of an Alert Group it makes critical, with the reason "Minimum severity
of the Route"; it never removes an acknowledgement or a Snooze. A lowered one records nothing and lowers their level,
as a falling Severity level does. The Root messages show the new level.

By default the `severity` label maps to a Severity level: `critical` is critical, `warning` is warning, `info` and
`none` are info, any other value counts as warning and is shown as received, and an Alert without the label counts as
info. The Organization can change the label and the mapping.

### Urgent

An Alert Group is **Urgent** when its Severity level is critical — from its Alerts, or from the
[Minimum severity](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#minimum-severity) of its Route. An Urgent Alert Group is posted first when many arrive at once, is
published on its own during a Storm, and a rise to Urgent can end a Snooze or remove an acknowledgement, where the
Route's "rise to Urgent removes the acknowledgement" is on. [What critical changes](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/what-critical-changes.md) lists
every effect of Urgent.

From 0.2.0 the urgent mark of a Route and the Organization's "critical is Urgent" no longer act: a Route marked urgent
got the Minimum severity critical, and a critical Alert Group is always Urgent. The API keeps `urgent` on Routes and
`critical_is_urgent` on the Organization until 0.3.0. A Route request with `urgent: true` and no `minimum_severity`, as
a 0.1 client sends it, sets the Minimum severity critical; `urgent: false`, `urgent` beside `minimum_severity`, and
`critical_is_urgent` change nothing. See the
[upgrade notes of 0.2.0](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/install/versions.md#upgrade-notes).

## Group keys

The **Group key** of a Route is a list of label names. Alerts of the Route that have the same values for all of those
labels go to the same Alert Group. A label that an Alert lacks counts as an empty value, so Alerts that all lack
`cluster` group together.

The default Group key is `alertname`, `severity` and `cluster`. Take these four Alerts, all taken by one Route:

| Alert | `alertname` | `severity` | `cluster` | `namespace` | `pod` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `KubePodCrashLooping` | `warning` | `prod` | `payments` | `api-1` |
| 2 | `KubePodCrashLooping` | `warning` | `prod` | `payments` | `api-2` |
| 3 | `KubePodCrashLooping` | `warning` | `prod` | `search` | `web-1` |
| 4 | `KubePodCrashLooping` | `warning` | `staging` | `payments` | `api-1` |

| Group key | Alert Groups | Which Alerts |
|---|---|---|
| `alertname`, `severity`, `cluster` (default) | 2 | 1, 2 and 3 in one; 4 in another |
| `alertname`, `cluster`, `namespace` | 3 | 1 and 2; 3; 4 |
| `alertname`, `cluster`, `namespace`, `pod` | 4 | one Alert Group per pod |
| `alertname` | 1 | all four |

There is no best key. A coarse key gives few Alert Groups that stay open longer, and a person takes the whole problem
with one Acknowledge. A fine key gives more Alert Groups and a clearer ownership of each. Pick the labels that tell
"who should look at this" apart. A good start is the labels your teams split on: the alert name, the cluster and the
namespace or the service.

In the Route editor, the **Group key** shows its labels in order:

1. To take a label out, select the × next to it.
2. To add one, type its name in the field under the labels and select **Add label**.

![The Group key of a new Route, alertname, severity and cluster, beside its preview: 2 Alert Groups](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/routes-and-group-keys/02-group-key.png){ .screenshot }

### Preview before you save

The Group key editor previews what a key would do. It reads the Stored Snapshots of the chosen period (24 hours by
default, at most the retention of Stored Snapshots; `routing.group_key_preview_period`) and shows how many Alert Groups
the current and the proposed key would produce, with the 5 largest as examples (`routing.group_key_preview_examples`).
It covers the Alerts that the Route would take
at its place in the current order, each Alert once, and it works for a Route you are still creating. Use it before every
change of a key.

2. Under **Group key preview**, choose the **Period**, 24 hours by default, and select **Preview**.
3. Compare **Current**, the saved key, with **Proposed**, the key in the editor: each table lists the largest Alert

### Do not put Instance labels in the key

**Instance labels** change when the process that reports an Alert restarts, such as `pod` and `instance`. If one is in the
Group key, every restart of a pod starts an Alert Group of its own and the old one stays open. Keep `pod` and `instance`
out of the key unless one Alert Group per pod is what you want. When it is, restarts cause no trouble, since the Alert Group
for a pod fires and resolves with that pod. Otherwise leave the labels out of the key, so that the restarted pod's
Alert is recognised as a **Replacement** inside the same Alert Group. See [Alert Groups](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/alert-groups.md#replacements)
and [Instance labels in informational alerts](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/alertmanager.md#instance-labels-in-informational-alerts).

### Static labels

**Static labels** belong to an Integration, not to the Route. Muster adds them to every Alert of the Integration before it
routes it, so the Matchers and the Group key can use them like labels the rules set. Their usual job is to name what
the Alertmanager cluster knows and the rules do not, such as `cluster=prod-eu`. That is why the default Group key can
include `cluster`: set it as a Static label on each Integration, and Alert Groups of different clusters stay apart even
when the alerting rules never mention the cluster. See [Static
labels](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/alertmanager.md#static-labels).

## Examples

**Only the Default route.** Every Alert goes to one Route, grouped by `alertname`, `severity` and `cluster`. Add one
Destination to the Default route and everything reaches one place. This is the setup of the [Quick start](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/quick-start.md)
plus a messenger.

**Pages for payments.** Create the Route `payments-critical` with the Matchers `team="payments"` and
`severity="critical"`, the Paging profile and a Destination that is the payments team's channel. Put it above the
Default route. Critical alerts of the payments team reach their channel and are Urgent. Their other alerts fall through
to the Default route.

**A service whose every alert matters.** Create the Route `hb` with the Matcher `svc="hb"` and the Minimum severity
**critical**. Even its warning Alerts start critical, and so Urgent, Alert Groups, and the Routes list marks the Route
"Minimum severity: critical".

**Certificates.** Create the Route `certificates` with the Matcher `alertname=~"Cert.*"`, the Informational profile and a
Destination for the platform channel. These Alert Groups send no ack timeout notices and no Reminders, and they offer
Snooze for a day, three days or a week.

**Muster's own alerts.** The Internal alerts, such as `MusterDestinationBroken`, are Alerts of the built-in "Muster"
Integration and are routed by Routes like any other. Route them to a Destination of another kind than the one that
failed. The Routes page offers a Route for `alertname=~"Muster.*"` once you have a Destination, and a Route for
`MusterHeartbeatLost` when an Integration has a [Heartbeat](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/heartbeat.md). See the [Internal
alerts](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/internal-alerts.md).

## What changes when you edit a Route

- Settings, such as the timers, the templates and the Snooze durations, apply at once, also to open Alert Groups, whose
  messages are brought up to date.
- A new Group key or new Matchers apply to Alerts that start firing afterwards. A fingerprint already in an open Alert
  Group stays there until that Alert Group is resolved.
- A changed [Minimum severity](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#minimum-severity) raises or lowers the Severity level of the open Alert Groups, Quietly.
- No edit changes the status of any Alert Group.
- Adding a Destination to a Route gives it the Route's open Alert Groups, Quietly. Removing one gives each open Root
  message one last edit that says it is no longer updated and links to Muster.
- Deleting a Route that has open Alert Groups is refused. You can move them to the Default route first.

# Snapshot semantics

Alertmanager does not tell Muster "this alert started" and "this alert stopped" as separate events. It sends a
**Snapshot**: the full state of one Alertmanager group at the moment of the notification, listing every alert it holds.
Grafana's built-in Alertmanager sends Snapshots too, through a webhook contact point.
Muster works out what changed by comparing Snapshots. This page explains the rules, and why an alert can be **Gone** or
**Stale** without anybody sending a "resolved".

You only need this page to understand why Muster resolved something, or why it did not. The settings that matter are on
[Connect Alertmanager](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/alertmanager.md), in particular its
[Repeat intervals](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/alertmanager.md#repeat-intervals), and on
[Connect Grafana](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/grafana.md).

## From a Snapshot to a message

Receiving is store-first. Muster checks the Integration token and the size, stores the Snapshot exactly as received, and
answers `202`. A worker then processes it, on any replica, and everything that follows is a chain of small steps:

```mermaid
flowchart TD
  am["Alertmanager or Grafana"] -- "Snapshot" --> ingest["Ingest"]
  ingest --> stored[("Stored Snapshot")]
  stored --> processing["Processing"]
  processing -- "Alert changes" --> routing["Routing"]
  routing -- "Route" --> groups["Alert Group"]
  groups -- "lifecycle event" --> desired["Desired state"]
  desired --> worker["Delivery worker"]
  worker --> root["Root message"]
```

1. **Ingest** stores the Snapshot as a **Stored Snapshot** and wakes a worker. It is kept for
   `retention.stored_snapshots` (14 days) for debugging, previews and replay.
2. **Processing** splits the Snapshot into Alerts by fingerprint, adds the Integration's Static labels, and applies the
   rules below. It emits Alert changes: a new firing, a resolution with its reason, a Continuation, a change of
   annotations. It processes the Snapshots of an Alertmanager group in the order they arrived.
3. **Routing** gives each newly firing Alert its Route and Severity level.
4. The **Alert Group** takes the Alert: it joins an open one, reopens a resolved one, or starts a new one. Every change
   records a lifecycle event in the Timeline.
5. The lifecycle event changes the **Desired state** of the Alert Group's message in each of its Destinations: what the
   Root message should look like right now.
6. The **delivery worker** brings the actual message to the Desired state with one call, and the **Root message** in the
   messenger shows it. See [Loud and Quiet](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/loud-and-quiet.md) for when that is an edit and when a new message.

Processing writes one log line per Snapshot, never one per Alert.

## One Alertmanager group, one stream of Snapshots

An Alertmanager group is the set of alerts that Alertmanager notifies about together under one `groupKey`. The
Snapshots of one group form a stream, and Muster compares each with the ones before it. Alertmanager sends a Snapshot
again each time the group changes and each time the route's `repeat_interval` elapses, and Muster learns from the gaps.

Two things about Snapshots matter in practice:

- **Copies from an HA pair are harmless.** Two Alertmanagers in a pair send their own copy, 0 to 15 seconds apart,
  sometimes one of them incomplete. Muster processes an Alert once for the same fingerprint, status and `startsAt`, so a
  duplicate changes nothing. Snapshots of one group that arrive within the Integration's **duplicate window**
  (`integration.duplicate_window`, 45 seconds) count as one, and an Alert listed in either of them counts as listed.
- **A Snapshot's status is the group's, an Alert's status is its own.** A Snapshot with `status: firing` can list alerts
  that are resolved, and each alert's own status decides for it.

## Resolved

The normal way an Alert ends is that Alertmanager lists it as `resolved`. Muster resolves it at once, with the reason
that Alertmanager resolved it, in the latest Alert Group where that fingerprint is still firing.

- A `resolved` for an Alert that is already resolved, or for an earlier firing of it, changes nothing. Prometheus re-sends
  a resolved alert for about 15 minutes, so the same `resolved` arrives several times.
- A `resolved` with an older `startsAt` than the Alert's current one resolves the current firing when its `endsAt` is
  later than the current `startsAt`: the two firings overlap. The replicas of a Grafana HA pair evaluate the rules each
  on its own, so their copies of one alert can carry different `startsAt`, and the resolve of either replica ends the
  Alert. Without an `endsAt`, or with one not later than the current `startsAt`, it is a resolve of an earlier firing.
  Alertmanager merges overlapping firings of one alert, so with one rule evaluator a resolve it sends of an earlier
  firing ends before the next one starts. With an HA pair of rule evaluators, such as two Prometheus replicas, it can
  send one that ends later, and Muster resolves the Alert, as it would with the `startsAt` that Alertmanager merged.
- A `resolved` for a fingerprint that fires nowhere is dropped, and counted.
- A Snapshot whose status is `resolved`, sent when all the alerts of its group resolved, resolves **every Alert of that
  group** that still fires, also the ones it does not list. After a restart of both Alertmanagers of a pair, such a
  Snapshot can list only some of them and the others are never sent. A truncated Snapshot from Grafana is the
  exception; see [Truncation](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/snapshot-semantics.md#truncation).

## Gone

Sometimes Alertmanager stops listing an alert without saying that it resolved. The rule evaluator may have stopped, an
alert may have been inhibited, or the route may have changed. Muster notices that the Alert is **Gone**:

> An Alert is Gone in an Alertmanager group when it has been missing from **at least two consecutive Snapshots** of that
> group, over **at least `processing.gone_min_absence`** (5 minutes), counted from the first Snapshot that missed it to
> the latest one.

A Snapshot that lists it ends the count. Both conditions are needed, for good reasons. A restarted Alertmanager starts
with an empty memory, and its first Snapshots can leave out a firing alert twice, a minute apart, because Prometheus
re-sends firing alerts only every 2 minutes. The minimum absence covers that. A restarted instance of an HA pair is
covered by the duplicate window, because the other instance sends the full Snapshot 15 seconds later.

| Snapshots of one group | Result |
|---|---|
| Alert A missing at T and T+60 s, listed at T+120 s | A stays firing; the count restarts |
| Alert A missing at T, T+60 s and T+6 min | A is Gone at T+6 min |
| Alert A missing at T only | A stays firing; one Snapshot never counts |

An Alert that appears in several Alertmanager groups is resolved by absence only when it is Gone (or Stale) in **every**
one of them.

## Stale

If the whole group sends nothing at all, there is no Snapshot to miss the Alert from. This happens when the group is
silenced, inhibited or muted as a whole in Alertmanager. Muster calls an Alert **Stale** when it has heard nothing about
it for too long:

> An Alert goes Stale when its Alertmanager group has sent no Snapshot for `stale_after`, which is
> `processing.stale_after_factor` (3) times the repeat interval that Muster has learned for the group's Alertmanager
> route.

Muster learns the interval from the gaps between Snapshots whose `notification_reason` is `repeat interval elapsed` or,
from senders without that field such as Grafana, between identical Snapshots, and uses the median of the last 9 gaps (`processing.repeat_samples`).
Until it has learned an interval, `stale_after` is `processing.stale_after_unlearned`, 25 hours. Alertmanager sends a
repeat on the first `group_interval` tick after `repeat_interval` has elapsed, so the gaps are about `repeat_interval` +
`group_interval`. With a 10-minute `repeat_interval` and Alertmanager's default `group_interval` of 5 minutes, Muster
learns an interval of about 15 minutes, and a silenced group's Alerts go Stale after about 45 minutes. With a daily
interval it takes more than three days, which is why the Integration page warns about an interval longer than
`processing.long_repeat_warning` (1 hour). See [Repeat intervals](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/alertmanager.md#repeat-intervals).

Gone and Stale both resolve the Alert with the reason "Alertmanager no longer reports this alert — it resolved without
notice, was silenced or inhibited in Alertmanager, or the Alertmanager routing changed". If a silenced alert's mute
ends within about 14 minutes, Alertmanager still reports it as resolved, so it resolves normally; after that it only
goes Stale.

## The Heartbeat

Stale resolution has a risk: if the path from Alertmanager to Muster breaks, Muster hears nothing, and every Alert would
go Stale, although they may still fire. The [Heartbeat](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/heartbeat.md) removes the risk. It is a regular
signal that proves the path works.

- **Stale applies only while the Integration's Heartbeat is live.** An Integration without a Heartbeat, or still waiting
  for the first signal, never resolves an Alert as Stale. Gone still works.
- While the Heartbeat is **lost** (no signal within `integration.heartbeat_timeout`, 5 minutes), the time does not count
  towards `stale_after`. From the last signal before the loss until the signal returns, nothing goes Stale. Muster's own
  downtime does not count either, beyond the timeout.
- The Integration shows "No contact with Alertmanager since HH:MM", and Muster raises the Internal alert
  `MusterHeartbeatLost`.

So with a live Heartbeat and a learned 5-minute interval, an Alert whose group last arrived at T goes Stale at T+15
minutes. If the last Heartbeat signal also came at T, the Heartbeat was lost at T+5 minutes, and the signal returned at
T+10 minutes, the Alert goes Stale at T+25 minutes: the 10 minutes from the last signal until its return did not count.

## Truncation

An Alertmanager webhook configuration with `max_alerts` above 0, like Grafana's webhook contact point with Max alerts
above 0, keeps only the first alerts of a group in each notification, and counts the rest in `truncatedAlerts`. A
truncated Snapshot lists only part of the group, so it can never be evidence that an Alert is missing.

- Muster does **not** count a truncated Snapshot as absence. While Snapshots of the group keep arriving truncated, the
  unlisted Alerts stay alive, which can keep an Alert open that has resolved in reality.
- Muster raises the Internal alert `MusterSnapshotTruncated` for the Integration while any group is truncated, and
  resolves it when none is left. The Integration page shows a warning too.
- Grafana computes the status of a Snapshot from the alerts it lists, not from the whole group, so a truncated
  Snapshot from Grafana can say `resolved` while alerts it left out still fire. Muster recognises a Snapshot from
  Grafana by its top-level `orgId`, which Alertmanager never sends. A truncated one with `status: resolved` resolves
  only the Alerts it lists as resolved, never its whole group.
- The fix is `max_alerts: 0` on the Alertmanager receiver, or Max alerts 0, the default, on Grafana's webhook contact
  point. See [Send every alert of the group](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/alertmanager.md#send-every-alert-of-the-group),
  [Connect Grafana](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/integrations/grafana.md#create-a-webhook-contact-point) and the
  [runbook](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/operations/runbooks/MusterSnapshotTruncated.md).

## Continuation

A Prometheus restart whose first evaluation falls exactly on the alerts' `endsAt` can make Alertmanager send them again
with a new `startsAt` and no `resolved` in between. A longer stop gives a `resolved` and a new firing. That first case
is a **Continuation**: the Alert stays the same, its start time is updated, and nothing is Loud and nothing reopens.
Once an Alert has resolved by any route, the next firing of the same fingerprint is a new firing.

## Why an Alert resolved

Every Alert resolved by Muster carries one of four reasons, which the Integration's Alerts view shows and the metric
`muster_alerts_resolved_total` counts:

`resolved`
:   Alertmanager sent `resolved`, for the Alert or for its whole group.

`gone`
:   Missing from at least two consecutive Snapshots for the minimum absence.

`stale`
:   Its group sent nothing for three learned repeat intervals, with a live Heartbeat.

`integration_deleted`
:   Its Integration was deleted.

When the last Alert of an Alert Group resolves, the Alert Group is resolved by the system with the reason of the last
one. See [Alert Groups](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/alert-groups.md#reopening) for what happens if the problem returns. If processing ever misbehaves,
`muster ingest replay` reprocesses Stored Snapshots, and because processing is idempotent it changes nothing that was
already right.

# Loud and Quiet

Every change to an Alert Group is either **Loud** or **Quiet**. A Loud change should make people's devices ring. A Quiet
change should only update what they see when they look. Muster fixes the loudness of every change; you decide only whom
a Loud change **Mentions**.

The rule behind it is simple, and it is the same in Mattermost, Slack and Telegram: **an edit never notifies anybody.**
Editing a message, even to add a Mention, does not ring a phone. So a Loud change is always a **new message**, a new
Root message or a reply in the Thread, and a Quiet change is an edit of the Root message or a new message without a
Mention.

## Which changes are Loud

A new Alert Group starts
:   Loud, as a new Root message · mentions: the entries for `new_alert_group`

New Alerts join a **firing** Alert Group
:   Loud, as a Thread reply; batched · mentions: `new_alerts`

New Alerts join an acknowledged or snoozed Alert Group
:   Quiet · mentions: nobody

A Reopen into firing
:   Loud · mentions: `reopen`

A Reopen into acknowledged
:   Loud · mentions: the Owner only

A Reopen into snoozed
:   Quiet · mentions: nobody

A Snooze ends while Alerts still fire
:   Loud, listing what accumulated · mentions: `snooze_ended`

A rise to Urgent that ends a Snooze or removes an acknowledgement
:   Loud, with the reason · mentions: the Owner it removed, and `rise_to_urgent`

A rise in Severity level that changes nothing else
:   Quiet · mentions: nobody

A Takeover
:   Loud · mentions: the previous Owner

An ack timeout notice
:   Loud · mentions: `ack_timeout`

A Reminder
:   Loud · mentions: the Owner

Auto-unacknowledge
:   Loud, with the reason · mentions: the Owner it removed

The Owner is disabled or deleted
:   Loud · mentions: nobody

Several notices missed during downtime
:   Loud, as one reply · mentions: as the notices it stands for

Acknowledge, Unacknowledge, Snooze, Unsnooze, Resolve, Unresolve
:   Quiet · mentions: nobody

An Alert resolves, is replaced or continues, or its annotations change
:   Quiet · mentions: nobody

The system resolves the Alert Group
:   Quiet, with a Thread reply that gives the reason · mentions: nobody

A Note
:   Quiet, and not sent to messengers · mentions: nobody

The pattern: Muster rings when something needs a human who may not be looking, such as a new problem, one that returned,
one that nobody took, or one that someone took and went quiet. It does not ring for what a person just did, because that
person is looking. The Takeover is the only result of a person's command that is Loud, because the previous Owner has
just lost the Alert Group.

Delivery adds a few cases of its own:

| Delivery event | Loud or Quiet |
|---|---|
| A Destination added to a Route receives its open Alert Groups | Quiet |
| A Destination recovers from [Broken](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/broken-destinations.md): an Alert Group still firing is published | Loud |
| The same recovery, for an Alert Group that is not firing, or an edit of a message already there | Quiet |
| A message published late, after the Alert Group already resolved | Quiet, with a note |
| A Root message posted again because it was deleted | Quiet, with a note |
| A Storm summary, when it first appears | Loud |
| A Storm summary's updates, and the Alert Groups posted after the Storm | Quiet |

You cannot change the loudness of an event. You can change whom it Mentions.

## What Loud means in each Destination

Mattermost
:   A Loud change is a new post with its Mentions: at the top of the attachment of a Root message or a Storm summary,
    in the text of a reply in the Thread.

    A Quiet change is an edit, or a new post without a Mention.

Slack
:   A Loud change is a new message, a Root message or a reply in the Thread, with its Mentions.

    A Quiet change is an edit, or a new message without a Mention.

Telegram
:   A Loud change is a new message, in the channel for a Root message and in the discussion group for a Thread reply.

    A Quiet change is an edit, or a new message sent with `disable_notification`.

Bridge
:   A Loud change is a new message that the bridge posts with `notify` on, with its Mentions.

    A Quiet change is an edit, or a new message that the bridge posts without a notification where it declares
    `quiet_messages`.

Outgoing webhook
:   A Loud change is an event with `notify` true (events mode), or `.Notify` true in a template (template mode).

    A Quiet change is `notify` false.

A few details:

- **Mattermost.** A reply in a Thread notifies the Thread's followers by their own settings, and a Mention makes the
  person a follower. So Quiet means "without a Mention", not "nobody is notified". A Loud Root message has its Mentions
  as the first line of its attachment, where a person's Mention, `@channel`, `@here` and `@all` notify; a push or
  desktop notification shows the summary line of the Alert Group, and an email shows no content. A Thread reply has
  its Mentions in its text. See [Who gets notified](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/mattermost.md#who-gets-notified).
- **Slack.** Slack cannot send a message without a notification: whom a new message notifies depends on each member's
  settings for the channel and the Thread. So Quiet means "without a Mention", not "nobody is notified". An edit carries
  no Mention, so it drops the Mentions of the message it edits. See [Who gets
  notified](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/slack.md#who-gets-notified).
- **Telegram.** `disable_notification` silences the sound but does not remove the push. Only members of the channel's
  discussion group get Thread replies, and the automatic copy of every Root message rings them, even for a Quiet one.
  Tell the people on call to join the group and mute the channel itself. See [Known limitation: Quiet Root messages ring
  the group](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/telegram.md#known-limitation-quiet-root-messages-ring-the-group).
- **Bridge.** Whether the bridge can post without a notification depends on the bridge. A bridge that does not declare
  `quiet_messages` posts a Quiet message as an ordinary one, without Mentions.
- **Outgoing webhook.** Muster rings nothing itself. In events mode every event is sent, Quiet ones too, with `notify`
  telling what a person-facing system should do with it. Your receiver decides, for example, to call a phone only when
  `notify` is true. See [events mode](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/events.md#event-names) and [Recipes](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/recipes.md).

## Mentions

A **Mention** is a deliberate @-reference that Muster adds to a message so that a person is notified. Muster adds a
Mention only where this page says it does. Alert data never mention anyone: an `@` in a label or an annotation is
neutralized with a zero-width space, so `@channel` in a label notifies nobody.

### Whom a Loud change Mentions

Each Destination has a list of **Mention entries**: each one a target — everyone in the chat, a user, a messenger
group, a Team, or the On-call of a Team — and the Loud changes it applies to: `new_alert_group`, `new_alerts`,
`reopen`, `ack_timeout`, `snooze_ended`, `rise_to_urgent` and `storm_started`. A Destination without entries mentions
nobody. [Whom a message mentions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md) describes the entries and
[what each messenger supports](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md#what-each-messenger-supports).

Some Mentions are not an entry. A **Reminder** and an auto-unacknowledge always Mention the Owner, a **Takeover**
Mentions the previous Owner, and a Reopen into acknowledged Mentions only the Owner.

### Account links

A Mention of a user needs an **Account link**: the tie between a Muster user and their account in the messenger. The
people who are Mentioned, and the Owner above all, link their accounts from their profile; see [Link your messenger
accounts](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/account-links.md). Muster renders a Mention in the messenger's own form:

- Mattermost: `@username`;
- Slack: a Mention of the person's Slack account;
- Telegram: a link to the person, whose text is the display name;
- Bridge: a marker that a bridge with `native_mentions` renders as the messenger's mention and any other bridge as the
  person's name.

**A person whom a Mention entry names is mentioned only through their Account link; without one they are left out.**
So an entry that names a person, directly or as an On-call, rings their phone only once they have linked their
account. The Owner of a Reminder, an auto-unacknowledge or a Takeover, and the footer's user — the Owner, who snoozed,
who resolved — are still named by their display name as text without a link, which notifies nobody.

For an outgoing webhook, Muster does not know the receiver's markup, so the Mention is data: the event's `mentions`
list, and `.Mentions` in a template, with the target's type, id, name and login. Your receiver or your template turns
it into whatever the receiving system understands.

## Storm summaries

When many Alert Groups start at once, one Route can flood a Destination. A **Storm** is a Route creating more new Alert
Groups in one minute than its Storm threshold (`route.storm_threshold`, 20 by default). Then each of the Route's
Destinations gets one **Storm summary** instead of a message per Alert Group:

- The summary is Loud only when it first appears, and Mentions the Destination's entries for `storm_started`.
- **Urgent** Alert Groups are still posted on their own, first.
- Its updates and its final state are Quiet. When the rate has stayed below the threshold for 5 minutes
  (`delivery.storm_calm_period`), the summary shows its final state, and the Alert Groups that are still open are
  posted gradually and Quietly. Those that already resolved are not posted.
- Messengers and the template mode of outgoing webhooks follow Storms. The events mode does not: it receives every event.

## Why the rule is fixed

A rule of thumb for on-call: the phones should ring for problems that need a human, and nothing else. Because an edit is
always silent, a person can acknowledge, snooze and resolve all day, and the message changes under their eyes without a
sound; and because the loudness of every change is fixed, a team never has to wonder whether a change will ring. The
Mention entries are the one place to tune who rings.

# Statuses and commands

An Alert Group is always in one of four statuses, and only a few things move it from one to another. Some are
**Commands** that a person gives, from the web UI, the API, a button in a messenger, or a Text command in a Thread. The
others are transitions that Muster makes on its own, always with a reason you can read.

## The four statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Firing** | The Alert Group has a live problem and nobody has taken it. |
| **Acknowledged** | A person has taken it and is handling it. That person is its **Owner**. |
| **Snoozed** | It is set aside until a chosen time, or with no end. Muster stays quiet about it meanwhile. |
| **Resolved** | It is closed, by a person or by Muster because its Alerts are gone. |

New Alerts that join an open Alert Group change its status only through a rise to Urgent, and edits of a Route never
change a status. Only the transitions below do.

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    state "Resolved by the system" as ResolvedBySystem
    state "Resolved by a user" as ResolvedByUser

    [*] --> Firing : created
    Firing --> Acknowledged : Acknowledge
    Snoozed --> Acknowledged : Acknowledge
    Acknowledged --> Acknowledged : Takeover
    Acknowledged --> Firing : Unacknowledge
    Acknowledged --> Firing : auto-unacknowledge (system)
    Acknowledged --> Firing : rise to Urgent (system)
    Firing --> Snoozed : Snooze
    Acknowledged --> Snoozed : Snooze
    Snoozed --> Snoozed : Snooze with a new end
    Snoozed --> Firing : Unsnooze
    Snoozed --> Firing : Snooze ends (system)
    Snoozed --> Firing : rise to Urgent (system)
    Firing --> ResolvedByUser : Resolve
    Acknowledged --> ResolvedByUser : Resolve
    Snoozed --> ResolvedByUser : Resolve
    Firing --> ResolvedBySystem : all Alerts resolved (system)
    Acknowledged --> ResolvedBySystem : all Alerts resolved (system)
    Snoozed --> ResolvedBySystem : all Alerts resolved (system)
    ResolvedBySystem --> Firing : Reopen (system)
    ResolvedBySystem --> Acknowledged : Reopen (system)
    ResolvedBySystem --> Snoozed : Reopen (system)
    ResolvedByUser --> Firing : Unresolve
    ResolvedBySystem --> [*] : Reopen window ends
    ResolvedByUser --> [*] : Grace period ends
```

Resolved appears twice because the two ways back differ. After the system resolved an Alert Group, it **reopens** by
itself if the problem returns within the Reopen window. After a person resolved it, nothing reopens it; the person can
**Unresolve** it, or Muster starts a new Alert Group if the Alerts still fire after the Grace period. See
[Reopening](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/alert-groups.md#reopening).

The ack timeout runs only while an Alert Group is Firing, and an Alert Group is **Unclaimed** after its last notice. See
[Ack timeouts, Reminders and auto-unacknowledge](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#ack-timeouts-reminders-and-auto-unacknowledge).

### Transitions Muster makes on its own

Each carries its reason, which you see on the Alert Group page, in its Timeline and, in a messenger, in the Root
message or a Thread reply.

Open → Resolved
:   reason you see: the reason of the last Alert

    The last Alert resolved: Alertmanager sent `resolved`, or the Alert went [Gone or Stale](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/snapshot-semantics.md), or
    its Integration was deleted. This also ends a Snooze.

Resolved by the system → previous status
:   reason you see: the Reopen, with the Reopen count

    An Alert with the same Route and Group key values fires within the Reopen window.

Snoozed → Firing
:   reason you see: the Snooze ended

    The Snooze ends while Alerts still fire. It has no Owner, and the message lists what accumulated.

Snoozed or Acknowledged → Firing
:   reason you see: the rise to Urgent

    A rise in Severity level makes the Alert Group Urgent. It ends a Snooze, unless the Snooze was set while the Alert
    Group was already Urgent, and removes an acknowledgement when the Route has `route.urgent_rise_removes_ack` on (the
    default).

Acknowledged → Firing
:   reason you see: "No answer to the last two Reminders"

    The Route's auto-unacknowledge removes the acknowledgement of an Owner who stopped answering.

Acknowledged → Firing
:   reason you see: "The owner was disabled" or "The owner was deleted"

    The Owner was disabled or deleted.

## Commands

| Command | Allowed from | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Acknowledge** | Firing, Snoozed | The caller becomes the Owner. It ends a Snooze. By the current Owner it changes nothing. By another user it is a **Takeover**. |
| **Unacknowledge** | Acknowledged | The Alert Group is Firing again, without an Owner, and the ack timeout starts over. Any user who may acknowledge can do it, not only the Owner. |
| **Resolve** | Firing, Acknowledged, Snoozed | The Alert Group is Resolved by the person. A Note can come with it. |
| **Unresolve** | Resolved by a person | The Alert Group is Firing again without an Owner. Web UI and API only. |
| **Snooze** | Firing, Acknowledged, Snoozed | The Alert Group is Snoozed until a time in the future, until a [Snooze preset](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#snooze-presets), or with no end. A Snooze with a new end changes the end. |
| **Unsnooze** | Snoozed | The Alert Group is Firing again without an Owner, and the ack timeout starts over. |
| **Add Note** | any | A Note appears in the Timeline with its author. Web UI and API only. |
| **Still on it** | Acknowledged, while a Reminder waits | The Owner answers the Reminder. See below. |

Details:

- **Takeover.** Acknowledging an Alert Group that somebody else owns moves it to you, without asking. The previous Owner
  is Mentioned in a Loud Thread reply, and your Reminders start from zero. The web UI says "Take over from {Owner}" on the
  button.
- **Snooze.** The web UI and the API accept any future time, a [Snooze preset](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#snooze-presets), or no end. The API
  makes you say which: a request names exactly one of `until`, `no_end` and `preset`. A Snooze with no end asks you to
  confirm "This Alert Group stays snoozed until someone unsnoozes it".
  Messengers offer the Route's **Snooze durations** as separate buttons or a menu (`route.snooze_durations`: 1 hour,
  4 hours and 24 hours on a Paging Route, 1 day, 3 days and 7 days on an Informational Route), and after them the
  two Snooze presets; see [In messengers](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#in-messengers).
- **Unresolve** is refused when the system resolved the Alert Group ("it reopens by itself when an alert returns"),
  when its Route was deleted, when a newer open Alert Group with the same key exists, and when all its Alerts have
  already resolved.
- **Notes** are up to 4,000 characters (`alert_group.note_max_length`).
- **Bulk commands.** In the list, select up to 100 Alert Groups (`alert_group.bulk_max`) and run Acknowledge, Resolve,
  Snooze or Unsnooze on all of them. Each goes through the rules on its own and the result lists the outcome per Alert
  Group. One refusal never stops the others. A bulk Acknowledge skips an Alert Group that another user owns ("skipped:
  owned by {Owner}"), so it never takes over by accident.
- **Refusals** carry a stable code and a message, such as "already resolved", "not acknowledged" or "not permitted". The
  API answers `409` for a precondition and `403` for a permission.
- Commands on one Alert Group are serialized. Two people acknowledging at the same moment leave the later one as Owner and
  one Takeover in the Timeline.

Every command is a Quiet event except the Takeover. See [Loud and Quiet](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/loud-and-quiet.md).

### Snooze presets

A **Snooze preset** ends a Snooze at the start of the working day instead of after a duration. Muster turns it into an
end when the command runs:

Until tomorrow 09:00
:   API value: `tomorrow` · ends at: the next start of the working day

Until Monday 09:00
:   API value: `week_start` · ends at: the next start of the working day on the first day of the week

"Next" is strictly after now. With the working day starting at 09:00:

- At 02:00, "Until tomorrow 09:00" ends at 09:00 the same day. The night belongs to the day before it, so "tomorrow"
  is the morning about to come, seven hours away.
- At 08:59 it ends one minute later: the preset means "until the working day starts".
- At 09:00 sharp, and at any later time of the day, it ends at 09:00 the next day.
- There is no working-week calendar, so on Friday evening "Until tomorrow 09:00" ends on Saturday at 09:00. For a
  weekend, use `week_start`, which ends on Monday at 09:00.
- The start is the time on the clock on the day of the end. A start that the clocks skip moves forward by the change:
  02:30 on the night the clocks jump from 02:00 to 03:00 is 03:30. A start that the clocks show twice, when they go
  back, is the first of the two.

The end is stored as a time. A later change of the settings or of a time zone never moves a Snooze already set, and the
Snooze ends like any other: if Alerts still fire, the Alert Group is Firing again without an Owner, with a Loud message.

**Time zone.** The preset counts in the time zone that the request names in `time_zone`. Without one, it counts in the
profile time zone of the user who snoozes, also through a Personal access token, and otherwise in the Organization's
time zone (`organization.time_zone`). A Service account has no profile, so it always gets the Organization's. A press on
a button in a messenger and a Text command carry no time zone, so they count in the profile time zone of the linked user
who pressed or wrote, and otherwise in the Organization's.

**Settings.** Two Organization settings define the presets, as the fields `workday_start` and `week_start` of the
organization in the API:

`organization.workday_start`
:   default: `09:00`

    The start of the working day, `HH:MM` on the 24-hour clock.

`organization.week_start`
:   default: `monday`

    The first day of the week, `monday` to `sunday`.

**API.** A Snooze takes `preset`, `tomorrow` or `week_start`, as a third choice beside `until` and `no_end`, with an
optional `time_zone`:

```json
{"preset": "tomorrow", "time_zone": "Europe/Berlin"}
```

A `time_zone` without `preset` is refused with `422` `unsupported`, and a time zone Muster does not know with `422`
`invalid_format`. A bulk Snooze with a preset computes the end once, so every Alert Group gets the same end.
`GET /api/v1/snooze-presets`, with an optional `time_zone`, answers the end that each preset would set now and the time
zone it used; it needs the Permission to snooze and changes nothing. The Audit log entry of a preset Snooze names the
preset and the time zone beside the end. See the [API reference](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/api.md).

#### In the web UI

The Snooze dialog of an Alert Group offers both presets after the Route's Snooze durations and before "Until" and "No
end", also on a Route without durations; the bulk Snooze of the list, which has no durations, offers them first. Under
each preset the dialog shows the end it would set now, such as "Ends Oct 20, 2026, 09:00": Muster computes it when the
dialog opens, on its own clock and in your time zone (the one of your profile, else your browser's), and the dialog sends
that time zone with the preset. Admins set the start of the working day and the first day of the week on Organization →
General. In the profile, a hint under the time zone says that presets pressed in messengers end in that time zone, and
in the Organization's while the profile follows the browser.

![The Snooze dialog with Until tomorrow 09:00 chosen: each preset with the end it would set, after the durations 1 h, 4 h and 24 h](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/statuses-and-commands/01-snooze-presets.png){ .screenshot }

#### In messengers

On a Route with Snooze durations, the Root message of a firing or an acknowledged Alert Group offers both presets as
two more Snooze buttons after the durations: "Until tomorrow 09:00" and "Until Monday 09:00", or «До завтра 09:00» and
«До понедельника 09:00» on a Route in Russian. The time is `organization.workday_start` and the day
`organization.week_start`, so with the week starting on Sunday the second button reads "Until Sunday 09:00". A snoozed
Alert Group offers no Snooze button, and a Route without Snooze durations offers no preset either, because an empty
`route.snooze_durations` means no Snooze from messengers. Telegram shows the presets on a row of their own under the
durations, and a bridge with a limit on buttons drops them first; see
[Bridge](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/bridge.md#what-muster-posts). Slack offers the durations and then the presets as the options of
one **Snooze…** menu, since its web client folds the buttons beyond five in a row.

The labels are part of the message. A change of `organization.workday_start` or `organization.week_start` edits each
open Root message once, Quietly, so that it shows the new labels, and a press on a button shown before the edit already
ends at the new start of the working day: the preset is computed when the command runs. On a bridge without buttons, the presets
are [Text commands](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#text-commands).

### Who may

A **Responder** or an **Admin** may acknowledge, resolve, snooze and add Notes. A **Viewer** may read and may not act:
the command is refused. A **Service account** may run the commands its Role allows, except Acknowledge and Still on it,
because an Owner is always a User. A Personal access token acts as its User, with at most that User's Permissions. Every
command is recorded in the Audit log with its actor and the Transport.

## Where a command comes from

The **Transport** is the way a command reaches Muster. It is recorded with the command, and Muster applies the same rules
to all of them.

Web UI
:   how: buttons on the Alert Group page and in the list, with dialogs for Snooze and Resolve · notes: everything above,
    including bulk commands, Notes and Unresolve

API
:   how: `POST` to `/api/v1/alert-groups/{id}/acknowledge` and its siblings, with a session or a token · notes: the
    same; see the [API reference](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/api.md)

Mattermost
:   how: a button on the message · notes: Acknowledge, Unacknowledge, Resolve, Snooze, Unsnooze, Still on it

Slack
:   how: a button on the message, and the **Snooze…** menu with the durations and then the presets · notes: the same

Telegram
:   how: an inline button on the message · notes: the same

Bridge
:   how: a button on the message, if the bridge declares `buttons`; or a **Text command** in the Thread, if it declares
    `thread_messages` · notes: the same

`system`
:   how: Muster itself · notes: the automatic transitions above, shown in the Timeline

Messengers never offer Unresolve or Notes, and never a Snooze with no end. A person who presses a button in a messenger
must have linked their messenger account to their Muster user; see [Link your messenger
accounts](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/account-links.md). A press from an unlinked account changes nothing, and the person is told
privately how to link it.

### What each button does

The buttons of a Root message depend on the status of its Alert Group, and on the [button set](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#button-sets) of its
Destination, which shows all of them by default:

| Status | Buttons |
|---|---|
| Firing | **Ack**, **Resolve**, **Snooze** |
| Acknowledged | **Unack**, **Resolve**, **Snooze** |
| Snoozed | **Ack**, **Unsnooze**, **Resolve** |
| Resolved | none; a link "Open in Muster" |

- **Ack** takes the Alert Group: you become its Owner, the Reminders start, and the message shows who has it. If somebody
  else has it, Ack takes it over from them.
- **Unack** gives it back. The Alert Group becomes Firing again with no Owner, and the ack timeout starts over.
- **Resolve** closes the Alert Group by hand. If Alerts still fire, the message shows "⚠ N alerts still firing in
  Alertmanager", and after the Grace period the Alerts that still fire start a new Alert Group.
- **Snooze** sets it aside. It offers the Route's Snooze durations, such as 1 hour, 4 hours and 24 hours, one choice for
  each, then the two [Snooze presets](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#in-messengers) "Until tomorrow 09:00" and "Until Monday 09:00", which end at the
  next start of the working day, or at its start on the first day of the week, in your profile time zone, or else the
  Organization's. When the time is up and Alerts still fire, the Alert Group comes back Firing and a Loud message says
  so.
- **Unsnooze** ends the Snooze early. The Alert Group becomes Firing again.
- On a **Reminder**, **Still on it** tells Muster you are still handling the Alert Group, and **Unack** gives it up.
- A **Storm summary** and an ack timeout notice carry only links to Muster. A test message carries the buttons of its
  status and of its Destination's button set, but pressing them changes nothing: you get "This is a test message;
  nothing was changed".

A press edits the Root message to show the result. A press that Muster refuses, such as a Resolve by a Viewer, is answered
privately to the person who pressed, and nothing changes.

### Button sets

A busy channel can show fewer buttons. Each messenger Destination shows on its Root messages the buttons of a **button
set**: the Organization's, which shows every button until an Admin lists them (`organization.buttons`), or a set of its
own (`destination.buttons`). A Destination "As in the Organization", the default, follows later changes of the
Organization's set. A set lists:

- **Acknowledge**, which brings **Unack** with it on an acknowledged Alert Group;
- **Resolve**;
- each Snooze duration, by its value, such as 24 hours;
- the [Snooze presets](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#snooze-presets) "Until tomorrow" and "Until {first day of the week}".

From its set, a Root message shows the buttons that its status offers and that the set lists, in the order of the table
above. Of the Route's Snooze durations it shows only those in the set, so a duration in the set that the Route does not
have shows nothing, and it shows the presets only on a Route with Snooze durations. **Unsnooze** on a snoozed Alert
Group is not part of any set and always shows. A Destination whose set is Acknowledge, 24 hours and "Until tomorrow"
shows, on a Route with the durations 1 h, 4 h and 24 h:

| Status | Buttons |
|---|---|
| Firing | **Ack**, **Snooze 24 h**, **Until tomorrow 09:00** |
| Acknowledged | **Unack**, **Snooze 24 h**, **Until tomorrow 09:00** |
| Snoozed | **Ack**, **Unsnooze** |

To choose the buttons of a Destination:

1. Open **Destinations** and select the Destination, or create one. The form of a Mattermost, Telegram, Slack or Bridge
   Destination has the section **Buttons**. An outgoing webhook sends no buttons and has no section.
2. Under **Buttons**, choose **Own for this Destination**. The tick boxes are **Acknowledge** (which brings **Unack**
   with it), **Resolve**, each Snooze duration of your Routes and both Snooze presets; they start as what the
   Destination shows now. Untick what the Root message should not show. **What {Destination} shows** lists the buttons
   of a firing and of an acknowledged Root message, in the order of the table above, and follows each tick box at once.
3. Select **Save**. Choose **As in the Organization** and save to follow the Organization's set again.

    ![The section Buttons of a Destination with Own for this Destination chosen: Acknowledge, Snooze 24 h and Until tomorrow ticked, and the buttons a firing and an acknowledged Root message show](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/statuses-and-commands/07-destination-buttons.png){ .screenshot }

To change the set of every Destination that follows the Organization, open **Organization → Alert handling** and use
**Buttons of Root messages**: **All buttons**, the default, or **Only the buttons I tick**, with the same preview. See
[Organization settings](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/organization.md#alert-handling). A Destination of its own set keeps it when the
Organization's set changes. A ticked Organization list names the Snooze durations it lists: a duration that a Route
gains later is not shown until you tick it, unlike with **All buttons**. The form offers the Snooze durations of your
Routes, or those of the Paging profile while no Route has any; a duration that the stored set names and no Route has
stays ticked, so saving never drops it. The preview of a Destination shows the durations of the first Route that sends
to it, as its Root message does.

The set changes only the buttons of Root messages. Reminders keep **Still on it** and **Unack**; the
[Text commands](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#text-commands) of Bridges, the web UI and the API keep every action; and a press on a button that a
message showed before its set changed still runs its command. Each messenger lays out the buttons it shows as before:
Slack leaves the **Snooze…** menu out when the set shows no duration and no preset, and a bridge with a limit on buttons
still drops the Snooze buttons beyond it. On a bridge without buttons, the line "Reply in the Thread: …" lists only the
Text commands of the buttons the set shows, while every other Text command still works. A test message and the preview
of a Destination show the buttons of its set.

A saved change of a set updates the open Root messages of the Destinations that use it within seconds, as edits, which
notify nobody. With the Organization's set left at every button, a Snooze duration added to a Route shows on the next
update of its Root messages. The sets are `buttons` of the Organization and of each messenger Destination in the
[API](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/api.md); an outgoing webhook sends no buttons and has no set. Each change is recorded in the Audit
log.

### Text commands

Some messengers have no buttons, or a bridge does not offer them. A **Text command** is a Command written as a reply in a
Thread. Only a **Bridge** Destination takes them, and only when the bridge declares `thread_messages`. A Root message
that is not resolved then ends with a line such as "Reply in the Thread: ack · resolve · snooze 1h".

A reply is a Text command when its whole text, trimmed, in any case and with an optional leading `/`, is one of:

| Text command | Same in Russian | Command |
|---|---|---|
| `ack` | `взял` | Acknowledge |
| `unack` | `отпускаю` | Unacknowledge |
| `resolve` | `решено` | Resolve |
| `snooze <duration>` | `отложить <duration>` | Snooze |
| `unsnooze` | `вернуть` | Unsnooze |
| `still on it` | `ещё работаю` (also `еще работаю`) | Still on it |

The `<duration>` is one of the Route's Snooze durations, written in minutes, hours or days: `30m`, `4h` or `1d`, and
`30м`, `4ч` or `1д` in Russian. It can also name a [Snooze preset](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#snooze-presets): `tomorrow` (`завтра`) for the next
start of the working day, or the first day of the week (`organization.week_start`), such as `monday` (`понедельник`),
for the start of the working day on that day. So `snooze tomorrow`, `/Snooze Tomorrow` and `отложить понедельник` (with
the week starting on Monday) all work. A day is written as it stands alone: `понедельник`, `вторник`, `среда`,
`четверг`, `пятница`, `суббота`, `воскресенье`. Every word works whatever the Route's language is. The writer needs an
Account link, and the same permissions as for a button.

Muster answers a Text command in the Thread, for example "Done: Acknowledged #42", or with the reason it refused. A
duration that is not one of the Route's, or a day other than the first day of the week, is answered with what the Route
offers: "Snooze for one of: 1h, 4h, 24h, tomorrow, monday". The sentence and the durations are in the writer's profile
language, and the two preset words in the Route's language. A Route without Snooze durations takes no preset either, and
answers "This Alert Group's Route has no Snooze durations; nothing was changed." **Any other text is ignored and never
answered**, so people can talk in the Thread. The Thread of an older Root message works too: a Text command there acts
on the Alert Group like any other, also when the message is past its
[edit window](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/bridge.md#edit-windows). A Text command older than an hour (`bridge.event_max_age`) is
dropped. If the bridge forwards only the messages that mention its bot, the line says "mentioning the bot", and the
command must mention it. See [Write a bridge](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/write-a-bridge.md#events) for how a bridge reports a Thread
message.

## Ack timeouts, Reminders and auto-unacknowledge

Three timers make sure nobody forgets: one for an Alert Group nobody has taken, one for an Alert Group somebody took and
then went quiet, and an optional one that takes the Alert Group back. They are Route settings; the Paging profile turns
the first two on and the Informational profile turns everything off.

### Ack timeout

The ack timeout runs while an Alert Group is Firing. It starts at the Alert Group's first Publication in a Destination, and
starts over each time it becomes Firing again. Acknowledge, Snooze and Resolve stop it.

When nobody acknowledges, Muster sends up to three **notices**, at doubling intervals from the Route's first interval
(`route.ack_timeout`, 15 minutes on a Paging Route). With 15 minutes they come at 15, 45 and 105 minutes after the start.
Each is a Loud Thread reply that Mentions whom the Destination's entries for `ack_timeout`
say ([Whom a message mentions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md)). After the last notice the
Alert Group is **Unclaimed**: its message shows "⚠ Nobody has taken this", and the Alert Group list has an Unclaimed
filter, column and badge. Unclaimed ends when the Alert Group stops being Firing.

An Alert Group that has not been published, as when its Route has no Destination or only Broken ones, has no ack timeout
until its first Publication.

### Reminders

When somebody acknowledges, Muster reminds the Owner while the Alert Group stays open (`route.reminders`, on a Paging
Route). The first **Reminder** comes 4 hours after the acknowledgement, and each next interval doubles up to 24 hours: at 4,
12, 28 and 52 hours, then every 24 hours. Each is a Loud Thread reply that Mentions the Owner, with the buttons **Still
on it** and **Unack**.

- **Still on it** is recorded in the Timeline and does not change the interval. The Owner can also answer from the Alert
  Group page or the API.
- A **Takeover** starts the Reminders over for the new Owner. A Reopen into acknowledged continues them. A snoozed Alert
  Group gets none. There are no quiet hours.
- An Owner who is disabled or deleted loses the acknowledgement: the Alert Group becomes Firing without an Owner, and the
  ack timeout starts over.
- After Muster was down, the overdue notices of one Alert Group collapse into one Loud Thread reply that says how many
  were missed.
- Buttons on Reminders are signed like the others, but are not updated after a key rotation. Once the old key is removed,
  a press is answered "This button has expired; use the buttons on the Alert Group's message".

### Auto-unacknowledge

If an Owner goes home and says nothing, the Alert Group would stay acknowledged forever. **Auto-unacknowledge**
(`route.auto_unacknowledge`, off by default) takes it back. The Owner answers a Reminder by pressing **Still on it**, by
running any command on the Alert Group or by adding a Note before the next Reminder is due. When the Owner has answered
neither of the last two Reminders (`timers.auto_unacknowledge_after`), the next Reminder is not sent. Instead Muster
unacknowledges the Alert Group with the reason "No answer to the last two Reminders". It becomes Firing without an Owner,
a Loud Thread reply gives the reason and Mentions the Owner, and the ack timeout starts over.

For example, Carol acknowledges at 10:00 and goes home. She answers neither the Reminder at 14:00 nor the one at 22:00.
At 14:00 the next day, instead of a third Reminder, Muster unacknowledges the Alert Group and Mentions Carol, and the
ack timeout notices begin again.

The Alert Group page shows the next scheduled notice or Reminder.

# Broken Destinations

A Destination is **Broken** when Muster has decided that it cannot deliver there until something changes: the messenger
refused the bot for good, or the messenger kept failing until Muster ran out of patience. A Broken Destination is not an
error to dismiss. It means that the people it serves are not seeing their alerts, so Muster raises an alert about it.

This page explains when a Destination becomes Broken, what Muster does meanwhile, and how it recovers. For the steps
when `MusterDestinationBroken` fires, use the [runbook](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/operations/runbooks/MusterDestinationBroken.md).

## When a Destination becomes Broken

Every failed attempt is sorted into a class, and the class decides what happens:

Retry after
:   examples: the messenger answers `429` and says how long to wait

    Muster waits exactly that long and then sends the latest state. It is not counted as an attempt.

Transient
:   examples: a `5xx`, a timeout, a network error

    Muster retries with exponential backoff and jitter, from 2 seconds up to 5 minutes. After **10 attempts within 30
    minutes** (`delivery.transient_budget`) the Destination becomes Broken as *unavailable*.

Fatal
:   examples: the messenger refuses the bot or the request for good

    The Destination becomes Broken **at once**.

Unknown
:   examples: an answer Muster does not recognize

    Not retried. That one delivery ends as *Not delivered*, and the Destination stays healthy.

What is Fatal depends on the messenger:

- **Mattermost**: `401` or `403`, such as an invalid token or a bot removed from the channel, and `404` for the channel,
  which is not found or archived.
- **Slack**: a token that Slack refuses (`invalid_auth`, `token_revoked`, `token_expired` and the like), a missing
  scope, and a channel the bot cannot use: `channel_not_found`, `not_in_channel` for a bot removed from the channel, and
  `is_archived`. A `429` is not Fatal: Muster holds the whole Connection for the seconds Slack asks.
- **Telegram**: `401`, and "bot was kicked" or missing rights, such as a bot that lost its admin rights in the channel.
- **Bridge**: the bridge answers `forbidden`, for example because the bot was removed from the channel; the bridge's own
  text becomes the reason. A bridge that does not answer at all becomes Broken through the Transient budget.
- **Outgoing webhook**: `401`, `403`, `404` and `410`, and a request that the Organization's outbound address policy blocks,
  since every later request would be blocked too.

Several things look like trouble and do **not** make a Destination Broken:

- A deleted Root message. Muster posts the Alert Group again once, Quietly, with the note "The previous message was
  deleted at HH:MM"; if that one is deleted too, the pair is marked "deleted in the messenger" and left alone. In Slack
  this holds while the bot is still in the channel and the channel is not archived; see [Edits and deleted Root
  messages](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/slack.md#edits-and-deleted-root-messages).
- A Telegram Thread that lost the automatic copy of its Root message. The delivery state shows "Thread not attached".
- A delivery that ends as Not delivered, such as a template that failed or a request the receiver rejected with `400`.
- A Bridge Connection whose bridge cannot be reached. The Connection does not become Broken itself; its Destinations do,
  through their own deliveries, and its Connection check shows the cause.
- Failed polls of a bridge or of Telegram. The poller backs off.

## What you see

- **The Destination and its Routes** show a banner: "Broken since HH:MM: {reason}. Muster tries again every 5 minutes."
  The reason is the error the messenger answered, or "unavailable after repeated failures" and the last error.
- **The Internal alert** `MusterDestinationBroken` is raised, with the severity critical and the labels `destination` and
  `destination_name`. Muster routes it by your Routes like any other Alert, so a Route for Internal alerts should lead to
  a Destination of **another kind** than the one that failed: if all your Telegram Destinations are Broken because
  Telegram is unreachable, an alert that goes to Telegram says nothing. See [Routes and Group
  keys](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#examples).
- **The chart rule** `MusterDestinationBroken` is the backup for when Muster cannot deliver its own alert. Send it through
  an Alertmanager route that bypasses Muster. See [Internal alerts](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/internal-alerts.md) and the
  [metrics](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/metrics.md).
- The **Alert Groups** whose delivery waits show a delivery problem. The Alert Group list has the filter **Delivery
  problem**, and the page of each shows the state "Waiting: {Destination} is Broken".
- The **System status** page lists the Broken Destinations and since when.

## What Muster does meanwhile

Everything except the delivery to that Destination goes on: ingestion, routing, Alert Groups, commands from the web UI
and the API, timers, and every other Destination.

- **Deliveries wait instead of failing.** Nothing is dropped, and Muster does not spend retries on a Destination it knows
  is down.
- **Muster probes the Destination every 5 minutes** (`delivery.broken_probe_interval`). If a delivery is waiting, the
  probe is an attempt at the oldest one. If nothing is waiting, the probe is the **Destination check**, which asks the
  messenger whether the bot can still reach and post to the channel, without posting. An outgoing webhook has no
  Destination check, so its next delivery is the probe.
- **A new Alert Group is created and shown in Muster** as usual, but its first Publication waits. Its ack timeout does not
  start until it is published, so a Route whose only Destinations are Broken sends no notices.

## Recovery

The Broken state ends when **any** of these succeeds:

- the automatic probe;
- **Check** on the Destination page, which runs the Destination check at once;
- **Send test message** on the Destination page, when every step succeeds.

Fix the cause first. Typical fixes are a replaced bot token, adding the bot back to the channel or giving it its admin
rights again, enabling comments on a Telegram channel, correcting the address or the proxy of the Connection or the
**Outbound addresses** policy, or waiting out a messenger outage. When the fix is made, press **Check**; if you do
nothing, the next probe notices by itself, with no Alert Group activity needed. The Internal alert then resolves.

### What is sent after recovery

Muster sends only the **current state**. It does not replay what it missed.

| What happened during the outage | After recovery |
|---|---|
| An Alert Group started and is still firing | It is published as a Loud new message. |
| An Alert Group started and is no longer firing, but still open | It is published Quietly. |
| An Alert Group was already published, then changed | Its Root message is edited once to the current state, with no Thread reply. |
| An Alert Group started and resolved during the outage | It is **not** published; it stays in the Timeline and in the web UI. |
| Thread replies came due | They are not sent. |

Urgent Alert Groups are published first, within the Destination's rate limits.

An **outgoing webhook in events mode** is the exception: it is a stream of events, so every event that came due during the
outage is sent after recovery, in order per Alert Group, with none dropped and no age limit. An outgoing webhook in
template mode sends only the current state, like a messenger.

If an Alert Group resolved while its first Publication was still waiting for a retry, but the Destination was not yet
Broken, it is still posted, Quietly, with the note "Delivered late: started HH:MM, resolved HH:MM while this Destination
was unavailable".

## Republishing past an edit window

Muster edits a Root message for as long as its Alert Group is open, which can take days. Whether a messenger lets it
depends on the messenger. This is not about a Broken Destination, but the messages that Muster can no longer edit look
similar from the outside, so here is the whole picture.

Telegram
:   None for the bot's own messages. A bot may edit them at any age, so Muster never republishes because of age.

Slack
:   None known for a bot's own messages. Whether a window that a workspace sets applies to a bot's messages hours or
    days old has not been verified; an edit Slack refuses ends that delivery as Not delivered.

Mattermost
:   The server setting `ServiceSettings.PostEditTimeLimit`, which is unlimited (`-1`) by default. Keep the default. If
    an administrator sets a limit, Muster's edits of older Root messages may be refused, and what Mattermost then
    answers has not been verified. See [Keep posts editable](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/mattermost.md#keep-posts-editable).

Bridge
:   The bridge declares it in its info, as `limits.edit_max_age_seconds`, or declares none.

Outgoing webhook
:   Muster does not know the chat's rules. In template mode an `update` request that the chat refuses ends as Not
    delivered or Broken by the usual classes.

For a **Bridge** with an edit window, Muster stops editing a Root message 60 seconds before the window closes
(`bridge.edit_window_margin`). After that:

- A change that keeps the status of the Alert Group, such as new Alerts or Alerts that resolve, reaches only the Thread.
  The old Root message keeps the state of its last edit.
- A **change of status**, such as Acknowledge, Resolve, Snooze or a Reopen, makes Muster publish a **new Root message**
  Quietly, in the same channel, and reply under the old one: "This message is no longer updated. The current one:
  {link}". Later Thread replies go under the new Root message.
- The old message keeps working: a press on its buttons, or a Text command in its Thread, still acts on the Alert Group.
- The Alert Group page shows "Past the edit window: updated on the next change of status" until then.

A bridge may also refuse an edit before the declared time, with the reason `edit_window_closed`. Muster treats that as the
end of the window, not as a deletion. See [Edit windows](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/bridge.md#edit-windows) for the details.

## After you delete a Destination

A deleted Destination receives one last edit of each open Root message, "No longer updated here; current state in Muster:
{link}", and nothing after that. For a Bridge Root message past its edit window, the last edit is a Thread reply with
the same text. An outgoing webhook in events mode sends no final event, and its queued events end as Not delivered.

# Teams

A **Team** says who owns what: Routes and Destinations may name the Team that owns them, so that each team finds its
own configuration with a filter. Alerts and Alert Groups belong to Teams by their labels, so that each team finds its
Alert Groups. A Route may also deliver to the Destinations of the Teams of its Alerts
([Routing to Teams](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md)). Teams are managed in the web UI, under **Teams** in the main navigation, and
through the API under `/api/v1/teams`.

A Team costs nothing to start with. It may have no people and no Destinations and only name Alerts: it then groups and
filters them, and you add people, Destinations and Routes when you want them.

## In the web UI

**Teams** lists the Teams with the people in each, and above them the Team label, and below them the values of it that name no
Team ([Values without a Team](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#values-without-a-team)). **New Team** makes a Team; a value without a Team has its own
**Create Team**.

![The Teams list with the Teams payments and search, and the values checkout and Payments of the Team label that name no Team](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/teams/01-teams-list.png){ .screenshot }

A Team's page has its people, its identity provider groups, its Working hours, its Destinations, its
[Schedules](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/schedules.md) with who is On-call now, and **Which Alerts
belong to this Team**: the Team label with its slug and the Team matchers, with an example of what an Alert gets.

![The people of the Team payments: alice and bob added by hand, petr through the identity provider group](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/teams/02-team-people.png){ .screenshot }

The Destinations of a Team are the Destinations of the team, each with a
[Severity condition](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#severity-conditions). They receive the Alert Groups of the Routes that deliver
to the Teams, and nothing else: owning a Destination is another thing.

![The Destinations of the Team payments: payments-critical for critical Alert Groups and payments-notice for the rest](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/teams/04-team-destinations.png){ .screenshot }

## What a Team is

A Team has:

- a **name**, unique in the Organization, compared without regard to case;
- a **slug**, such as `payments`. A slug is 1 to 63 characters — lowercase letters, digits, `-`, `_` and `.` — and
  starts with a letter or a digit (`team.slug_format`), so `Payments` and `-x` are refused. It is unique in the
  Organization and compared exactly;
- a **description**;
- its **Team matchers**, which pick the Alerts that belong to it beside its slug;
- the **people** in it, which may be nobody, added by hand or through the groups of the identity provider;
- its **Team mentions**, the group that mentions it in each messenger ([Team mentions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#team-mentions));
- its **Working hours**: its own, else the Organization's.

What a Team owns
:   A Route or a Destination names its owning Team in the field `owning_team_id`; reads carry it as `owning_team`,
    with the Team's id, name and slug. `listRoutes` and `listDestinations` take the parameter `team`: a Team's id, or
    `mine` for the Teams you are in ("My Teams"). The owner only organizes: owning a Destination does not make it
    deliver to the Team, and a Route's owning Team adds no delivery.

What a Team does not grant
:   A Team grants no Permission and limits none: Roles stay the same for the whole Organization. A Responder in a Team
    cannot change the Team, and a Viewer in a Team is refused Acknowledge as any Viewer is. Only an Admin creates,
    edits and deletes Teams and adds people to them; every Role reads them.

Deleting a Team
:   The people leave it, its Working hours, Team matchers and Team mentions are removed, and the Routes and
    Destinations it owned have no owner from then on, each with a new version. Its slug and name are free at once for a
    new Team, and its slug no longer gives Alerts the Team. The Team itself is kept, marked deleted, for what still
    names it: the Alert Groups that had it among their Teams list it by its name, with `deleted` true.

Every change of a Team and of the people in it is recorded in the Audit log, and the metric `muster_team_info` has
one series per Team that is not deleted, with its name ([Metrics](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/metrics.md)). The operations are listed
in the [API reference](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/api.md).

## Which Alerts belong to a Team

An Alert belongs to a Team in two ways, and may belong to several Teams.

By the Team label
:   The Team label is an Organization setting, `team` by default (`organization.team_label`, `team_label` of the
    Organization's settings). An Alert whose value of the Team label, with the Static labels of its Integration,
    equals a Team's slug exactly belongs to that Team: with the Team "payments", `team="payments"` belongs to it and
    `team="Payments"` does not. The Team label cannot be a label that an Internal alert sets itself, such as
    `alertname`, `severity`, `integration`, `schedule` or `team_id`: such a label is refused with `422`
    `reserved_label`.

By Team matchers
:   A Team's `matchers` use the syntax of Route Matchers on any label — `=`, `!=`, `=~` and `!~`, regular expressions
    anchored at both ends, a missing label counting as empty — and each is enough on its own: an Alert that any one of
    them picks belongs to the Team. The Team with the matchers `namespace=~"checkout|billing"` and
    `pod=~"checkout-.*"` gets an Alert with `namespace="billing"` and one with `pod="checkout-api-1"`, and not one
    with `namespace="search"`. Matchers are saved with the Team, with `If-Match`, and replaced as a whole; an update
    that omits them keeps them. An expression that is not RE2 is refused with `422` `invalid_regex` at its pointer.
    A matcher with only a negative operator, such as `env!="prod"`, picks every Alert that lacks the label, Internal
    alerts included, because a missing label counts as empty.

Muster decides the Teams of an Alert when it starts firing — its first firing, and each firing after it resolved —
and records them on the Alert with the rule that matched: `label`, or `matcher` with the matcher's text. The Alerts
view of an Integration shows them as `teams`: the Teams of the Alert's latest firing, which replace those of an earlier
one. A Continuation is not a new firing and decides nothing. Saving a Team or the Team label also evaluates the Alerts
that are firing in open Alert Groups again, and only adds Teams to them
([Values without a Team](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#values-without-a-team)).

The Teams of an Alert Group are those of every Alert that ever joined it. They only grow: a Team stays among them
after its Alerts resolve. Each Team that joins records the Quiet lifecycle event `team_joined` in the Timeline, naming
the Team and the rule that brought it; `getAlertGroup` and `listAlertGroups` carry the Teams as `teams`, and outgoing
webhooks in events mode carry them in `alert_group.teams` ([Events mode](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/events.md)). An event
carries the state of the Alert Group, not a change: each `team_joined` lists every Team the Alert Group has at that
moment, the new one included. The
parameter `team` of `listAlertGroups` and `getAlertGroupCounts` takes a Team's id, or `mine` for the Alert Groups of
the Teams you are in ("My Teams"). The Teams of an Alert Group exist whatever its Route says: they do not change where
it is delivered.

A worked example
:   A rule generator sets `category`, `team` and `system` on every alert rule, so that the rule for a container that
    was killed for lack of memory, `KubernetesContainerOOMKilled`, reaches Muster with the labels
    `category="infra"`, `team="payments"` and `system="k8s"`. With the Team label `team` (the default), the slug of
    each Team is the value its rules carry: the Teams `payments`, `checkout` and `search` pick up their Alerts with no
    Team matcher at all, and the Alerts with `team="payments"` have `payments` among the Teams of their Alert Group.
    The labels `category` and `system` belong to the Route: `category="infra"` is the Matcher of the Route
    "Infrastructure" ([Routing to Teams](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#operations-and-team-destinations-by-severity)), and `system`
    is a label to group by. A Team matcher is for Alerts that carry no Team label, or a wrong one: the Team `devops`
    with the matcher `category="infra"` also gets every infrastructure Alert, whichever `team` it says: they belong to it.

Teams without people
:   A Team does not need people to be useful. A Team with nobody in it, such as `search` above, still names its Alerts
    and Alert Groups, so that the list of Alert Groups filters by it and an outgoing webhook event carries it in
    `alert_group.teams`, and may still have Destinations that receive the Alert Groups of its Alerts. People join it
    later, by hand or through the identity provider, and nothing has to be redone.

Test clusters with one shared namespace
:   When every team deploys into the same namespace of a test cluster, `namespace` does not tell them apart. Match
    another label instead: the Pod name (`pod=~"checkout-.*"`), an app label the workloads carry
    (`app_kubernetes_io_part_of="checkout"`), or put the Team label itself on the workloads, so that their Alerts carry
    `team="checkout"`. In the first two ways the Team `checkout` has the Team matchers `pod=~"checkout-.*"` and
    `app_kubernetes_io_part_of="checkout"`, and an Alert that either one picks belongs to it. In the third, no matcher
    is needed, and the Alerts of a production cluster and a test cluster reach the same Team. The Route
    [for test clusters](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#a-route-for-test-clusters) then sends them to the team's own Destinations.

## Values without a Team

A value of the Team label that names no Team — a typo, a team without a Team yet, or a case that differs, such as
`Payments` — adds nothing to the Alert Group: it is delivered to its Route's Destinations as before. Its Timeline
records the Quiet lifecycle event `unknown_team_value` with the value, once per value and Alert Group, and outgoing
webhooks in events mode receive it. Create a Team with that value as its slug, or add a Team matcher that picks those
Alerts, and the Alerts that start firing from then on belong to it. A value that is not a valid slug, such as
`Payments`, reaches a Team only through a Team matcher.

The values of the Team label seen without a Team in the last 14 days (`organization.team_values_window`) are listed by
`listTeamLabelValues` (`GET /api/v1/team-label-values`, Permission `teams:read`), the most recently seen first, each
with when it was last seen, how many Alerts carried it and `valid_slug`, false for a value that is not a valid slug.

Saving a Team — creating one, or changing its slug or its Team matchers — or saving a new Team label evaluates the
Alerts that are firing in open Alert Groups again, and only adds:

- An Alert that the save now brings to a Team records that Team, and the Team joins the Alert's Alert Group with a
  Quiet `team_joined`. The Root messages are updated Quietly, with no new message and no Mention, and no Escalation
  chain starts.
- A Team that an Alert no longer belongs to stays where it is, on the Alert and in its Alert Group.
- Saving the name, the description, the people or the Working hours of a Team evaluates nothing. A new name edits the
  Root messages of the open Alert Groups that have the Team once, Quietly, so that their field Teams shows it.

So "Create Team" for a value without a Team brings the Team the open Alert Groups of that value at once: with an open,
firing Alert Group of `team="checkout"` and no such Team, creating the Team with the slug `checkout` adds it to the
Alert Group, and `checkout` leaves the list of values without a Team. The evaluation runs on any replica shortly after
the save; a replica that stops during it leaves the rest to another one, which goes on where it stopped. It records no
`unknown_team_value` and leaves an Alert Group that gets no new Team as it is.

With the default Team label `team` and no Teams defined yet, every Alert that carries a `team` label records
`unknown_team_value` in its Alert Group's Timeline and sends it to outgoing webhooks in events mode. Create the Teams,
or change the Team label to a label your Alerts do not use for something else, to avoid it.

## People

An Admin adds Users to a Team by hand and removes them, through the API (`addTeamUser`, `removeTeamUser`). A User may be
in several Teams. The list of a Team's people shows each person's Role, how they are in the Team — `by_hand` for a
person an Admin added, `idp` for a person the identity provider put in it — and the messengers they linked.

- Disabling a User keeps them in their Teams.
- Deleting a User takes them out of every Team.

## Schedules

A Team may have Schedules that say who in it is On-call. The Team's On-call is the On-call of its main Schedule, which
Mentions of the Team's On-call use, and a Team that has Schedules cannot be deleted. The Team page lists its Schedules
and shows who is On-call now. See [Schedules](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/schedules.md): Rotation layers, several Schedules, Overrides, gaps and the
iCal feed.

## Membership from the identity provider

A Team may name groups of the identity provider in `idp_entries`; the people in those groups who sign in through
OIDC are in the Team. Each entry has a group and, optionally, the claim it reads:

![The identity provider groups of the Team payments: the entry teams/payments/*, which reads the groups claim of the OIDC settings](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/teams/03-team-idp.png){ .screenshot }

The claim of an entry
:   An entry reads its own claim, by its exact name, or, without one, the groups claim of the OIDC settings. Muster
    reads it from the ID token, and from userinfo when the ID token lacks it, as for the Role mapping; GitLab, for
    example, puts its role claims such as `https://gitlab.org/claims/groups/maintainer` in userinfo
    ([Sign-in with GitLab](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/sign-in/oidc-gitlab.md#3-map-groups-to-roles)).

Exact groups and subtrees
:   An entry `teams/payments` matches that group exactly, letter case included. A subtree `teams/payments/*` matches
    `teams/payments` and every group below it, such as `teams/payments/oncall`, at `/` boundaries only: never
    `teams/paymentsx`. There are no other wildcards; a `*` anywhere else is refused.

Sign-in and re-checks
:   At every OIDC sign-in, and at every successful background re-check, a person joins each Team with an entry that
    matches them and leaves each Team they are in only through entries that no longer match. This happens whether or
    not **Sync Role at sign-in** is on, and never changes a Role. A re-check reads only the refreshed ID token: a claim
    it lacks counts as no groups once an earlier re-check found it in a refreshed ID token, and until then — as for a
    claim found only in userinfo — an entry of that claim keeps the person as the last sign-in left them. A refused or failed re-check changes no membership.

People added by hand
:   The identity provider never takes out a person an Admin added. A person added by hand whom an entry matches has
    both sources, `by_hand` and `idp`, and the entries that matched them. Removing such a person by hand takes away
    only `by_hand`: they stay in the Team through the identity provider until a sign-in finds that no entry matches
    them; removing by hand a person who is in the Team only through the identity provider is refused with `404`.

Identity providers without an offline token
:   GitLab, and any provider that grants no offline token, cannot be re-checked in the background, so its people join
    and leave Teams only when they sign in.

A change of a Team's entries changes nobody's membership at once: each person follows the new entries at their next
sign-in or re-check. Every change the identity provider makes is recorded in the Audit log as `team.user_added` or
`team.user_removed` by the system, with the source `idp` and the entry that matched.

For example, a Team whose people are the members of the GitLab group `corp/payments` and its subgroups:

```json
"idp_entries": [{"group": "corp/payments/*"}]
```

With the groups claim `groups`, which GitLab fills with a person's groups and the groups below them, this takes in a
person of `corp/payments/oncall` and nobody of `corp/paymentsx`. Subtree entries are safe here, also with a role claim:
a Team grants no Permission and no Role.

## Team mentions

A **Team mention** is the group that mentions a Team through one Connection: when a message mentions the Team in a
channel of that Connection, it mentions that group, whose members the messenger notifies. A Team has at most one Team
mention per Connection, in `team_mentions`, each with its Connection and its group:

```json
"team_mentions": [
  {"connection_id": "CNAGK7M3QX9P2R", "group": "payments_team"},
  {"connection_id": "CNB4R8J2M6Q1TX", "group": "payments-team", "group_id": "S0FAKEPAYTEAM"}
]
```

The Team page edits them in the section **How to mention the Team**, and a Destination's entry "Team" mentions them:
[Whom a message mentions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md) says which entries, and what each messenger supports.

What each messenger takes:

Mattermost
:   The name of a Mattermost group, as `@payments_team` mentions it: its members are mentioned in a Root message and in
    a Thread reply, and a custom group can always be mentioned. A bot with the role Member and no added permission
    reads the groups of the server, so **Check connection** reads them in its step **Groups**, and the API lists them
    for the Team mention (`listConnectionGroups`). A server that does not list them to the bot fails nothing: the step
    says so, and a Team mention is still saved by the name you type. This was checked on Mattermost 11.2.2 with the
    Entry licence; groups synced from LDAP were not checked (F-121, F-122, F-123).

Slack
:   A Slack user group, chosen from those the Connection lists, by its handle and its id. `<!subteam^…>` mentions the
    group's members in a Root message and in a Thread reply (F-126). The bot needs the optional scope
    `usergroups:read`; until the Connection's check has found it, a Team mention for a Slack Connection is refused.
    See [The scopes](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/slack.md#the-scopes). A workspace may let only its Admins and Owners create user
    groups (F-125), so ask one of them for the group.

Bridge
:   A group name of the bridge's messenger, when the bridge declares group mentions in its `info`
    (`group_mentions`). Muster does not list the groups of a bridge: you type the name.

Telegram
:   Telegram has no group mentions, so a Telegram Connection takes no Team mention. The Team's On-call is still
    mentioned, as a person, by the entry "On-call of a Team".

Saving a Team mention for a Connection that cannot carry one — Telegram, a Slack Connection without `usergroups:read`,
a bridge that declares no group mentions, or a Notification gateway — is refused with `422` and the code
`group_mentions_unsupported` at the entry's `connection_id`. A read of a Team lists under `mention_connections` every
Connection that may have a Destination, with the Team's Team mention for it or none, and for those that cannot mention
a group the reason: `telegram_no_groups`, `usergroups_read_missing` or `bridge_no_group_mentions`. A Notification
gateway is not listed.

A Team without a Team mention for a Connection is mentioned as nobody through it: Muster never mentions the Team's
On-call instead. A Team mention whose Connection later loses the ability — a Slack app reinstalled without
`usergroups:read`, a bridge that stops declaring group mentions — stays, reads `available` false with the reason, and
mentions nobody until the ability is back. A save that sends it again unchanged, with the same Connection, group and
user group id, keeps it and is not refused; only a new or changed Team mention is checked against what its Connection
can mention. Deleting a Connection deletes its Team mentions, each recorded in the Audit
log of its Team; a change of a Team's Team mentions is recorded in its Audit log as every other change of the Team.

Which buttons a Destination's messages show is a separate setting of the Destination:
[Button sets](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#button-sets).

## Working hours

Working hours are days of the week, a start and an end on the 24-hour clock, and a time zone. The end is later than
the start on the same day: Working hours never span midnight. Holidays are not part of them.

The Organization's Working hours
:   Monday to Friday, from the start of the working day — the time at which the Snooze presets end, `09:00` by
    default — to `18:00`, in the Organization's time zone (`organization.working_hours`). An Admin changes the days
    (`working_days`) and the end (`workday_end`) with the Organization's settings; an end that is not later than the
    start is refused.

A Team's Working hours
:   A Team's own Working hours, with days, a start, an end and an IANA time zone such as `Europe/Berlin`
    (`own_working_hours`), else the Organization's. A Team without its own moves with the Organization: a new start of
    the working day moves the Team's start too.

# Routing to Teams

One Route can serve many teams. Each Destination of a Route, and each Destination of a Team, may be limited by
Severity level, and a Route may also deliver its Alert Groups to the Destinations of the Teams of their Alerts. Every
Destination then shows the same Alert Group with one status. The settings are on the Route form, and each links here:
[the Severity condition of a Destination](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#severity-conditions), ["Also to the Teams from the
label"](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#also-to-the-teams-from-the-label) with ["Run the Teams' Escalation chains"](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#also-to-the-teams-from-the-label),
the Group key's [Team label](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#teams-that-want-their-own-alert-groups), the
[preview](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#preview-where-alerts-go), the [Owning Team](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#what-a-team-is) and the
[Minimum severity](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/what-critical-changes.md).

The feature is optional. Many installations need only two operations Destinations — one for critical Alert Groups and one
for the rest — and a Route that names both with a Severity condition each. Teams and their Destinations come in when
teams want their own Destinations.

## Severity conditions

A Route names its Destinations in `destination_entries`, each with a `severity_condition`, and a Team names the
Destinations of the Team the same way:

`any`
:   Every Severity level, the default.

`critical`
:   Only an Alert Group whose Severity level is critical.

`below_critical`
:   Every level but critical.

The condition is checked against the Alert Group's Severity level: the highest level of its firing Alerts, raised to
the Route's Minimum severity. A Destination may be named by several Routes and Teams, each with its own condition, and
is reached when the condition of any of its entries is met.

![The Destinations of the Route Infrastructure: infra-critical for critical Alert Groups and infra-notice for those below critical](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/teams/05-route-destinations.png){ .screenshot }

Until 0.3.0 Routes still read and take `destination_ids`, derived from `destination_entries`: a request that sends only
`destination_ids` keeps the condition of each Destination it keeps and gives a new one `any`.

## Also to the Teams from the label

With "Also to the Teams from the label" (`deliver_to_teams`) on, an Alert Group of the Route also goes to the
Destinations of each of its Teams — the Teams its Alerts belong to by the Team label or by Team matchers
([Teams](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#which-alerts-belong-to-a-team)) — whose condition its Severity level meets. With it off, the Alert
Group goes only to the Route's own Destinations. The owning Team of a Route adds nothing: it only organizes and
filters.

![The option Also to the Teams from the label on the Route form, switched on, with its hint](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/teams/06-route-teams-option.png){ .screenshot }

"Run the Teams' Escalation chains" (`run_team_chains`) is stored with the option; it acts once Escalation chains run.

### Operations and team Destinations by Severity

Operations has `infra-critical` and `infra-notice`, and the Team `payments` has `payments-critical` and
`payments-notice`. The Route "Infrastructure" takes `category="infra"`, names `infra-critical` for `critical` and
`infra-notice` for `below_critical`, and has "Also to the Teams from the label" on. The Team `payments` names
`payments-critical` for `critical` and `payments-notice` for `below_critical`.

A warning Alert of `team="payments"` makes one Alert Group with one Root message in `infra-notice` and one in
`payments-notice`, and none in the critical Destinations. If a critical Alert joins it, the Alert Group is also published
in `infra-critical` and `payments-critical`; its messages in the notice Destinations stay and keep being updated, also
after the critical Alert resolves. The Destinations of an Alert Group only grow, and only when a Team joins it —
which adds that Team's Destinations — or its Severity level rises: a level that falls again, or a new Alert that
brings neither, adds no Destination below critical where the Alert Group was not published before.

## One Alert Group, one status

An Alert Group has one status and one list of Alerts in every Destination it is in. Acknowledge it from `payments-notice`
and its message in `infra-notice` shows the same Owner; resolve it anywhere and every message shows it resolved. The
Alert Group page names its Teams, and the deliveries of the Alert Group show where it went.

![The header of the Alert Group KubernetesContainerOOMKilled, with its Teams: payments, by the Team label](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/teams/08-alert-group-teams.png){ .screenshot }

The delivery state of the Alert Group (`listAlertGroupDeliveries`) names, for each Destination, the entries that reached
it — the Route, or a Team — with their conditions.

When an Alert Group reaches a Destination after its first Publication elsewhere — a new Alert raised its Severity level
into the Destination's condition, or brought a Team — the lifecycle event `destination_joined` publishes it there:
Loudly, with the Mentions of a new Alert Group, while it is firing; Quietly while it is acknowledged or snoozed. The
other Destinations see only an update of their Root message. During a Storm of the Route the Destination counts it in
its Storm summary instead, unless it is Urgent.

A change of configuration publishes Quietly and never with `destination_joined`: a raised Minimum severity, or a new
Team whose Alerts are already in an open Alert Group, adds that Alert Group to the Destinations it now reaches without
a Mention.

### Teams that want their own Alert Groups

In a Route whose Group key lacks the Team label, Alerts of different Teams with the same key share one Alert Group,
shown in the Destinations of all of them: an Alert of "search" that joins a firing Alert Group of "payments" is published
in `search-notice` with `destination_joined`. Teams that want their own Alert Groups add the Team label, `team` by
default, to the Group key of the Route: with `alertname`, `system` and `team` as the Group key, the "search" Alert gets
an Alert Group of its own, with its own status, and the Route form offers **Add team to the key** whenever the Route
delivers to the Teams and its Group key lacks the Team label.

## Preview where alerts go

Before saving a Route, ask where its Alerts would go. The preview (`previewGroupKey`, `routes:write`) takes the Alerts
reported firing in the last day that the Route would take, as the Group key preview does, and the settings of the form
as they are, saved or not: the Minimum severity, the Destinations with their conditions, "Also to the Teams from the
label" and the proposed Group key. A setting left out is the saved Route's; a Route that is not saved starts from none. The answer has a `delivery` part when the request names a saved Route
(`route_id`), gives `destination_entries` or gives `deliver_to_teams`; a Minimum severity alone does not add it.

The answer lists the largest Alert Groups the Alerts would form under the key, five by default, each with its Group
key values, its number of Alerts, its Severity level raised to the Minimum severity, its Teams and the Destinations it
would reach, with the entries that reach each: the Route's, or a Team's. The Destinations of an Alert Group only grow, so the
preview follows each Alert Group as its Alerts arrived, by their `startsAt`: it reaches the Destinations of every
Severity level the group rose through, and of each Team from the Alert that brought it. A Group key without the
severity can thus list Destinations for `warning` and for `critical`, as the group reached them in turn; the Severity
level shown is the highest. Alerts that started at the same time count together. When an Alert has no `startsAt`
there is no order, and the preview lists the Destinations the group may reach: those of each Alert's own level. For every Destination that any of the Alert
Groups reaches it counts how many of all of them reach it, those listed or not, so that a Destination reached by
hundreds of Alert Groups is seen before the Route is saved.

Teams are read as they are saved: the Team label and the Team matchers of the Teams now, not any change you have not
saved on the Teams page. When the Route delivers to the Teams and the Group key lacks the Team label, the answer says
so (`shared_key_warning`), and the form offers to add the Team label to the key.

The preview only reads: it saves nothing, writes no Audit log entry, publishes nothing and sends no message.

![Where alerts go: the Alert Groups the Route Infrastructure would form, with their Teams and the Destinations each would reach](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/teams/07-route-preview.png){ .screenshot }

## When you change a Route or a Team

A save of a Route's Destination entries or of "Also to the Teams from the label", and a save of a Team's Destination
entries, reach the open Alert Groups at once, without a lifecycle event in their Timelines:

- A new Destination, a changed condition that is met now, or the Destinations of the Teams when "Also to the Teams
  from the label" is turned on add Root messages, Quietly and without `destination_joined`: each open Alert Group is
  published in each of those Destinations that it reaches at its current Severity level and was not in. A save
  publishes only through the entries it added or changed: an Alert Group whose level fell from critical, and that was
  never published in a Destination below critical, stays out of it when an unrelated entry is saved. A condition is
  checked against the level as it is: a Destination named for `critical` receives only the open critical Alert
  Groups. A change of configuration starts no Escalation chain.
- No change removes a Root message by a condition. A Destination whose condition is not met any more, or a condition
  narrowed back, keeps its Root messages, which stay current until their Alert Groups resolve.
- A Destination taken away — removed from the Route or from the Team, reached only through the Teams when "Also to the
  Teams from the label" is turned off, or named by a deleted Team — gets the final edit "No longer updated here" with a
  link to the Alert Group in Muster, for each open Alert Group that no entry still names it for. If the Alert Group
  reaches it again later — its Severity level rises into a condition, or a Team that names it joins — it is published
  there afresh with `destination_joined`, Loudly while it is firing. If the final edit has not been carried out yet, as
  while the Destination is Broken, the Root message is kept instead and edited Quietly back to the current state, with no
  `destination_joined` and no Mention (C-26.FR-5). An entry that still
  names it keeps the Root message, whether its condition is met or not: in the example above, if the Route also names
  `payments-notice` for `critical`, removing `payments-notice` from the Team keeps the Root message of a warning Alert
  Group there.

## A Route for test clusters

Test alerts should reach the team that owns the workload, in its own Destinations, and Page nobody: the Teams' Destinations still deliver as they are set, but no Escalation chain starts. A Route "Test
clusters" placed above the Route "Infrastructure", with the Matcher `cluster=~".*-stage|.*-preprod"`, no Destination of
its own, "Also to the Teams from the label" on and "Run the Teams' Escalation chains" off, does that: the Alert Group of
`team="payments"` from `shop-stage` goes to the Destinations of the Team `payments` and to nothing else, with their
Severity conditions, and starts no Escalation chain of the Team. Routes are checked in order, and the first one that
picks an Alert takes it, so it never reaches "Infrastructure". An Alert of a test cluster whose `team` value names no
Team goes nowhere: the Route has no Destination of its own, so the Alert Group is only in the web UI. Where that
matters, give the Route one Destination of its own, such as a Destination for test alerts.

The Teams of such Alerts are those of the Team label and the Team matchers, as everywhere. On a test cluster where
every team shares one namespace, use a Team matcher on the Pod name or an app label, or put the Team label on the
workloads ([Teams](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#which-alerts-belong-to-a-team)).

A Minimum severity on the Route would make every test alert critical, and so [Urgent](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/what-critical-changes.md); leave it
at none.

# What critical changes

An Alert Group whose Severity level is **critical** is **Urgent**. Nothing else makes an Alert Group Urgent, and there
is no separate switch: since 0.2.0 the urgent mark of a Route and the Organization's "critical is Urgent" no longer act
([Urgent](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#urgent)). This page lists everything that Urgent changes, and then what the
[Minimum severity](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routes-and-group-keys.md#minimum-severity) of a Route adds, so that you know what a critical Alert,
or a Route that raises its Alert Groups to critical, makes Muster do.

The Severity level of an Alert Group is the highest level of its firing Alerts, raised to the Minimum severity of its
Route. It follows the Alerts as they fire and resolve, so an Alert Group may become Urgent and stop being Urgent again.

## What an Urgent Alert Group changes

The order of the delivery queue
:   The delivery queue of a Destination takes Urgent Alert Groups first. They never pass the rate limit of the
    Destination: an Urgent Alert Group waits for its place within the limit like any other, only before the rest.
    The edits of Root messages that only a change of configuration made, such as a new button set, come after the other
    work of their urgency: those of Urgent Alert Groups right after the Root messages of Urgent Alert Groups and the
    edits that Alerts and Commands make to them, and before anything that is not Urgent; those of the other Alert Groups
    last, after the Root messages, the edits, the Thread replies and the events of outgoing webhooks of the other Alert
    Groups, for as long as those use up the rate limit ([Organization](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/configure/organization.md#alert-handling)).
    After an outage of a Destination, the Alert Groups that were not published are published Urgent first
    ([Broken Destinations](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/broken-destinations.md#what-is-sent-after-recovery)).

Individual publication during a Storm
:   When a Route creates more new Alert Groups in a minute than its Storm threshold, each of its Destinations gets one
    Storm summary instead of a message per Alert Group. An Urgent Alert Group is not held back by it: it is published
    on its own, at once and ahead of the queue ([Storm summaries](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/loud-and-quiet.md#storm-summaries)). The same holds
    for a Destination that an Alert Group joins during the Storm: unless the Alert Group is Urgent, the Destination
    counts it in its Storm summary ([Routing to Teams](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#one-alert-group-one-status)).

The end of a Snooze or of an acknowledgement on a rise
:   When a rise in Severity level makes an Alert Group Urgent, it ends a Snooze, unless the Snooze was set while the
    Alert Group was already Urgent. It also removes the acknowledgement where the Route's setting "Rise to Urgent
    removes the acknowledgement" (`route.urgent_rise_removes_ack`) is on, which it is by default. The Alert Group is
    Firing again, and the change is Loud, with the reason. It Mentions the Owner it removed and the Destination's
    Mention entries for `rise_to_urgent` ([Loud and Quiet](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/loud-and-quiet.md#which-changes-are-loud),
    [Statuses and commands](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#transitions-muster-makes-on-its-own)). A rise that changes
    nothing else is Quiet.

The filter and the column of the list
:   The list of [Alert Groups](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/alert-groups.md#in-the-web-ui) has the filter **Urgent**, which shows only the Urgent
    Alert Groups or only those that are not, and the column **Urgent**, which marks each Urgent Alert Group. The API
    takes the parameter `urgent` of `listAlertGroups`.

`urgent` in outgoing webhook events
:   An event in events mode carries the Alert Group with `alert_group.urgent`, true exactly when its Severity level is
    critical ([Events mode](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/events.md)), and a template reads it as `.AlertGroup.Urgent`
    ([Templates](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/templates.md#what-the-templates-read)).

The count in Storm summaries
:   The Storm summary of a Route says how many Alert Groups the Storm holds and how many of them are Urgent, such as
    "Storm on payments: 24 Alert Groups, 3 urgent", and a template reads `.Storm.UrgentCount`.

Urgent is not a status, and no person sets it: the Root message of an Alert Group shows its Severity level, and the
page of the Alert Group marks it Urgent.

## What the Minimum severity adds

The Minimum severity of a Route raises the Severity level of its Alert Groups, never that of their Alerts. With the
Minimum severity critical, every Alert Group of the Route is critical, and so Urgent, from its first Alert, even where
every Alert says `warning`: everything above applies to it, and the Destinations of the Route with the Severity
condition `critical` receive it ([Severity conditions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#severity-conditions)). With the Minimum
severity `warning`, an Alert Group of `info` Alerts is a warning one, which is not Urgent, and a Destination with the
condition `below_critical` still receives it.

A change of the Minimum severity applies at once to the open Alert Groups of the Route, and is gentler than a rise of
an Alert:

- It never removes an acknowledgement and never ends a Snooze, also where it makes an Alert Group Urgent: the Timeline
  records a Quiet `urgency_raised` with the reason "Minimum severity of the Route", and nothing else.
- It starts no Escalation chain, and the Root messages are edited Quietly.
- The Alert Groups it raises into the Severity condition of a Destination reach that Destination Quietly, with no
  Mention ([When you change a Route or a Team](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md#when-you-change-a-route-or-a-team)).
- A lowered Minimum severity records nothing and lowers the level of the Alert Groups, as a falling level does. The
  messages that an Alert Group already has stay where they are.

The Alerts keep their own level: the Alerts view, the Alerts of the Alert Group page and `alerts[]` of outgoing webhook
events show what each Alert says.

# Schedules

A **Schedule** says who in a Team is On-call and when. It is made of Rotation layers, in which people take Shifts in
turn, with Overrides on top. Schedules are managed through the API under `/api/v1/schedules`; nothing about a Schedule
sends a message.

## What a Schedule is

A Schedule belongs to one Team and has:

- a **name**, unique in the Team, compared without regard to case;
- a **time zone**, such as `Europe/Berlin`. A Schedule created without one takes the Organization's time zone of that
  moment and keeps it when the Organization's changes later (`schedule.time_zone`);
- its **Rotation layers**, from the lowest to the highest;
- its **Overrides**;
- its **gap alert**, whether it reports its gaps as an Internal alert (see
  [Gaps and the Internal alert](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/schedules.md#gaps-and-the-internal-alert)).

The **On-call** of a Schedule at a moment is the person of its Override then; otherwise the person on Shift in the
highest Rotation layer that has a Shift then; otherwise nobody, which is a **gap**. `getScheduleOnCall` gives the
**Result** of a period — by default from now to 14 days ahead (`schedule.gap_view`), at most 67 days — cut into
segments, each with the person On-call or nobody, together with the Shifts of each Rotation layer and the Overrides.
Muster computes it on its own clock, so the development clock moves it.

Only Admins create, change and delete Schedules (`schedules:write`); everyone reads them (`schedules:read`). Every
change is recorded in the Audit log, and the metric `muster_schedule_info` has one series per Schedule, with its name
([Metrics](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/metrics.md)). The operations are listed in the [API reference](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/api.md).

## Rotation layers

A Schedule has at most 20 Rotation layers. A Rotation layer has a name and the people who take Shifts in turn, in
order, at most 100. A Shift lasts:

- a **day**, handed over every day at the handover time;
- a **week**, handed over on a day of the week at the handover time;
- a number of **whole hours**, given as `shift_seconds`, a multiple of 3600 such as 43200 for 12 hours, up to 28 days.

The first Shift starts on the start date, a day from 2000-01-01 to 2100-12-31, at the handover time, with the first
person of the list. Times are wall-clock times in the Schedule's time zone, so a handover at 10:00 stays at 10:00 when
the clocks change. A time that the clocks skip when they move forward, such as 02:30 on the night of the change, is
the moment they move; a time that happens twice when they move back is its first occurrence.

A Rotation layer may be **limited** to some days of the week and, optionally, a time of day, such as Monday to Friday
11:00–19:00. Outside the limits it has no Shift. A time of day whose end is earlier than its start crosses midnight,
and each window belongs to the day it starts on: Friday 19:00–09:00 runs from Friday 19:00 to Saturday 09:00.

A higher Rotation layer wins wherever it has a Shift. [A worked example](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/schedules.md#a-worked-example) follows.

A Rotation layer may only add people who can be Paged: Users in the Team who are neither Viewers nor disabled. Adding
anyone else is refused with `422` `not_pageable` at the person, with the reason `viewer`, `disabled` or `not_in_team`.
Someone who becomes one of these later stays in the Rotation layer: their Shifts are skipped — the Rotation layer
below, or a gap, takes the time — and the Result lists a warning about them until an Admin removes them.

## A worked example

The Schedule "Main" of the Team "payments", in the time zone `Europe/Berlin`, has two Rotation layers. Both hand over
on Monday at 10:00, and week 1 below is a week in which both lists are at their first person:

Around the clock, below
:   Weekly Shifts handed over on Monday at 10:00 among Petr, Sergey and Olga.

Working hours 11–19, above
:   Weekly Shifts handed over on Monday at 10:00, limited to weekdays from 11:00 to 19:00, among Olga and Petr.

Each week the next person of each list is on Shift:

| | Week 1 | Week 2 | Week 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Around the clock, below | Petr | Sergey | Olga |
| Working hours 11–19, above | Olga | Petr | Olga |

Who is On-call on a Wednesday of week 1:

- **At 15:00** both Rotation layers have a Shift, and the upper one wins: **Olga** is On-call.
- **At 21:00** the upper Rotation layer has no Shift, as its Shifts end at 19:00, so the lower one has it: **Petr**
  is On-call. He is also On-call on the Saturday at 15:00, as the upper Rotation layer has no Shifts at the weekend.

In week 2 the same Wednesday gives Petr at 15:00 and Sergey at 21:00. The handover of each Rotation layer is at 10:00
on the Monday, so on the Monday of week 2 Petr is still On-call until 10:00, then Sergey until 11:00, and from 11:00
Petr, who is on Shift in the upper Rotation layer.

The page of the Schedule shows the two weeks, with a row for each Rotation layer, the row "Overrides" and, below them,
the row "Result — who is On-call", which is what Muster uses. In the picture, Sergey has been disabled: his Shifts in
the lower Rotation layer are hatched, and the evenings, nights and the weekend of week 2, when only he is on Shift
there, are gaps in red, as is the hour from 10:00 to 11:00 of its Monday, after his handover and before the upper
Rotation layer begins. The section "Warnings" lists them. [Gaps](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/schedules.md#gaps-and-the-internal-alert) says what happens
then.

![The page of the Schedule Main of the Team payments: who is On-call now, and the Warnings with the disabled Sergey and the gaps](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/schedules/01-schedule-page.png){ .screenshot }

![The two-week view of the Schedule Main: the rows of the Rotation layers Working hours 11–19 and Around the clock, the Overrides and the row Result, with the gaps of week 2 in red](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/schedules/02-schedule-view.png){ .screenshot }

## Several Schedules

A Team may have several Schedules, such as a first and a second line. One of them is its **main Schedule**, whose
On-call is the Team's On-call: the first Schedule of a Team is its main one, and an Admin makes another one main with
`main` set to true, which takes the mark off the previous one. The main Schedule cannot be unmarked on its own; deleting
it leaves the Team without one until an Admin marks another.

For example, "payments" has its "Main" Schedule, whose On-call is the Team's, and a Schedule "Second line" with one
Rotation layer, "Second line, working hours", of the people who back the first line up. A Mention of the Team's On-call
([Mentions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md)) names the person of "Main"; an [Escalation chain](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/escalation-chains.md) whose step Pages the
On-call of a Schedule names the Schedule itself, so a step can Page "Second line" while the Team's On-call stays that of
"Main".

Each Schedule has its own Rotation layers, Overrides and gap alert: a Schedule that is not main reports its gaps only
when an Admin turns its gap alert on (see [Gaps and the Internal alert](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/schedules.md#gaps-and-the-internal-alert)). "On call now"
([below](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/schedules.md#on-call-now)) shows the main Schedule of each Team only.

A Team that has Schedules cannot be deleted: `deleteTeam` answers `409` `team_in_use` and names them.

## Overrides

An **Override** makes one person On-call from one time to another, on top of every Rotation layer: within its span it
wins over all of them, and so it also closes a gap. It may name the person whose Shift it takes, which changes nothing
in the On-call. Where Overrides overlap, the one created last wins.

Who may make them
:   Responders and Admins (`schedule-overrides:write`). A Responder must be in the Schedule's Team, else `409`
    `not_in_team`; an Admin need not. The person who takes the Shift must be able to be Paged, else `422`
    `not_pageable` at `user_id`; the person whose Shift it takes may be anyone.

When
:   An Override may be planned any time ahead, but it may not rewrite the past. It starts at most 5 minutes before now
    (`422` `out_of_range` at `start`), ends after its start and after now, and lasts at most 31 days (`422`
    `out_of_range` at `end`). A span that would overlap 1000 other Overrides of the Schedule is refused the same way.

Changing and deleting
:   Before an Override begins, everything in it may change, and deleting it removes it. Once it has begun, only its end
    may change — another start or person is refused with `422` `unsupported` — and deleting it ends it now: the part
    that has passed stays in the Schedule. Once it has ended, it can be neither changed nor deleted (`409`
    `override_ended`), so the past stays as it was.

No messages
:   Overrides need no approval and send no message, and neither do Shifts: who is On-call is shown by Muster and its
    API, and every Override is recorded in the Audit log with its people and span.

Olga, who is On-call on Wednesday from 11:00 to 19:00 in the example above, gives that Shift to Petr. On the page of
the Schedule she selects "Add Override", chooses "I give my Shift to…", selects her segment in the row "Result" and
Petr as the person who takes it. The Override is exactly that segment: the Result shows Petr On-call on Wednesday from
11:00 to 19:00, and nothing else changes. "I take the Shift of…" is the form the other way round: the person who makes
the Override takes the Shift, and chooses whose Shift it is.

![The Override form of olga: the mode I give my Shift to..., the segment of Wednesday 11:00 to 19:00 chosen in the row Result, and petr as the person who takes it](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/schedules/03-override-form.png){ .screenshot }

## iCal feed

Each User may subscribe a calendar application to a personal **iCal feed** of the times when they are On-call. The
feed has one event for each span in which the User is On-call in a Schedule of any of their Teams, named
"{Team} · {Schedule}", from 7 days back to 60 days ahead (`ical.window`), counted in whole days in UTC:

- It follows the Result: where a higher Rotation layer or someone else's Override has the time, it is not in the feed.
  With the two Rotation layers above, a week in which Petr has the lower Rotation layer shows only its nights and its
  weekend.
- An Override that makes the User On-call is an event of its own span; adjacent spans of one Schedule are otherwise
  one event, so a Rotation layer limited to Friday 19:00–09:00 is one event from Friday 19:00 to Saturday 09:00.
- Times are in UTC, so a calendar application shows them in its own time zone, across the changes of the clocks too.

The link
:   `issueMyIcalFeed` (`POST /api/v1/me/ical-feed`) gives the link, `MUSTER_PUBLIC_URL/ical/{token}.ics`, from the web
    session only. It is shown once: Muster keeps only a hash of its token (`ical.token`, starting with `mstr_cal_`), so
    copy it at once. `getMyIcalFeed` tells whether a feed exists and when it was issued.

A new link revokes the old one
:   Each User has one feed. Issuing a new link stops the old one at once, and `revokeMyIcalFeed` stops the current one;
    a stopped link, and the link of a disabled or deleted User, answers `404`. Deleting a User deletes their feed.
    After a link stops, calendar applications keep showing the copy they last fetched.

An Admin revokes the feed of a User
:   If a link leaked, an Admin revokes it for the User: "Revoke iCal feed" on the User's page, or `revokeUserIcalFeed`
    (`DELETE /api/v1/users/{user_id}/ical-feed`, permission `users:write`). The link stops at once on every replica,
    the Audit log records `ical_feed.revoked` with the reason `revoked_by_admin`, and the User can issue a new link
    from the profile.

Counting the fetches
:   `muster_ical_feed_requests_total{outcome}` counts the fetches: `served`, `not_found` (a malformed, unknown or
    revoked token, or a disabled or deleted User's) and `failed`. It has no label for the token or the User.

Calendar applications fetch the link without signing in, so treat it as a password: anyone who has it sees the times.
Muster never writes it to its logs, which show the path as `/ical/[redacted].ics`; a reverse proxy or ingress in
front of Muster records the real path in its own access log, so keep that log private or mask `/ical/` there.
Schedules are not imported from iCal: the feed only shows what the Schedules say.

## Gaps and the Internal alert

A **gap** is a time when no Override and no Rotation layer has a Shift with a person who may be Paged, so nobody is
On-call. The people whose Shifts are skipped are Viewers, disabled Users and Users who are no longer in the Team — after
the groups of the identity provider changed, for example: the Rotation layer below takes their time, or a gap does.
`getScheduleOnCall` lists a warning `gap` with its `start` and `end` for each gap of the period, besides the warnings
`disabled`, `viewer` and `not_in_team` about those people, and every Schedule carries `next_gap`, the first gap of the
next 14 days (`schedule.gap_view`), or null. A Rotation layer limited to Friday 19:00–09:00 alone has its gaps from
Saturday 09:00 to Friday 19:00; the night from Friday to Saturday is no gap.

A Schedule may report its gaps as the Internal alert `MusterScheduleGap` (`schedule.gap_alert`):

`by_default`
:   The default: on while the Schedule is its Team's main one, off otherwise. When an Admin makes another Schedule
    main, the alert follows it at the next scan.

`on`
:   On whether or not the Schedule is main.

`off`
:   Never.

`gap_alert_active` says whether it is on now. Every 5 minutes (`schedule.gap_scan_interval`) the Leader looks at the
next 7 days (`schedule.gap_alert_horizon`) of each Schedule whose gap alert is active. A gap now, or one that starts
within those 7 days, raises `MusterScheduleGap` with the Schedule's and its Team's id and name, the Team's slug under
the Organization's Team label, so that the alert belongs to the Team, and the start of the nearest gap in the annotation
`gap_start`. The alert stays raised while the gap lasts and resolves at the next scan once no gap is current or within
the 7 days — an Override or a change of the Rotation layers closed it, or it is past — and when the gap alert is turned
off or the Schedule is deleted. Renaming the Schedule or its Team updates the open alert. The metric
`muster_schedule_gap` is 1 for each Schedule with such a gap and 0 for the others, and the chart rule of the same name
is the backup for the Internal alert; its [runbook](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/operations/runbooks/MusterScheduleGap.md) says how to fill a gap.

In the worked example, with Sergey disabled, nobody is On-call in the evenings, nights and weekend of week 2, nor in the hour after his Monday handover: the upper
Rotation layer has no Shift then and the lower one belongs to Sergey, whose Shifts are skipped. A Responder of the Team
fills the gaps with Overrides, or an Admin replaces Sergey in the Rotation layer; once no gap is left within the next
7 days, the alert resolves at the next scan.

A Schedule that covers only part of the day on purpose, such as a second line in working hours, has gaps every evening:
keep its gap alert off. With `by_default` it already is, as long as it is not its Team's main Schedule.

## On call now

`getOnCallNow` lists every Team by name — or with `teams=mine` the Teams of the caller — with the On-call of its main
Schedule now: the person and the Rotation layer whose Shift it is, until when, and what follows, a person or a gap,
within the next 14 days. A Team without a main Schedule has `no_schedule` true. The Home page shows it as "On call
now", and the Team page shows the same for its Team.

![On call now on the Home page: devops with @ivan, payments with @olga until Tuesday 19:00 marked unreachable in Mattermost, and search with No Schedule](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/schedules/04-on-call-now.png){ .screenshot }

In the picture the On-call of "payments" is Olga, whose account is not linked in Mattermost, so the row says
"unreachable in Mattermost" ([Unreachable On-calls](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/schedules.md#unreachable-on-calls)); "search" has no Schedule.

## Unreachable On-calls

A Mention of the On-call notifies them only where it reaches them. For each segment of the period in which someone is
On-call, `getScheduleOnCall` lists a warning `unreachable` for each [Destination of their
Team](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md) that a Mention of the person cannot reach, whatever its Severity condition, with the
Destination, its `messenger` and the `reason`; `getOnCallNow` lists the same, for the On-call now, in `unreachable`.
The warning changes nothing that Muster sends: it says ahead of time what a Mention would not do.

`no_account_link`
:   The person has no Account link where the Destination posts: a Mattermost, Slack or Bridge Destination needs a link
    through its Connection, a Telegram one a Telegram link. The person links their account in their profile
    ([Link your messenger accounts](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/account-links.md)); the warning goes once the link is made.

`bridge_no_native_mentions`
:   The bridge of a Bridge Destination does not declare `native_mentions` in its
    [info](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/write-a-bridge.md#getinfo), or Muster has not read its info yet, so a Mention is the person's
    name in plain text, which notifies nobody, linked or not. A bridge that renders Mentions declares it; check the
    Connection again once it does.

`webhook_no_thread_reply`
:   An [outgoing webhook in template mode](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/templates.md) has no **Reply in thread** request, so
    the follow-ups that carry Mentions are never sent. Add the request; an outgoing webhook in events mode mentions
    nobody and gets no warning.

`not_in_discussion_group`
:   The last Destination check of a Telegram Destination saw the person outside the discussion group of its channel,
    and Telegram notifies only the members of the group about the replies in a Thread, a reply that mentions them
    included. The person joins the discussion group
    ([Join the discussion group](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/telegram.md#join-the-discussion-group-and-mute-the-channel)), and
    then someone selects **Check** on the Destination page, or saves the Destination.

The check of a Telegram Destination — on save, on **Check** and in `muster doctor`, which prints the people it found
outside the group without recording them — asks Telegram, with `getChatMember`, about every active person of the Teams
that name the Destination who has a Telegram Account link and is not a Viewer (a disabled User or a Viewer is never
On-call), and lists those outside the group in its item **Members of the discussion group**, which always passes. It
makes one request per person, one after the other, limited by the Connection's limiter, not by the Destination's, as
it sends nothing to the chat; on save and on **Check** it stops after 10 seconds
(`destination.telegram.group_people_budget`), and `muster doctor` paces its requests at the Connection's limiter and
stops after as long. A Team with hundreds of linked people may so leave some unasked.

The warning is as old as the last check. A leave reaches Telegram's answer only some time later, about a minute
(F-128), so a check right after someone left the group may still see them in it, and a person who left may show as
unreachable only later; check again later. A person the check could not
tell — Telegram answered with an error, did not answer in time, or was not asked before the check stopped — keeps what
the check before saw: the warning of someone who was outside the group stays until a check sees them in it, and
someone never seen outside it gets no warning.

For an ordinary member, muting the discussion group did not hide a Mention: a Mention in a comment, written as a
`tg://user?id=` link, notified a member who had muted it, on the desktop and on the phone (F-129 of the verified facts in
`design/facts.md` of the repository), so such an On-call may mute the discussion group.

One case the warning cannot see: the creator of the discussion group, who is anonymous there, was not notified of a
Mention in a group they had muted (F-129). The check sees the creator in the group, so they get no warning; an On-call
who created the discussion group, or is anonymous in it, should not mute it. That one a Destination check cannot see.

# Notification rules

A **Notification rule** says how a Page reaches a person. Every User has two: the **normal** rule and the
**important** rule. A rule is a short sequence of actions:

- **Mention**: a Thread reply that mentions the person;
- **Call**: a call through a [Notification gateway](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/notification-gateways.md) to the person's confirmed
  contact there;
- **Wait**: a pause of whole minutes before the next action.

Actions without a wait between them happen at the same moment. Muster sends no direct messages to Page anyone. The
rules are read and changed through the API under `/api/v1/me/notification-rules` and
`/api/v1/users/{user_id}/notification-rules`, listed in the [API reference](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/api.md).

## Normal and important

The Organization sets the default rules, the field `notification_rules` of the Organization
(`organization.notification_rules`):

- **normal**: a Mention;
- **important**: a Mention, a wait of 5 minutes and a second Mention.

In 0.2.0 an Admin changes them only through the API, with `updateOrganization`; the change is recorded in the Audit
log as `organization.updated`, at `/notification_rules/normal` and `/notification_rules/important`. A call in the
Organization's rules needs only an existing Notification gateway.

A rule has at least one Mention or call and at most 10 actions, and a wait lasts from 1 to 60 minutes
(`notification_rule.limits`). The API gives a wait in seconds, `wait_seconds`, a multiple of 60 from 60 to 3600: a wait
of 5 minutes is `{"kind": "wait", "wait_seconds": 300}`. A rule outside the limits is refused with `422`: `out_of_range`
at the rule for more than 10 actions or at its `wait_seconds` for a wait such as 90 or 3660, and `required` at the rule
when it has no Mention or call.

A call names its gateway with `gateway_id`, which may be left out while only one Notification gateway exists. A call
whose gateway is deleted later, or at which the person no longer has a confirmed contact, stays in the rule: a read
shows it with `available` false and `unavailable_reason` `gateway_deleted` or `no_confirmed_contact`.

## The switch

Each person's rules are in one of four states. The switch "Also call through the Notification gateway" chooses among
the first three:

`organization`, "As in the Organization"
:   The default. The person follows the Organization's current rules: when an Admin changes them, the change reaches
    the person at once.

`call`, "Call"
:   The Organization's rules as they were when the person chose "Call", with a call through the gateway at the start
    of the important rule, at the same moment as its first action. With the default rules, the important rule becomes
    a call and a Mention, a wait of 5 minutes and a Mention; the normal rule stays a Mention. When the Organization's
    important rule already calls through that gateway, nothing is added.

`no_call`, "Don't call"
:   The Organization's rules as they were when the person chose "Don't call", without any call. A rule left without a
    Mention gets one in place of its first call and keeps its waits.

A later change of the Organization's rules does not reach a person in "Call" or "Don't call": they keep the rules of
the moment they chose. "Reset to the Organization's rules", `resetMyNotificationRules`, returns a person to "As in the
Organization".

"Call" needs a Notification gateway and a confirmed contact of the person there
([Contact for calls and SMS](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/notification-gateways.md#contact-for-calls-and-sms)). With contacts at
several gateways, `gateway_id` names the one; without it the answer is `422` `required` at `/gateway_id`. While no
gateway exists the answer is `422` `gateway_required`, and for a gateway without the person's confirmed contact
`contact_required`.

A person changes their own rules from their web session only: a Personal access token gets `403`
(`session_required`) on `/me/notification-rules`. An Admin sees and changes any User's rules with
`getUserNotificationRules` and `updateUserNotificationRules`, by session or token, except their own, which they change
from their web session. Every change is recorded in the Audit log as `notification_rules.updated` with a before/after
diff of the state, the gateway and both rules. A change needs `If-Match` with the ETag of the last read, and a stale
one gets `412`.

## Custom rules

The state `custom` takes any normal and important rule within the limits, through the API:

```json
{
  "state": "custom",
  "normal": [{"kind": "mention"}, {"kind": "wait", "wait_seconds": 600}, {"kind": "mention"}],
  "important": [{"kind": "mention"}, {"kind": "call", "gateway_id": "CN0000000000G1"}]
}
```

A call in a person's custom rules needs their confirmed contact at its gateway (`contact_required` at the action), and
a call while no gateway exists is `gateway_required` at the action. Custom rules stay as they are when the
Organization's rules change. "Reset to the Organization's rules" brings back "As in the Organization".

Deleting a User deletes their rules. How the rules run when an Escalation step Pages a person is described with the
Escalation chains.

# Escalation chains

An **Escalation chain** Pages more and more people while an Alert Group stays firing. Its steps Page the On-call of a
Schedule, chosen people or a whole Team, call through a [Notification gateway](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/notification-gateways.md),
wait, send to an outgoing webhook and repeat. Routes and Teams name their chains. Chains are managed through the API under
`/api/v1/escalation-chains`, listed in the [API reference](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/api.md).

Chains run on Alert Groups at the points described under [When chains run](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/escalation-chains.md#when-chains-run): their steps follow one
another with their waits and repeats, and a step is skipped where it cannot be made. A webhook step sends its event
to its outgoing webhook ([Webhook steps](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/escalation-chains.md#webhook-steps)). A step that Pages people or a Team writes a Mention in the
Threads of its chain's own Destinations ([Where chains Page](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/escalation-chains.md#where-chains-page)); the calls through a Notification
gateway follow in a later version. Where no chain runs, the ack timeout of a Route keeps working as before.

## What an Escalation chain is

An Escalation chain has:

- a **name**, unique in the Organization, compared without regard to case;
- a **description**;
- an optional **owning Team**, which organizes the chains — the list filters by Team, `team=<public_id>` or `team=mine`
  — and whose Working hours its steps use;
- its **steps**, in their order, numbered from 1.

Only Admins create, change and delete chains (`escalation-chains:write`); everyone reads them
(`escalation-chains:read`). A change is saved with `If-Match`, like every configuration, and is recorded in the Audit log
as `escalation_chain.created`, `escalation_chain.updated` or `escalation_chain.deleted` with the values before and after.
The metric `muster_escalation_chain_info` has one series per chain, with its name ([Metrics](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/metrics.md)).

A chain's read also lists the Routes and Teams that name it (`referrers`), the Destinations it writes in — those of
those Routes and Teams (`destinations`) — and its `warnings`, what the chain or a step cannot do:

- `pages_nobody`: no step of the chain Pages anyone — it has no step that Pages the On-call, chosen people or a Team,
  or calls, such as an empty chain or one of waits, repeats and webhooks. It concerns the whole chain, so its `step` is
  null, and it comes first. The chain is saved all the same;
- `everyone_unsupported`: a step "Page the whole Team" whose Team mention, or word for everyone in the chat, the
  Connection of one of the chain's Destinations cannot mention, such as a Telegram Destination, with the Destination and
  the reason;
- `gateway_deleted`: a call step whose Notification gateway was deleted, or is no Notification gateway any more;
- `destination_deleted`: a webhook step whose Destination was deleted;
- `team_deleted`: a step "Page the whole Team" whose Team was deleted since it was saved, instead of its
  `everyone_unsupported` warnings.

A chain that a Route or a Team names cannot be deleted: `deleteEscalationChain` answers `409` `chain_in_use`, naming
them in `in_use_by`. A Team that owns a chain cannot be deleted either (`409` `team_in_use`), and a Schedule that a step
names cannot be deleted (`409` `schedule_in_use`).

## Steps

A chain has at most 20 steps (`escalation.limits`). Each step is of one kind and takes the fields of its kind only; a
field of another kind is refused with `422` `unsupported` at it, and a missing one with `required`:

| Kind | What it does | Its fields |
|---|---|---|
| `page_on_call` | Pages the On-call of a Schedule of any Team | `schedule_id`, `importance` |
| `page_people` | Pages chosen people | `user_ids`, `importance` |
| `page_team` | Pages a whole Team by its Team mention, or everyone in the chat | `team_id` or `everyone` |
| `call` | Calls the On-call of a Schedule or chosen people through a Notification gateway | `call_target`, `schedule_id` or `user_ids`, `gateway_id` |
| `wait` | Waits a number of whole minutes | `wait_seconds` |
| `webhook` | Sends to an outgoing webhook Destination | `destination_id` |
| `repeat` | Goes back to an earlier step, a number of times | `repeat_from`, `repeat_times` |

- **Importance.** A Page is `normal` or `important`, `normal` when omitted; it runs the person's
  [Notification rule](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/notification-rules.md) of that name.
- **People.** A step names people who may be Paged: a Viewer or a disabled User that a save adds is refused with `422`
  `not_pageable` at `/steps/{i}/user_ids/{j}`, with the `reason` `viewer` or `disabled`. A person the stored steps
  already name is kept when the chain is saved again, even if they have become a Viewer or been disabled since; a
  running chain will skip them.
- **The whole Team.** Without `team_id` and `everyone`, the step Pages the chain's owning Team; a chain without an
  owning Team must name the Team (`required` at `team_id`). `everyone` is `channel`, `here`, `all` or `everyone`.
- **Calls.** `call_target` is `on_call`, with `schedule_id`, or `people`, with `user_ids`. A call step needs a
  Notification gateway: without one it is refused with `422` `gateway_required`; with one, `gateway_id` may be omitted
  and the step takes that gateway; with several, `gateway_id` is required.
- **Waits.** A wait lasts from 1 to 1,440 minutes, given in seconds: `wait_seconds` is a multiple of 60 from 60 to
  86400, so a wait of 10 minutes is `{"kind": "wait", "wait_seconds": 600}`. Another value is refused with `422`
  `out_of_range` at it.
- **Webhooks.** The Destination of a webhook step is an outgoing webhook in the mode `events` or `both`; another one is
  refused with `422` `webhook_events_required`.
- **Repeats.** `repeat_from` is the number of an earlier step, and `repeat_times` from 1 to 10.
- **Limits.** Every step but `wait` and `repeat` takes `working_hours_only` — the step is made only in the Working
  hours of the chain's owning Team, else the Organization's — and a `severity_condition`, `any` by default, `critical`
  or `below_critical`.

A gateway or a webhook Destination that a stored step names may be deleted; the chain warns about it and can still be
saved.

### Webhook steps

A step "Send to an outgoing webhook" (`webhook`) sends the event `escalation_webhook` to the outgoing webhook
Destination it names when the step is made. The event carries the Alert Group, as every event does, with the chain and
the step. The Destination receives it whether or not the Alert Group is delivered there, so an outgoing webhook on
none of the Alert Group's Routes can be called at a chosen step of a chain. Such a Destination receives no other event
of the Alert Group, and the events-mode Destinations of the Alert Group's Routes do not receive `escalation_webhook`.
A Destination that is also one of the Alert Group's receives it once, in order among its other events. The event is
signed, ordered and delivered at least once like every event
([escalation_webhook](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/events.md#escalation_webhook)).

The Timeline records the Quiet `escalation_webhook`, of the kind `escalation`, with the chain, the step and the
Destination; nothing appears in a messenger. Like every step that sends, a webhook step can be limited to Working hours
and to a Severity condition. A running chain skips a webhook step, recording `escalation_step_skipped`, when its
Destination:

- was deleted since the step was saved: `destination_deleted`;
- is no longer an outgoing webhook in the mode `events` or `both`, such as one switched to the template mode:
  `webhook_events_required`.

## Naming chains on Routes and Teams

Routes and Teams name their chains in `chain_entries`, as they name their Destinations: each entry is a chain with a
Severity condition — `any`, the default, `critical` or `below_critical` — checked against the Severity level of the
Alert Group. An entry list replaces the stored one as a whole, and an update that omits it keeps it. Reads give the
entries and the chains by name, `chains`, and the Audit log of the Route or the Team records the change.

```json
{"chain_entries": [{"chain_id": "ECK7M3QX9P2RTA", "severity_condition": "critical"}]}
```

Naming a chain on a Route needs `routes:write`, and on a Team `teams:write`. A chain Pages only in the Threads of its
own Destinations: a chain of a Route in those of the Route's Destinations that have the Alert Group's Root message; a
chain of a Team in those of the Team's Destinations that have it, and, where none has, in those of the Route's
Destinations. So a step that Pages everyone in the chat never rings another team's chat.

## When chains run

The chains that run on an Alert Group are those its Route names and — where the Route has "Also to the Teams from the
label" (`deliver_to_teams`) and "Run the Teams' Escalation chains" (`run_team_chains`) on — those that each of its Teams
names, each whose Severity condition its Severity level meets. Several chains may run on one Alert Group at the same
time. A chain that the Route and a Team both name, or several Teams, runs once, with all of them as its sources. With
"Run the Teams' Escalation chains" off, the Teams' Destinations still receive the Alert Group, but their chains do not
start.

A chain runs only while the Alert Group is firing, and only Alerts and Commands start chains:

| Point | What happens to the chains |
|---|---|
| The Alert Group's first Publication; for one that a Storm summary stood for, its own Publication; for an outgoing webhook in events mode, its delivered `created` event | The chains that apply start at their first step |
| The Alert Group is created while no Destination reaches it, or only Broken ones | The chains that apply start at once |
| It becomes firing again: a Reopen into firing, Unacknowledge, Unsnooze, the end of a Snooze, Unresolve, a rise to critical that ends an acknowledgement or a Snooze, an auto-unacknowledge, its Owner disabled or deleted | The chains that apply start over at their first step |
| Its Severity level rises by its Alerts while it is firing and nobody owns it | The chains whose condition it now meets start; running chains go on |
| A Team joins it by a new Alert while it is firing and nobody owns it | That Team's chains start, where the Route runs the chains of Teams |
| A change of configuration: saving a Team, turning on "Also to the Teams from the label" or "Run the Teams' Escalation chains", naming a chain on a Route or a Team or changing its condition, changing the Minimum severity | No chain starts, and the running chains go on |
| Acknowledge, Snooze, Resolve | Every chain stops |

A chain whose condition the Alert Group no longer meets after its Severity level falls goes on. A chain that a change of
configuration would apply to starts at the next point that starts or starts over the chains, such as an Unacknowledge.
A held Alert Group that a raised Minimum severity releases is published Quietly and starts its ack timeout, but no
chain: that release is a change of configuration.

Each run keeps the steps its chain had when it started, until it stops or starts over: a change of the chain applies to
the runs that start after it. A step that Pages or sends, limited to Working hours, is skipped outside the Working hours
of the chain's owning Team, else the Organization's; a step whose Severity condition the Alert Group does not meet, a
step that Pages the On-call of a Schedule with a gap now, and each person a step names who has become a Viewer or been
disabled since the step was saved are skipped too. Each skip records the Quiet lifecycle event
`escalation_step_skipped` in the Timeline, of the kind `escalation`, with the chain, the step and the reason —
`outside_working_hours`, `severity`, `schedule_gap`, `viewer` or `disabled`, for a webhook step
`destination_deleted` or `webhook_events_required` ([Webhook steps](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/escalation-chains.md#webhook-steps)), and for a step "Page the whole
Team" that none of its chain's Destinations can mention `no_mention_possible` ([Where chains Page](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/escalation-chains.md#where-chains-page))
— and the chain goes on with its
next step at once. A wait delays the next step by its length on the clock of Muster, and "Repeat from step 3, up to 2 times" makes
steps 3 to the repeat three times in all, then goes on. Outgoing webhooks in the mode events receive
`escalation_step_skipped` with the chain, the step and the reason ([Events](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/events.md)).

Steps are timer rows that any replica claims, and each step is a change of its Alert Group made by Muster, so a replica
that stops never stops a chain. `getAlertGroup` gives the runs of the chains since they last started in
`escalation_chains`: each with its chain, its sources, its state — `running`, `ran_out` once it passed its last step,
`stopped` by Acknowledge, Snooze or Resolve —, the step it is at and `next_step_at`, when its next step is due.
`listEscalationChainRuns` (`GET /api/v1/escalation-chains/{escalation_chain_id}/runs`) lists the recent runs of a chain,
newest first: each with its Alert Group, the steps it made and skipped with their reasons, and what stopped it — the
Command and the person who ran it — or that it ran out. An Alert Group whose chains started over has a run per start.

## Chains and the ack timeout

When the first chain of an Alert Group starts, its running ack timeout stops and sends no more notices: the steps take
their place. When the Alert Group becomes firing again, its ack timeout starts over only if none of its chains starts.
An Alert Group on which no chain applies keeps the ack timeout of its Route, as in L1
([Statuses and commands](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/statuses-and-commands.md#ack-timeouts-reminders-and-auto-unacknowledge)).

## Where chains Page

A chain Pages only in the Threads of its own Destinations, so that a step that Pages everyone in the chat never rings
another team's chat:

- a chain of the Route, in those of the Route's Destinations that have the Alert Group's Root message;
- a chain of a Team, in those of the Team's Destinations that have it, and, where none of them has, in those of the
  Route's Destinations;
- a chain that the Route and a Team both name, which runs once, in the Threads of both.

A Destination whose first Publication of the Alert Group is still on its way counts as one with the Root message: the
chains start with the first Publication in one Destination, and the Page waits there for the Root message to reply
under. One that a Storm holds does not.

Which Destinations a Route or a Team reaches is the rule of [Routing to Teams](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/routing-to-teams.md): the Severity
condition of each entry against the Alert Group's Severity level. A Destination is the Route's or a Team's when that
Route's or Team's own entry reached it, at a level the Alert Group had, and still names it. Destinations only grow, so
a Destination stays the Route's or the Team's after the level falls: when the critical Alerts resolve and the level
falls to warning, the chain of a Team still Pages in the Team's critical channel, which has the Root message, and does
not fall back to the Route's. A channel that several name belongs only to those whose entry reached it: when Team
devops names a shared channel for any level and Team payments names it for critical only, a warning Alert Group that
was never critical has its Root message there by devops' entry, so the chain of payments does not Page there, and its
`@here` never rings devops' channel. A Page is a Thread reply that delivery sends like any
other: Loud, due at once without the Thread batching window, never combined with another reply. It says which step
and which chain it comes from:

```text
Step 1: @ivan, you are needed
Chain of the Route Infrastructure · On-call of devops Main · normal
```

- **The people.** A step "Page the On-call of a Schedule" Pages the On-call of that moment, and a step "Page people"
  each person it names; each person by their [Notification rule](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/notification-rules.md) of the step's importance. The
  first Mentions of the rules that come due together are one reply per Destination naming them all. An important
  Page says "important, again in 5 min" when the person's rule Mentions them again — for a Page naming several people,
  only when every one of their rules does after the same time — and that later Mention repeats the first line with
  "· important, again". The rule stops when the Alert Group is acknowledged, snoozed or resolved, or
  when its chain starts over, and a Mention that comes due after that is not made.
- **A whole Team or everyone in the chat.** A step "Page the whole Team" writes the Team's Team mention for the
  Destination's Connection — the chain's owning Team when the step names none, the owning Team the chain had when its
  run started — or the word for everyone in the chat, `@here` for example. Where a Destination's Connection can
  mention neither, the step makes no reply there; where it can reply in none of the chain's Destinations, as when the
  only one is a Telegram channel, it records `escalation_step_skipped` with `no_mention_possible`.
- **The Timeline.** Each Page records the Loud lifecycle event `paged`, of the kind `escalation`, with its people,
  its chain and its step; outgoing webhooks in events mode receive it as every event
  ([Events](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/outgoing-webhooks/events.md)).

In this version a Page by Mention replies in Mattermost and Slack Destinations. Telegram and Bridge Destinations and
outgoing webhooks take Pages in a later version: until then a chain makes no Thread reply there.

## How a Page reaches a person

A Page names each person as a Mention only where they have an Account link in the Identity space of that Destination's
Connection ([Account links](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/account-links.md)); a person without one is named in the line "Page not
delivered: @petr has no linked Mattermost account" instead, which mentions nobody.

| Where | A Page is | It reaches the person when |
|---|---|---|
| Mattermost | a Thread reply with `@username` | they have an Account link for the Connection |
| Slack | a Thread reply with `<@U…>` | they have an Account link for the Connection |
| Telegram, Bridge, outgoing webhooks | from a later version | — |
| Notification gateway | a call to the confirmed contact, from a later version | the gateway reaches them, whatever the messengers do |

!!! warning "At night, only a Notification gateway is reliable"
    Whether a Mention in a Thread pushes a notification to a phone is still to be verified for Mattermost and Slack.
    Until it is, rely on a call through a Notification gateway for Pages at night.

## Undelivered Pages

A Page that reaches its person nowhere records the Quiet `page_not_delivered` with the person and the reason:

- `no_root_message`: none of the chain's Destinations has the Alert Group's Root message — such as a Route with a
  chain and no Destination, whose chains start when the Alert Group is created;
- `no_account_link`: the person has no Account link in the Identity space of any of the chain's Destinations;
- `not_delivered`: every Thread reply that would mention them ended Not delivered, or was withheld while its
  Destination was [Broken](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/broken-destinations.md); a reply withheld while Broken is not sent after the Destination
  recovers.

A Page counts in `muster_pages_total{chain,kind,outcome}` once it ended: `delivered` once a delivered reply mentioned
one of its people — for a Team or everyone in the chat, once one of its replies was delivered — and `not_delivered`
otherwise; a Page whose chain was deleted before it ended is not counted ([Metrics](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/reference/metrics.md)).

## Unclaimed

A chain has run out when it has passed its last step. An Alert Group with chains is Unclaimed once every chain started
on it has run out without an acknowledgement: it records `unclaimed`, and the Root message and the UI show "Nobody has
taken this", as after the last ack timeout notice. A chain that starts later on an Unclaimed Alert Group — for a Team
that joins, or a rise — makes it no longer Unclaimed while that chain runs, and it is Unclaimed again, recording
`unclaimed` again, once that chain has run out too. Acknowledge, Snooze and Resolve end Unclaimed.

# Whom a message mentions

Mention entries say who is mentioned. Each Destination has one list of them, each entry one target and the Loud events
it applies to, so that one Destination can mention `@here` for a new Alert Group and the On-call of the Alert Group's
Team on every Loud event. A Quiet message mentions nobody, and an edit never adds a Mention.

## Mention entries

The targets of an entry (`mention_entries[].target.kind`):

`everyone`
:   Everyone in the chat, with the word of the messenger: `channel`, `here`, `all` or `everyone`.

`user`
:   A person, by User. The message mentions them through their linked account in the Destination's messenger; a
    person without a linked account there is not mentioned.

`group`
:   A messenger group by its name, such as a Mattermost group; in Slack a user group with its id (`group_id`).

`team`
:   A [Team](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md), by its [Team mention](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#team-mentions) for the Destination's Connection.

`teams_from_label`
:   Each Team of the Alert Group, by its Team mention.

`on_call_of_team`
:   The On-call of a Team: the person whom the Team's main [Schedule](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/schedules.md) has on Shift at the moment of the
    event.

`on_call_of_teams_from_label`
:   The On-call of each Team of the Alert Group.

The Loud events an entry applies to (`events`), all of them for a new entry:

`new_alert_group`
:   A new Alert Group; it also applies when an Alert Group joins the Destination Loudly (`destination_joined`).

`new_alerts`, `reopen`, `snooze_ended`, `rise_to_urgent`
:   New Alerts, a Reopen, the end of a Snooze and a rise to critical.

`ack_timeout`
:   The ack timeout notice.

`storm_started`
:   The first publication of a [Storm](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md#start-of-a-storm) summary.

A Destination without entries mentions nobody. Each person and group is mentioned once in a message, however many
entries name them. The rules for the Owner stay as they were: Reminders and auto-unacknowledge always mention the
Owner, a Takeover the previous Owner, and a Reopen into acknowledged only the Owner.

The API saves the entries in order with `If-Match`, and the Audit log shows the change in the diff of
`destination.updated`. A read gives each entry with `available`, whether the Connection can mention it now, and
`resolves_to`, what it mentions now: for the targets from the label, one item per Team.

```json
"mention_entries": [
  {"target": {"kind": "on_call_of_teams_from_label"}},
  {"target": {"kind": "everyone", "everyone": "here"}, "events": ["new_alert_group", "storm_started"]}
]
```

## An example: infra-critical

The operations Destination `infra-critical` should reach the On-call of the Team that owns the Alert Group on every
Loud event, and everyone on duty in the channel only when it matters. Two entries do that:

| Entry | Loud events |
|---|---|
| On-call of the Team from the label | all seven |
| `@here` | New Alert Group, Rise to critical, Start of a Storm |

So a new Alert Group of the Team payments mentions its On-call and `@here`; New Alerts, a Reopen, the end of a Snooze
and the ack timeout mention only the On-call; a rise to critical and the start of a Storm mention both again. A Quiet
message mentions nobody. A new entry is ticked for every Loud event, so untick the events `@here` does not take.

Open **Destinations**, select the Destination and find the section **Mentions**. Each row of the grid is one entry,
with its Loud events beside it as seven boxes, in this order: New Alert Group, New Alerts, Reopen, Ack timeout, End of
Snooze, Rise to critical and Start of a Storm. **Add** puts a new entry at the end. Under each target the row reads what it mentions now. In the picture, `@here` is ticked for a new
Alert Group, a rise to critical and the start of a Storm only.

![The Mentions of a Mattermost Destination: the entry "On-call of the Team from the label" ticked for all seven Loud events and the entry @here ticked for three of them, a warning above them about a person without a linked account, and the table of what each messenger supports under them](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/mentions/01-mentions-grid.png){ .screenshot }

An entry that names a person without a linked account in the Destination's messenger is warned about above the grid
([Undelivered Mentions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md#undelivered-mentions)).

## What each messenger supports

A Destination takes only the targets its Connection can mention. Any other target is refused with `422` and the code
`mention_unsupported` at `/mention_entries/{i}/target`:

| Destination type | Everyone in the chat | Groups and Teams | People and the On-call |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mattermost | `channel`, `here`, `all` | Mattermost groups | linked people |
| Slack | `channel`, `here` | user groups, with the scope `usergroups:read` | linked people |
| Telegram | none | none | linked people |
| Bridge | the words the bridge declares | when the bridge declares group mentions | linked people, by the plain name without `native_mentions` |
| Outgoing webhook | every target, as data | every target, as data | every target, as data |

Mattermost
:   `@channel`, `@here` and `@all` mention in a Root message's attachment as in a post, and `@payments_team` mentions
    the members of a custom group in a Root message and in a Thread reply; a custom group can always be mentioned,
    since its setting to allow references cannot be turned off. The bot reads the groups with the role Member and no
    added permission. Checked on Mattermost 11.2.2 with the Entry licence (F-122, F-123, F-124); groups synced from
    LDAP were not checked (F-121). See [Connect Mattermost](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/mattermost.md#who-gets-notified).

Slack
:   Slack offers only `<!channel>` and `<!here>`, and no `everyone`: a bot's `<!everyone>` mentions only in the
    workspace's general channel, where `<!channel>` reaches the same people, and nothing tells the two cases apart
    (F-127). A user group or a Team mention, `<!subteam^…>`, mentions the group's members in a Root message and in a
    Thread reply (F-126). A Slack Connection takes user groups and Teams once its check found the optional scope
    `usergroups:read`, which reaches the bot only after the app is reinstalled; the reinstall keeps the bot token, so
    check the Connection again, do not edit it (F-125). A workspace may let only its Admins and Owners create user
    groups. See [Connect Slack](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/slack.md#add-usergroupsread-to-an-installed-app).

Telegram
:   Telegram mentions only people, by a link to their account, and only in the discussion group. See
    [Telegram](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md#telegram).

Bridge
:   A bridge declares in its `info` the mentions of everyone it has (`everyone_mentions`) and whether it has group
    mentions (`group_mentions`); a bridge that has native mentions renders the markers of people as the messenger's
    mentions ([Write a bridge](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/write-a-bridge.md)).

Saving an entry whose kind the bridge does not declare is refused. At delivery Muster sends a bridge only the groups and
mentions of everyone that its copy of `info` declares, and leaves out the others. A bridge never refuses a message for
the kind of an entry: one that receives a `group` or an `everyone` entry it no longer declares shows the marker's link
text, which notifies nobody. A person is named by their plain name when the bridge has no native mentions
([Undelivered Mentions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/mentions.md#undelivered-mentions)). Muster reads `info` again when its copy is older than
`bridge.info_max_age`.

An entry stays when its Connection later loses an ability, such as a Slack app reinstalled without the scope: it reads
`available: false` with the reason in `unavailable_reason`, and mentions nobody. An entry whose User or Team was deleted
stays too, shows them as deleted and mentions nobody.

A bridge receives a group and a mention of everyone as entries of `mentions` of their own kind
([What Muster posts](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/bridge.md#what-muster-posts)).

An outgoing webhook receives the targets as data in `mentions` and `.Mentions`: a Team as `{"type": "team", "slug":
…, "name": …}`, one per Team of the Alert Group for "Team from the label", and the On-call as a `user`.

## Teams and the On-call

"Team payments" mentions the Team mention of payments for the Destination's Connection, such as `@payments_team` in
Mattermost. When the Team has no Team mention for that Connection, the entry mentions nobody: Muster never mentions the
On-call instead. To reach the On-call, add "On-call of a Team" or "On-call of the Team from the label".

The On-call is taken from the Team's main Schedule at the moment of the event, also when the message is sent later.
A Team without a main Schedule, or one with a gap at that moment, gives nobody. A Team mention that is set later, or an
On-call who links an account later, is mentioned from the next Loud event on.

The Team mention is set on the Team page, in the section **How to mention the Team**, one field for each Connection
that may have a Destination. A Connection that cannot mention a group says why beside its field. See
[Team mentions](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/concepts/teams.md#team-mentions).

![The section How to mention the Team of the Team payments: the Mattermost Connection oncall-mm with the group payments_team, and the bridge, Slack and Telegram Connections that cannot mention a group, each with its reason](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/mentions/03-team-mentions.png){ .screenshot }

When a Destination becomes a Destination of a Team, Muster adds the entry "Team {Team}" for every Loud event, unless
the Destination already has an entry naming that Team or has 100 entries, the most a Destination has. A Telegram
Destination gets none, since Telegram cannot mention Teams. An Admin may remove the entry; naming the Destination
again later adds it again only when it has none.

## Telegram

A Telegram Destination mentions people only, each by a link to their Telegram account. When you select **Add** in its
**Mentions** section, everyone in the chat, groups and Teams are greyed with the reason "Telegram cannot mention
everyone or groups", and it offers a person and the two On-call entries.

![The list that Add opens on a Telegram Destination: Person, On-call of a Team and On-call of the Team from the label can be added, and everyone in the chat, groups and Teams are greyed with the reason "Telegram cannot mention everyone or groups"](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/assets/screenshots/concepts/mentions/02-telegram-mentions.png){ .screenshot }

A Mention in a Thread reaches only the members of the channel's discussion group:

- A subscriber of the channel who is not in the discussion group is not mentioned (F-014). The Destination check names
  the people of its Teams who are outside the group, in its item **Members of the discussion group**. A person who left
  the group shows as outside only some time later, about a minute (F-128).
- Muting the discussion group does not hide a Mention from an ordinary member: a Mention in a comment rang a member who
  had muted the group, on the desktop and on the phone (F-129).
- The creator of the discussion group, who is anonymous in it, was not rung by a Mention in a group they had muted
  (F-129). So a person who created the discussion group, or is anonymous in it, should not mute it. The Destination
  check sees the creator as a member, so it gives no warning for this.

[Connect Telegram](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/messengers/telegram.md#join-the-discussion-group-and-mute-the-channel) tells the people on call to
join the group.

## Undelivered Mentions

A person whom an entry names is sometimes not mentioned as their messenger mentions people:

- They have no linked account in the Destination's messenger. The message leaves them out. Only the Owner of a Reminder,
  an auto-unacknowledge or a Takeover, the footer and the Assignees name a person without a link, by their name as
  text.
- The Destination is a bridge whose `info` does not declare `native_mentions`, or Muster has not read its `info` yet.
  The message names them by their plain name, which notifies nobody.

Through a bridge the line of Mentions keeps, in their order, the whole Mentions that fit half of the bridge's body
limit, and leaves out the rest. A Mention left out for its length is still recorded as delivered today, so it appears
in no Timeline entry and in no warning.

Nothing is written in the Thread for them: Muster never posts a message about a Mention. Instead, once the Loud
message is delivered, the Alert Group's Timeline records the delivery event "Mention not delivered in
{Destination}" (`mention_not_delivered`). Each person, entry and message gets one, with the entry, the Loud event and
the reason: `no_account_link` or `bridge_cannot_mention_people`. A message that ends Not delivered records none, nor
does a Quiet message or an edit, since neither mentions anyone. The first publication of a Storm summary belongs to no
Alert Group, so its undelivered Mentions appear in no Timeline. They still warn on the Destination.

The Destination page warns about each person and entry whose latest "Mention not delivered" is not followed by a
delivered Mention of that person through that entry. The API reads the warnings in `mention_warnings` of every
Destination, each with the person, the entry, the messenger, the reason and since when. For example: "Mention not
delivered: @petr, On-call of payments, has no linked Mattermost account."

The warning clears by itself, because Muster computes it from the delivery events and never stores it:

- When the person links an account (or the bridge declares `native_mentions`), the next Loud message that mentions
  them through that entry clears it.
- When the events behind it are older than `retention.alert_details`, which removes them, the warning goes too.

A warning whose entry was removed from the Destination since goes at once. So does the warning of a deleted User.

## Start of a Storm

The first publication of a Storm summary mentions the Destination's entries for `storm_started`. "Team from the label"
and "On-call of the Team from the label" take the Teams of the Alert Groups the Storm counts. The summary's later edits
mention nobody, and the summary starts no Escalation chain.

## Upgrading from 0.1

Version 0.1 kept one Mention setting per kind of Loud event: nobody, everyone in the chat, chosen Users or messenger
groups. The 0.2.0 migration turns them into entries with the same effect: each target chosen for a kind becomes one
entry for the kinds it was chosen for. An entry for `new_alert_group` also applies to `storm_started`, since a 0.1
Storm summary mentioned the targets of a new Alert Group. A Destination that mentioned nobody has no entries.

For example, "`@channel` for new Alert Groups, Alice for the ack timeout" becomes two entries: `channel` for
`new_alert_group` and `storm_started`, and Alice for `ack_timeout`.

Until 0.3.0 the API still returns `mentions`, the 0.1 form, computed from the entries that 0.1 could express on the
Destination's type, and still takes it in a request without `mention_entries`. Such a request replaces only those
entries and keeps the others, and a request that sends back the `mentions` it read changes nothing, so that 0.1 clients
keep working. The 0.1 form read back has the same Users and groups for each kind, but lists them in the order of the
first kind that lists each, since the entries are one list. 0.3.0 removes the field. The
[upgrade notes](https://muster-io.github.io/muster/latest/install/versions.md#upgrade-notes) describe the migration.
