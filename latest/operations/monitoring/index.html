Monitoring¶

Muster exports Prometheus metrics, ships alert rules and a Grafana dashboard in its Helm chart, and raises Internal alerts about its own problems. Use all three: the metrics and the rules tell you from outside, and the Internal alerts reach you through Muster itself.

Metrics¶

Muster serves /metrics on the internal listener, port 8082 by default (MUSTER_LISTEN_INTERNAL), next to /health/live and /health/ready. It never pushes metrics. Do not expose that listener outside the cluster.

With the Prometheus Operator, set serviceMonitor.enabled: true. The chart then creates a ServiceMonitor for the internal Service, with serviceMonitor.interval (default 30 seconds) and serviceMonitor.scrapeTimeout (default 10 seconds), and extra labels from serviceMonitor.labels for the selector of your Prometheus. Without the operator, scrape the internal Service yourself, or the pods on port 8082.

Every metric is in the metrics reference. Entities appear by their immutable id; their names are in the *_info metrics, which the dashboard joins. No metric carries an alert label, a user or an Alert Group number.

Alert rules¶

The chart renders one group of rules, named muster, when you enable one of:

alerting.prometheusRule.enabled
renders: a PrometheusRule · needs: the Prometheus Operator CRDs
alerting.vmRule.enabled
renders: a VMRule · needs: the VictoriaMetrics Operator CRDs

Both take extra labels (alerting.prometheusRule.labels, alerting.vmRule.labels) for the rule selector of your operator. Each rule has a description and a runbook_url that points to its runbook, built from alerting.runbookBaseURL and the version of Muster the chart deploys.

What you can tune:

  • alerting.jobSelector is the label matcher that selects Muster's series in every rule. By default it is job="muster", the job the ServiceMonitor gives. Change it when your scrape job has another name.
  • alerting.rules.<Name>.enabled switches one rule off, and .for sets how long its condition holds before it fires.
  • Some rules have thresholds: window (the range of the rate or increase), seconds (MusterDeliverySlow: p95 delivery latency; MusterClockSkew: the skew), size (MusterDeliveryQueueGrowing), backlog and delaySeconds (MusterIngestBacklog), unauthorized (MusterIngestRejected) and failures (MusterLoginFailures).

The defaults are in the chart values reference, and the expression of each rule is on its runbook page. The rules are covered by unit tests in CI.

The five rules with severity critical are MusterDown, MusterNoLeader, MusterDeliveryFailing, MusterDestinationBroken and MusterHeartbeatLost. The others are warning.

Send the critical rules around Muster¶

Do not route the critical rules through Muster

A rule such as MusterDown or MusterDeliveryFailing fires when Muster cannot do its job. If Alertmanager sends it to Muster, the alert goes exactly where it cannot be delivered. Use an Alertmanager route that bypasses Muster for them, to a receiver you trust to work when Muster does not.

The chart rules carry the labels alertname (starting with Muster) and severity: critical or warning, plus the labels Prometheus adds from the series. Match on both, and put the bypass route first, above the route that sends everything to Muster, with continue false (the default) so that the alert stops there. Here it is added to the configuration of Connect Alertmanager, whose receivers muster-prod-eu, oncall and default stay as they are:

route:
  receiver: default
  routes:
    - receiver: platform-direct          # not Muster
      matchers: ['alertname=~"Muster.+"', 'severity="critical"']
    - receiver: muster-prod-eu
      continue: true
      repeat_interval: 10m
    - receiver: oncall
      matchers: ['severity="critical"']
    - receiver: default

receivers:
  # ... muster-prod-eu, oncall and default
  - name: platform-direct
    webhook_configs:
      - url: https://chat.example.org/hooks/<id>          # a chat or a pager of your own
        send_resolved: true

The matchers use alertname=~"Muster.+" for the rules of this chart; severity="critical" limits the bypass to the ones that need it, so the warnings still reach Muster, and every other alert still reaches Muster and your own receivers. Point the receiver platform-direct at a path that does not depend on Muster.

The Internal alerts of Muster itself do not go through Alertmanager, so this route does not see them.

Dashboard¶

The chart ships one dashboard, "Muster", with the fixed uid muster, a data source variable, and the variables namespace/job, integration, route and destination. It has four rows: Overview, Ingestion, Delivery and Platform. You enable it in either or both ways, each behind its own flag:

dashboard.configMap.enabled

renders: a ConfigMap with the dashboard JSON

For the Grafana sidecar; dashboard.configMap.labels defaults to grafana_dashboard: "1".

dashboard.grafanaDashboard.enabled

renders: a GrafanaDashboard of the Grafana Operator

References that ConfigMap, so it needs dashboard.configMap.enabled too; instanceSelector, folder and allowCrossNamespaceImport are values.

Internal alerts¶

Muster raises its own problems as Internal alerts: MusterSnapshotTruncated, MusterHeartbeatLost, MusterDestinationBroken, MusterTemplateError and MusterOIDCSecretExpiring. They are Alerts of the built-in "Muster" Integration, which has no tokens and cannot be changed. They go through Routes like any Alert, and they reach nobody until a Route sends them to a Destination. The Internal alerts reference lists them with their labels.

Give them a Route:

  1. Open Routes. At the top of the list, Muster suggests Send Muster's own alerts to a Destination when no Route other than the Default route matches them.
  2. Choose a Destination, and press Create the route. The Route matches alertname=~"Muster.*".

Use a Destination that does not depend on what the alert reports: not the one that is Broken. A second messenger, or an outgoing webhook to a chat of your own, serves well. When a Route for MusterHeartbeatLost exists, the Internal alerts Route goes directly below it. Every Internal alert has a runbook, and MUSTER_RUNBOOK_BASE_URL (set by the chart from alerting.runbookBaseURL) is the base of its link.

Two Internal alerts have a chart rule with the same name, MusterHeartbeatLost and MusterDestinationBroken. The rule is the backup for when Muster cannot deliver; the Internal alert carries the labels of what it is about: the Integration, with its Static labels, for MusterHeartbeatLost, and the Destination for MusterDestinationBroken.

Watching that Muster is alive¶

Metrics and rules need Prometheus. To notice that Muster, its cluster or its network is down altogether, configure the outgoing heartbeat: an external service alerts you when the calls stop.

The System status page (Organization → System status, Admins only) shows the same from the inside: the Leader and every live replica with the key ids it holds, recovery after downtime, delivery queues, Broken Destinations, Integrations that need attention, template errors, Storms and the outgoing heartbeat.
