Teams¶

A Team says who owns what: Routes and Destinations may name the Team that owns them, so that each team finds its own configuration with a filter. Alerts and Alert Groups belong to Teams by their labels, so that each team finds its Alert Groups. Teams are managed through the API under /api/v1/teams.

What a Team is¶

A Team has:

  • a name, unique in the Organization, compared without regard to case;
  • a slug, such as payments. A slug is 1 to 63 characters — lowercase letters, digits, -, _ and . — and starts with a letter or a digit (team.slug_format), so Payments and -x are refused. It is unique in the Organization and compared exactly;
  • a description;
  • its Team matchers, which pick the Alerts that belong to it beside its slug;
  • the people in it, which may be nobody;
  • its Working hours: its own, else the Organization's.
What a Team owns
A Route or a Destination names its owning Team in the field owning_team_id; reads carry it as owning_team, with the Team's id, name and slug. listRoutes and listDestinations take the parameter team: a Team's id, or mine for the Teams you are in ("My Teams"). The owner only organizes: owning a Destination does not make it deliver to the Team, and a Route's owning Team adds no delivery.
What a Team does not grant
A Team grants no Permission and limits none: Roles stay the same for the whole Organization. A Responder in a Team cannot change the Team, and a Viewer in a Team is refused Acknowledge as any Viewer is. Only an Admin creates, edits and deletes Teams and adds people to them; every Role reads them.
Deleting a Team
The people leave it, its Working hours and Team matchers are removed, and the Routes and Destinations it owned have no owner from then on, each with a new version. Its slug and name are free at once for a new Team, and its slug no longer gives Alerts the Team. The Team itself is kept, marked deleted, for what still names it: the Alert Groups that had it among their Teams list it by its name, with deleted true.

Every change of a Team and of the people in it is recorded in the Audit log, and the metric muster_team_info has one series per Team that is not deleted, with its name (Metrics). The operations are listed in the API reference.

Which Alerts belong to a Team¶

An Alert belongs to a Team in two ways, and may belong to several Teams.

By the Team label
The Team label is an Organization setting, team by default (organization.team_label, team_label of the Organization's settings). An Alert whose value of the Team label, with the Static labels of its Integration, equals a Team's slug exactly belongs to that Team: with the Team "payments", team="payments" belongs to it and team="Payments" does not. The Team label cannot be a label that an Internal alert sets itself, such as alertname, severity, integration, schedule or team_id: such a label is refused with 422 reserved_label.
By Team matchers
A Team's matchers use the syntax of Route Matchers on any label — =, !=, =~ and !~, regular expressions anchored at both ends, a missing label counting as empty — and each is enough on its own: an Alert that any one of them picks belongs to the Team. The Team with the matchers namespace=~"checkout|billing" and pod=~"checkout-.*" gets an Alert with namespace="billing" and one with pod="checkout-api-1", and not one with namespace="search". Matchers are saved with the Team, with If-Match, and replaced as a whole; an update that omits them keeps them. An expression that is not RE2 is refused with 422 invalid_regex at its pointer. A matcher with only a negative operator, such as env!="prod", picks every Alert that lacks the label, Internal alerts included, because a missing label counts as empty.

Muster decides the Teams of an Alert when it starts firing — its first firing, and each firing after it resolved — and records them on the Alert with the rule that matched: label, or matcher with the matcher's text. The Alerts view of an Integration shows them as teams: the Teams of the Alert's latest firing, which replace those of an earlier one. A Continuation is not a new firing and decides nothing; a Team created or changed later reaches the Alerts that fire from then on.

The Teams of an Alert Group are those of every Alert that ever joined it. They only grow: a Team stays among them after its Alerts resolve. Each Team that joins records the Quiet lifecycle event team_joined in the Timeline, naming the Team and the rule that brought it; getAlertGroup and listAlertGroups carry the Teams as teams, and outgoing webhooks in events mode carry them in alert_group.teams (Events mode). An event carries the state of the Alert Group, not a change: each team_joined lists every Team the Alert Group has at that moment, the new one included. The parameter team of listAlertGroups and getAlertGroupCounts takes a Team's id, or mine for the Alert Groups of the Teams you are in ("My Teams"). The Teams of an Alert Group exist whatever its Route says: they do not change where it is delivered.

A worked example
A rule generator sets category, team and system on every alert rule. With the Team label team, each Team's slug is the value its rules carry — payments, checkout — and the Alerts reach their Teams without any matcher. A Team without people that only names Alerts is fine: it groups and filters them.
Test clusters with one shared namespace
When every team deploys into the same namespace of a test cluster, namespace does not tell them apart. Match another label instead: the Pod name (pod=~"checkout-.*"), an app label the workloads carry (app_kubernetes_io_part_of="checkout"), or put the Team label itself on the workloads, so that their Alerts carry team="checkout".

Values without a Team¶

A value of the Team label that names no Team — a typo, a team without a Team yet, or a case that differs, such as Payments — adds nothing to the Alert Group: it is delivered to its Route's Destinations as before. Its Timeline records the Quiet lifecycle event unknown_team_value with the value, once per value and Alert Group, and outgoing webhooks in events mode receive it. Create a Team with that value as its slug, or add a Team matcher that picks those Alerts, and the Alerts that start firing from then on belong to it. A value that is not a valid slug, such as Payments, reaches a Team only through a Team matcher.

With the default Team label team and no Teams defined yet, every Alert that carries a team label records unknown_team_value in its Alert Group's Timeline and sends it to outgoing webhooks in events mode. Create the Teams, or change the Team label to a label your Alerts do not use for something else, to avoid it.

People¶

An Admin adds Users to a Team by hand and removes them, through the API (addTeamUser, removeTeamUser). A User may be in several Teams. The list of a Team's people shows each person's Role, how they are in the Team — by_hand for a person an Admin added — and the messengers they linked.

  • Disabling a User keeps them in their Teams.
  • Deleting a User takes them out of every Team.

Working hours¶

Working hours are days of the week, a start and an end on the 24-hour clock, and a time zone. The end is later than the start on the same day: Working hours never span midnight. Holidays are not part of them.

The Organization's Working hours
Monday to Friday, from the start of the working day — the time at which the Snooze presets end, 09:00 by default — to 18:00, in the Organization's time zone (organization.working_hours). An Admin changes the days (working_days) and the end (workday_end) with the Organization's settings; an end that is not later than the start is refused.
A Team's Working hours
A Team's own Working hours, with days, a start, an end and an IANA time zone such as Europe/Berlin (own_working_hours), else the Organization's. A Team without its own moves with the Organization: a new start of the working day moves the Team's start too.
