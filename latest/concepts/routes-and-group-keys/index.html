Routes and Group keys¶

A Route decides what happens to an Alert after Muster has read it: which Alert Group it joins, who is told, and how the Alert Group is handled afterwards. A Group key decides which Alerts share an Alert Group. Together they are the two settings that shape what your team sees.

Do not confuse a Route with an Alertmanager route. An Alertmanager route is a rule inside Alertmanager that picks a receiver and a repeat interval. A Route of Muster is evaluated after the Alert has arrived, on the labels of the Alert.

How an Alert is routed¶

  1. The Alert arrives in a Snapshot of an Integration.
  2. The Integration's Static labels are added to it. If the Alert already has a label of that name, its own value wins, and Muster warns about the label.
  3. Muster walks the Routes in list order. The first Route whose Matchers all match takes the Alert.
  4. The Route's Group key says which Alert Group the Alert goes to. See Alert Groups.

Only an Alert that starts firing is routed. An Alert that already lives in an open Alert Group stays in it.

Routes¶

A Route has:

  • a name, unique in the Organization, and a description;
  • Matchers, which pick the Alerts;
  • a position in the list;
  • a Minimum severity;
  • a Group key;
  • the Destinations that receive its Alert Groups;
  • policy fields for how its Alert Groups are handled: the Reopen window and the Grace period, whether a rise to Urgent removes an acknowledgement, the Snooze durations, the Thread batching window and the Storm threshold, the templates and the language, and the ack timeout, the Reminders and auto-unacknowledge.

Matchers¶

A Matcher is a condition on one label, written as in Alertmanager: =, !=, =~ and !~, where the last two take an RE2 regular expression. The Matchers of a Route are all required: they combine with AND. The Route editor builds them from fields; the API takes the same structure; an invalid expression is refused with a pointer to the field.

team="payments" and severity="critical"
Takes Alerts of the payments team that are critical.
namespace=~"prod-.*"
Takes Alerts in any namespace that starts with prod-.
alertname!="Watchdog"
Takes everything except the Watchdog.

A missing label counts as an empty value. So team!="payments" also matches an Alert that has no team label.

In the Route editor, under Matchers:

  1. Select Add matcher. A row appears with the label, the operator and the value.
  2. Enter the label, such as team, choose the operator, = by default, and enter the value, such as payments. The × at the end of a row removes it.

Order, and the Default route¶

Routes are evaluated in list order, and the first Route that matches takes the Alert. Put the narrow Routes first and the broad ones after them. Drag the Routes on the Routes page to reorder them.

The Default route is always last. It has no Matchers, cannot be deleted or moved, and takes every Alert that no other Route took. A small installation may keep only the Default route. A Route with no Destinations is fine too: its Alert Groups exist in Muster and in no messenger.

Edits to Routes use optimistic locking: if somebody changed the Routes while you were editing, your save is refused and you reload.

Route profiles¶

When you create a Route, you choose a Route profile. A profile only fills in the settings; it is not stored, and you can change every field afterwards. There are two:

Ack timeout (route.ack_timeout)
On-call: on; first notice after 15 minutes · Informational: off
Reminders (route.reminders)
On-call: on; first after 4 hours · Informational: off
Snooze durations (route.snooze_durations)
On-call: 1 h, 4 h, 24 h · Informational: 1 d, 3 d, 7 d
Group key (route.group_key)
On-call: alertname, severity, cluster · Informational: the same
Reopen window and Grace period
On-call: 15 minutes each · Informational: the same

Choose Informational for alerts nobody needs to act on at once, such as a certificate that expires in a month: no notices, no Reminders, and long Snooze durations. Choose On-call for the rest. See Statuses and commands for what the timers do.

Minimum severity¶

The Minimum severity of a Route (route.minimum_severity: none, warning or critical; none by default) is the lowest Severity level of its Alert Groups. An Alert Group's Severity level is the highest Severity level of its firing Alerts, raised to the Minimum severity of its Route. The Minimum severity only raises: it never lowers an Alert Group, and it never changes the level of an Alert, which the Alerts of the Alert Group page, the Alerts view and the alerts[] of outgoing webhook events still show as received.

Use it for a Route whose Alerts all matter as much as a critical one, whatever their severity label says: with the Minimum severity critical, a warning Alert of the Route starts a critical, and so Urgent, Alert Group. The Route that Muster suggests for MusterHeartbeatLost has the Minimum severity critical.

A changed Minimum severity applies at once to the open Alert Groups of the Route, Quietly. A raised one records a Quiet severity_raised, or the Quiet urgency_raised of an Alert Group it makes critical, with the reason "Minimum severity of the Route"; it never removes an acknowledgement or a Snooze. A lowered one records nothing and lowers their level, as a falling Severity level does. The Root messages show the new level.

By default the severity label maps to a Severity level: critical is critical, warning is warning, info and none are info, any other value counts as warning and is shown as received, and an Alert without the label counts as info. The Organization can change the label and the mapping.

Urgent¶

An Alert Group is Urgent when its Severity level is critical — from its Alerts, or from the Minimum severity of its Route. An Urgent Alert Group is posted first when many arrive at once, is published on its own during a Storm, and a rise to Urgent can end a Snooze or remove an acknowledgement, where the Route's "rise to Urgent removes the acknowledgement" is on.

From 0.2.0 the urgent mark of a Route and the Organization's "critical is Urgent" no longer act: a Route marked urgent got the Minimum severity critical, and a critical Alert Group is always Urgent. The API keeps urgent on Routes and critical_is_urgent on the Organization until 0.3.0. A Route request with urgent: true and no minimum_severity, as a 0.1 client sends it, sets the Minimum severity critical; urgent: false, urgent beside minimum_severity, and critical_is_urgent change nothing. See the upgrade notes of 0.2.0.

Group keys¶

The Group key of a Route is a list of label names. Alerts of the Route that have the same values for all of those labels go to the same Alert Group. A label that an Alert lacks counts as an empty value, so Alerts that all lack cluster group together.

The default Group key is alertname, severity and cluster. Take these four Alerts, all taken by one Route:

Alert alertname severity cluster namespace pod
1 KubePodCrashLooping warning prod payments api-1
2 KubePodCrashLooping warning prod payments api-2
3 KubePodCrashLooping warning prod search web-1
4 KubePodCrashLooping warning staging payments api-1
Group key Alert Groups Which Alerts
alertname, severity, cluster (default) 2 1, 2 and 3 in one; 4 in another
alertname, cluster, namespace 3 1 and 2; 3; 4
alertname, cluster, namespace, pod 4 one Alert Group per pod
alertname 1 all four

There is no best key. A coarse key gives few Alert Groups that stay open longer, and a person takes the whole problem with one Acknowledge. A fine key gives more Alert Groups and a clearer ownership of each. Pick the labels that tell "who should look at this" apart. A good start is the labels your teams split on: the alert name, the cluster and the namespace or the service.

In the Route editor, the Group key shows its labels in order:

  1. To take a label out, select the × next to it.
  2. To add one, type its name in the field under the labels and select Add label.

Preview before you save¶

The Group key editor previews what a key would do. It reads the Stored Snapshots of the chosen period (24 hours by default, at most the retention of Stored Snapshots; routing.group_key_preview_period) and shows how many Alert Groups the current and the proposed key would produce, with the 5 largest as examples (routing.group_key_preview_examples). It covers the Alerts that the Route would take at its place in the current order, each Alert once, and it works for a Route you are still creating. Use it before every change of a key.

  1. Change the Matchers or the Group key in the Route editor.
  2. Under Group key preview, choose the Period, 24 hours by default, and select Preview.
  3. Compare Current, the saved key, with Proposed, the key in the editor: each table lists the largest Alert Groups with the values of the key's labels and the number of Alerts. A new Route has only the proposed side. When you change the Matchers, the key or the period after a preview, the preview says so; select Preview again.

Do not put Instance labels in the key¶

Instance labels change when the process that reports an Alert restarts, such as pod and instance. If one is in the Group key, every restart of a pod starts an Alert Group of its own and the old one stays open. Keep pod and instance out of the key unless one Alert Group per pod is what you want. When it is, restarts cause no trouble, since the Alert Group for a pod fires and resolves with that pod. Otherwise leave the labels out of the key, so that the restarted pod's Alert is recognised as a Replacement inside the same Alert Group. See Alert Groups and Instance labels in informational alerts.

Static labels¶

Static labels belong to an Integration, not to the Route. Muster adds them to every Alert of the Integration before it routes it, so the Matchers and the Group key can use them like labels the rules set. Their usual job is to name what the Alertmanager cluster knows and the rules do not, such as cluster=prod-eu. That is why the default Group key can include cluster: set it as a Static label on each Integration, and Alert Groups of different clusters stay apart even when the alerting rules never mention the cluster. See Static labels.

Create a Route¶

  1. Open Routes and select Create route. Choose a Route profile, On-call or Informational; Choose another profile takes you back.

  2. Enter a Name and, if you like, a Description. Add the Matchers and check the Group key with its preview.

  3. Under Destinations, choose each Destination in Add a Destination. The list shows each one with its type and health; a Route without Destinations sends no messages.

  4. Check the policy below, filled in by the profile: Lifecycle (the Reopen window and the Grace period), Delivery (the Thread batching window and the Storm threshold), Snooze durations, Ack timeout and Reminders — see Statuses and commands — and Message, which Messages and templates explains.

  5. Select Create. The Routes list opens with the new Route just above the Default route, with its Matchers, its Group key and its open Alert Groups; drag it, or use its arrows, to change the order. Above the list, Muster may suggest Routes for its own alerts.

Examples¶

Only the Default route. Every Alert goes to one Route, grouped by alertname, severity and cluster. Add one Destination to the Default route and everything reaches one place. This is the setup of the Quick start plus a messenger.

Pages for payments. Create the Route payments-critical with the Matchers team="payments" and severity="critical", the On-call profile and a Destination that is the payments team's channel. Put it above the Default route. Critical alerts of the payments team reach their channel and are Urgent. Their other alerts fall through to the Default route.

Certificates. Create the Route certificates with the Matcher alertname=~"Cert.*", the Informational profile and a Destination for the platform channel. These Alert Groups send no ack timeout notices and no Reminders, and they offer Snooze for a day, three days or a week.

Muster's own alerts. The Internal alerts, such as MusterDestinationBroken, are Alerts of the built-in "Muster" Integration and are routed by Routes like any other. Route them to a Destination of another kind than the one that failed. The Routes page offers a Route for alertname=~"Muster.*" once you have a Destination, and a Route for MusterHeartbeatLost when an Integration has a Heartbeat. See the Internal alerts.

What changes when you edit a Route¶

  • Settings, such as the timers, the templates and the Snooze durations, apply at once, also to open Alert Groups, whose messages are brought up to date.
  • A new Group key or new Matchers apply to Alerts that start firing afterwards. A fingerprint already in an open Alert Group stays there until that Alert Group is resolved.
  • A changed Minimum severity raises or lowers the Severity level of the open Alert Groups, Quietly.
  • No edit changes the status of any Alert Group.
  • Adding a Destination to a Route gives it the Route's open Alert Groups, Quietly. Removing one gives each open Root message one last edit that says it is no longer updated and links to Muster.
  • Deleting a Route that has open Alert Groups is refused. You can move them to the Default route first.
