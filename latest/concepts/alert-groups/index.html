Alert Groups¶

An Alert Group is a set of Alerts that Muster treats as one problem. It is the thing people act on: it has a number like #412, a status, and at most one Owner, and each Destination that receives it shows it as a single message that Muster keeps up to date. Acknowledge it, snooze it or resolve it, and everyone looking at it sees the result.

This page explains what an Alert Group is and how it relates to the other things Muster handles. The pages that follow go deeper:

  • Routes and Group keys: how an Alert finds its Alert Group.
  • Snapshot semantics: how Alertmanager's notifications become Alerts, and when an Alert counts as gone.
  • Statuses and commands: the four statuses, the commands, ack timeouts and Reminders.
  • Loud and Quiet: which changes ring a phone, and whom they Mention.
  • Broken Destinations: what happens when a messenger stops answering.

The things Muster handles¶

Four words sound alike and are not the same. Muster keeps them apart:

Term What it is
Snapshot One notification from Alertmanager. It lists the current Alerts of one Alertmanager group. It is input, not an Alert.
Alertmanager group The alerts Alertmanager notifies about together under one groupKey. It is Alertmanager's idea of "together", decided by the group_by of its route.
Alert One firing problem reported by a source, identified by its full set of labels, such as one pod, one node or one domain.
Alert Group The problem Muster shows people. It contains Alerts, and which ones go together is decided by a Route and its Group key, not by Alertmanager.

Muster does not copy Alertmanager's grouping. Alertmanager's groups exist to batch notifications. An Alert Group exists so that a person can say "I have this one". The two often look alike, but you can change either without the other.

What an Alert Group has¶

Attribute Meaning
Number, #N Assigned per Organization, without gaps, when the Alert Group is created. It is what people say aloud: "I took #412".
Status firing, acknowledged, snoozed or resolved. See Statuses and commands.
Owner The user who acknowledged it. Only a user can be an Owner; a Service account cannot.
Title The alertname that its Alerts share. When Alerts with different alertname values join it, the Group key values instead, such as cluster=prod, namespace=payments. It is set when the Alert Group is created and changes at most once, at that switch.
Summary The summary annotation of the first of its Alerts that has one. It is set once.
Route The Route that took its Alerts. It decides the Destinations and the timers.
Severity level The highest Severity level of its firing Alerts: critical, warning or info.
Urgent Set when its Route is marked urgent, or when its Severity level is critical and the Organization treats critical as Urgent (organization.critical_is_urgent, on by default).
Reopen count How many times Muster reopened it.
Alerts One entry per fingerprint, firing first, resolved ones struck through with their reason.
Timeline The ordered record of everything that happened to it.

The Alert Group page shows all of this, the Alerts with their labels and annotations, the labels they share, and the previous Alert Groups with the same Route and Group key values. The page and the list work at phone width.

How Alerts join an Alert Group¶

When an Alert starts firing, Muster picks its Route and reads the values of the Route's Group key from the Alert's labels. Then, in this order:

  1. If an open Alert Group of that Route has the same Group key values, the Alert joins it.
  2. Otherwise, if the system resolved an Alert Group of that Route with the same values within the Reopen window, that Alert Group reopens.
  3. Otherwise a new Alert Group starts, with the next number.

An Alert that is already in an open Alert Group is not routed again: it stays there until the Alert Group is resolved, even if you change the Route's Matchers or Group key meanwhile.

What a new Alert does to an Alert Group depends on its status, because the status says whether anybody is paying attention:

The Alert Group is A new Alert joining it is
firing a Loud event, added to the Thread in batches
acknowledged a Quiet event; the acknowledgement stays, always
snoozed a Quiet update of the Root message only; what accumulated is listed when the Snooze ends

Replacements¶

An exporter restarts and reports the same problem with a new pod label. That is a different Alert, since its labels differ, but it is the same problem. When a new Alert differs from a firing Alert of the same Alert Group only in Instance labels, Muster records a Replacement: a Quiet event in the Timeline that names the label that differs. The old Alert resolving is Quiet too. The Alert Group page suggests the real fix, which is to aggregate the label away in the rule, for example with without(pod, instance).

Instance labels¶

Instance labels are the labels that change when the process reporting an Alert restarts. The Organization lists them in organization.instance_labels; by default pod, instance, container and endpoint. See Instance labels in informational alerts for when to keep them out of your rules.

Reopening¶

An Alert Group is resolved by the system when its last Alert resolves, whether Alertmanager said so, or the Alert went Gone or Stale, or its Integration was deleted. A person can resolve it too.

The two ways to resolve differ in what happens when the problem returns:

Resolved by When an Alert with the same Route and Group key values fires again
The system Within the Reopen window (route.reopen_window, 15 minutes by default) the same Alert Group reopens: same number, Reopen count plus one, and it returns to the status it had before, with the same Owner if it was acknowledged. After the window, a new Alert Group starts.
A person The Reopen window does not apply. If the Alerts that were still firing are still reported when the Grace period (route.grace_period, 15 minutes by default) ends, they are grouped again as if they had just fired. Within the Grace period, a new firing starts a new Alert Group at once.

A reopen is keyed by the Route and the Group key values, not by one Alert: any Alert with the same values brings the Alert Group back, whether it is one of its own Alerts or a new one. How loud a reopen is depends on where it returns: Loud into firing, Loud into acknowledged but Mentioning only the Owner, Quiet into snoozed.

A person who resolved the wrong Alert Group presses Unresolve in the web UI or the API. Unresolve is not offered in messengers, and it works only on an Alert Group that a person resolved, not on one that reopens by itself.

The reason is always visible. A resolution by the system carries the reason, such as "Alertmanager no longer reports this alert", and so does every other automatic change; the page, the Timeline and the message show it.

Where an Alert Group shows up¶

A Route lists the Destinations that receive its Alert Groups. For each Alert Group and each of its Destinations, Muster keeps one Root message and brings it to the latest state each time something changes. Replies, such as new Alerts joining or a Reminder, go to the message's Thread. See Loud and Quiet for which changes are new messages and which are edits.

An Alert Group whose Route has no Destination, as with the Default route in the Quick start, exists only in Muster. Its page, the list, the statistics and the Timeline still work.

The Timeline¶

The Timeline of an Alert Group shows, in order: Alerts added, resolved, replaced and continued; status changes with the actor and the Transport; Reopens; Notes; Takeovers; ack timeout notices, Reminders and their answers; use of the Fallback template; delivery events such as a Publication, a possible duplicate or a delivery that was Not delivered; moves to the Default route; and the periods when Muster itself was down. Each entry that records something Muster might tell people also shows whether it was Loud and whom it Mentioned.

You can filter the Timeline by kind and read it oldest first or newest first. Notes belong to it: Responders and Admins can add one, up to 4,000 characters (alert_group.note_max_length).

Changing a Route does not change an Alert Group¶

Edits of a Route's settings apply at once, also to its open Alert Groups, whose messages are updated. They never change an Alert Group's status. A new Group key or new Matchers apply only to Alerts that start firing afterwards.

A Route that still has open Alert Groups cannot be deleted. Muster refuses and offers to move them to the Default route. A moved Alert Group keeps its Group key values, takes no new Alerts, and never reopens; it stays open until its Alerts resolve or a person resolves it.

How long Alert Groups are kept¶

What Kept for
Alerts in Alert Groups, Timeline entries, delivery events retention.alert_details, 90 days
The summary row of an Alert Group: number, title, summary, times, Route, status, and its Notes retention.alert_group_summaries, 2 years

After the details are removed, search still finds the Alert Group by its title and number, and its page shows the summary with the notice that the Alerts and the Timeline were removed. The list shows Alert Groups whose lifetime overlaps the chosen time range, 7 days by default (alert_group.list_range), so an Alert Group that has been firing for weeks is always in the default view. Searching by number ignores the range.
