Broken Destinations¶

A Destination is Broken when Muster has decided that it cannot deliver there until something changes: the messenger refused the bot for good, or the messenger kept failing until Muster ran out of patience. A Broken Destination is not an error to dismiss. It means that the people it serves are not seeing their alerts, so Muster raises an alert about it.

This page explains when a Destination becomes Broken, what Muster does meanwhile, and how it recovers. For the steps when MusterDestinationBroken fires, use the runbook.

When a Destination becomes Broken¶

Every failed attempt is sorted into a class, and the class decides what happens:

Retry after

examples: the messenger answers 429 and says how long to wait

Muster waits exactly that long and then sends the latest state. It is not counted as an attempt.

Transient

examples: a 5xx, a timeout, a network error

Muster retries with exponential backoff and jitter, from 2 seconds up to 5 minutes. After 10 attempts within 30 minutes (delivery.transient_budget) the Destination becomes Broken as unavailable.

Fatal

examples: the messenger refuses the bot or the request for good

The Destination becomes Broken at once.

Unknown

examples: an answer Muster does not recognize

Not retried. That one delivery ends as Not delivered, and the Destination stays healthy.

What is Fatal depends on the messenger:

  • Mattermost: 401 or 403, such as an invalid token or a bot removed from the channel, and 404 for the channel, which is not found or archived.
  • Slack: a token that Slack refuses (invalid_auth, token_revoked, token_expired and the like), a missing scope, and a channel the bot cannot use: channel_not_found, not_in_channel for a bot removed from the channel, and is_archived. A 429 is not Fatal: Muster holds the whole Connection for the seconds Slack asks.
  • Telegram: 401, and "bot was kicked" or missing rights, such as a bot that lost its admin rights in the channel.
  • Bridge: the bridge answers forbidden, for example because the bot was removed from the channel; the bridge's own text becomes the reason. A bridge that does not answer at all becomes Broken through the Transient budget.
  • Outgoing webhook: 401, 403, 404 and 410, and a request that the Organization's outbound address policy blocks, since every later request would be blocked too.

Several things look like trouble and do not make a Destination Broken:

  • A deleted Root message. Muster posts the Alert Group again once, Quietly, with the note "The previous message was deleted at HH:MM"; if that one is deleted too, the pair is marked "deleted in the messenger" and left alone. In Slack this holds while the bot is still in the channel and the channel is not archived; see Edits and deleted Root messages.
  • A Telegram Thread that lost the automatic copy of its Root message. The delivery state shows "Thread not attached".
  • A delivery that ends as Not delivered, such as a template that failed or a request the receiver rejected with 400.
  • A Bridge Connection whose bridge cannot be reached. The Connection does not become Broken itself; its Destinations do, through their own deliveries, and its Connection check shows the cause.
  • Failed polls of a bridge or of Telegram. The poller backs off.

What you see¶

  • The Destination and its Routes show a banner: "Broken since HH:MM: {reason}. Muster tries again every 5 minutes." The reason is the error the messenger answered, or "unavailable after repeated failures" and the last error.
  • The Internal alert MusterDestinationBroken is raised, with the severity critical and the labels destination and destination_name. Muster routes it by your Routes like any other Alert, so a Route for Internal alerts should lead to a Destination of another kind than the one that failed: if all your Telegram Destinations are Broken because Telegram is unreachable, an alert that goes to Telegram says nothing. See Routes and Group keys.
  • The chart rule MusterDestinationBroken is the backup for when Muster cannot deliver its own alert. Send it through an Alertmanager route that bypasses Muster. See Internal alerts and the metrics.
  • The Alert Groups whose delivery waits show a delivery problem. The Alert Group list has the filter Delivery problem, and the page of each shows the state "Waiting: {Destination} is Broken".
  • The System status page lists the Broken Destinations and since when.

What Muster does meanwhile¶

Everything except the delivery to that Destination goes on: ingestion, routing, Alert Groups, commands from the web UI and the API, timers, and every other Destination.

  • Deliveries wait instead of failing. Nothing is dropped, and Muster does not spend retries on a Destination it knows is down.
  • Muster probes the Destination every 5 minutes (delivery.broken_probe_interval). If a delivery is waiting, the probe is an attempt at the oldest one. If nothing is waiting, the probe is the Destination check, which asks the messenger whether the bot can still reach and post to the channel, without posting. An outgoing webhook has no Destination check, so its next delivery is the probe.
  • A new Alert Group is created and shown in Muster as usual, but its first Publication waits. Its ack timeout does not start until it is published, so a Route whose only Destinations are Broken sends no notices.

Recovery¶

The Broken state ends when any of these succeeds:

  • the automatic probe;
  • Check on the Destination page, which runs the Destination check at once;
  • Send test message on the Destination page, when every step succeeds.

Fix the cause first. Typical fixes are a replaced bot token, adding the bot back to the channel or giving it its admin rights again, enabling comments on a Telegram channel, correcting the address or the proxy of the Connection or the Outbound addresses policy, or waiting out a messenger outage. When the fix is made, press Check; if you do nothing, the next probe notices by itself, with no Alert Group activity needed. The Internal alert then resolves.

What is sent after recovery¶

Muster sends only the current state. It does not replay what it missed.

What happened during the outage After recovery
An Alert Group started and is still firing It is published as a Loud new message.
An Alert Group started and is no longer firing, but still open It is published Quietly.
An Alert Group was already published, then changed Its Root message is edited once to the current state, with no Thread reply.
An Alert Group started and resolved during the outage It is not published; it stays in the Timeline and in the web UI.
Thread replies came due They are not sent.

Urgent Alert Groups are published first, within the Destination's rate limits.

An outgoing webhook in events mode is the exception: it is a stream of events, so every event that came due during the outage is sent after recovery, in order per Alert Group, with none dropped and no age limit. An outgoing webhook in template mode sends only the current state, like a messenger.

If an Alert Group resolved while its first Publication was still waiting for a retry, but the Destination was not yet Broken, it is still posted, Quietly, with the note "Delivered late: started HH:MM, resolved HH:MM while this Destination was unavailable".

Republishing past an edit window¶

Muster edits a Root message for as long as its Alert Group is open, which can take days. Whether a messenger lets it depends on the messenger. This is not about a Broken Destination, but the messages that Muster can no longer edit look similar from the outside, so here is the whole picture.

Telegram
None for the bot's own messages. A bot may edit them at any age, so Muster never republishes because of age.
Slack
None known for a bot's own messages. Whether a window that a workspace sets applies to a bot's messages hours or days old has not been verified; an edit Slack refuses ends that delivery as Not delivered.
Mattermost
The server setting ServiceSettings.PostEditTimeLimit, which is unlimited (-1) by default. Keep the default. If an administrator sets a limit, Muster's edits of older Root messages may be refused, and what Mattermost then answers has not been verified. See Keep posts editable.
Bridge
The bridge declares it in its info, as limits.edit_max_age_seconds, or declares none.
Outgoing webhook
Muster does not know the chat's rules. In template mode an update request that the chat refuses ends as Not delivered or Broken by the usual classes.

For a Bridge with an edit window, Muster stops editing a Root message 60 seconds before the window closes (bridge.edit_window_margin). After that:

  • A change that keeps the status of the Alert Group, such as new Alerts or Alerts that resolve, reaches only the Thread. The old Root message keeps the state of its last edit.
  • A change of status, such as Acknowledge, Resolve, Snooze or a Reopen, makes Muster publish a new Root message Quietly, in the same channel, and reply under the old one: "This message is no longer updated. The current one: {link}". Later Thread replies go under the new Root message.
  • The old message keeps working: a press on its buttons, or a Text command in its Thread, still acts on the Alert Group.
  • The Alert Group page shows "Past the edit window: updated on the next change of status" until then.

A bridge may also refuse an edit before the declared time, with the reason edit_window_closed. Muster treats that as the end of the window, not as a deletion. See Edit windows for the details.

After you delete a Destination¶

A deleted Destination receives one last edit of each open Root message, "No longer updated here; current state in Muster: {link}", and nothing after that. For a Bridge Root message past its edit window, the last edit is a Thread reply with the same text. An outgoing webhook in events mode sends no final event, and its queued events end as Not delivered.
