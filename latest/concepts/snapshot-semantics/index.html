Snapshot semantics¶

Alertmanager does not tell Muster "this alert started" and "this alert stopped" as separate events. It sends a Snapshot: the full state of one Alertmanager group at the moment of the notification, listing every alert it holds. Grafana's built-in Alertmanager sends Snapshots too, through a webhook contact point. Muster works out what changed by comparing Snapshots. This page explains the rules, and why an alert can be Gone or Stale without anybody sending a "resolved".

You only need this page to understand why Muster resolved something, or why it did not. The settings that matter are on Connect Alertmanager, in particular its Repeat intervals, and on Connect Grafana.

From a Snapshot to a message¶

Receiving is store-first. Muster checks the Integration token and the size, stores the Snapshot exactly as received, and answers 202. A worker then processes it, on any replica, and everything that follows is a chain of small steps:

flowchart TD
  am["Alertmanager or Grafana"] -- "Snapshot" --> ingest["Ingest"]
  ingest --> stored[("Stored Snapshot")]
  stored --> processing["Processing"]
  processing -- "Alert changes" --> routing["Routing"]
  routing -- "Route" --> groups["Alert Group"]
  groups -- "lifecycle event" --> desired["Desired state"]
  desired --> worker["Delivery worker"]
  worker --> root["Root message"]
  1. Ingest stores the Snapshot as a Stored Snapshot and wakes a worker. It is kept for retention.stored_snapshots (14 days) for debugging, previews and replay.
  2. Processing splits the Snapshot into Alerts by fingerprint, adds the Integration's Static labels, and applies the rules below. It emits Alert changes: a new firing, a resolution with its reason, a Continuation, a change of annotations. It processes the Snapshots of an Alertmanager group in the order they arrived.
  3. Routing gives each newly firing Alert its Route and Severity level.
  4. The Alert Group takes the Alert: it joins an open one, reopens a resolved one, or starts a new one. Every change records a lifecycle event in the Timeline.
  5. The lifecycle event changes the Desired state of the Alert Group's message in each of its Destinations: what the Root message should look like right now.
  6. The delivery worker brings the actual message to the Desired state with one call, and the Root message in the messenger shows it. See Loud and Quiet for when that is an edit and when a new message.

Processing writes one log line per Snapshot, never one per Alert.

One Alertmanager group, one stream of Snapshots¶

An Alertmanager group is the set of alerts that Alertmanager notifies about together under one groupKey. The Snapshots of one group form a stream, and Muster compares each with the ones before it. Alertmanager sends a Snapshot again each time the group changes and each time the route's repeat_interval elapses, and Muster learns from the gaps.

Two things about Snapshots matter in practice:

  • Copies from an HA pair are harmless. Two Alertmanagers in a pair send their own copy, 0 to 15 seconds apart, sometimes one of them incomplete. Muster processes an Alert once for the same fingerprint, status and startsAt, so a duplicate changes nothing. Snapshots of one group that arrive within the Integration's duplicate window (integration.duplicate_window, 45 seconds) count as one, and an Alert listed in either of them counts as listed.
  • A Snapshot's status is the group's, an Alert's status is its own. A Snapshot with status: firing can list alerts that are resolved, and each alert's own status decides for it.

Resolved¶

The normal way an Alert ends is that Alertmanager lists it as resolved. Muster resolves it at once, with the reason that Alertmanager resolved it, in the latest Alert Group where that fingerprint is still firing.

  • A resolved for an Alert that is already resolved, or for an earlier firing of it, changes nothing. Prometheus re-sends a resolved alert for about 15 minutes, so the same resolved arrives several times.
  • A resolved with an older startsAt than the Alert's current one resolves the current firing when its endsAt is later than the current startsAt: the two firings overlap. The replicas of a Grafana HA pair evaluate the rules each on its own, so their copies of one alert can carry different startsAt, and the resolve of either replica ends the Alert. Without an endsAt, or with one not later than the current startsAt, it is a resolve of an earlier firing. Alertmanager merges overlapping firings of one alert, so with one rule evaluator a resolve it sends of an earlier firing ends before the next one starts. With an HA pair of rule evaluators, such as two Prometheus replicas, it can send one that ends later, and Muster resolves the Alert, as it would with the startsAt that Alertmanager merged.
  • A resolved for a fingerprint that fires nowhere is dropped, and counted.
  • A Snapshot whose status is resolved, sent when all the alerts of its group resolved, resolves every Alert of that group that still fires, also the ones it does not list. After a restart of both Alertmanagers of a pair, such a Snapshot can list only some of them and the others are never sent. A truncated Snapshot from Grafana is the exception; see Truncation.

Gone¶

Sometimes Alertmanager stops listing an alert without saying that it resolved. The rule evaluator may have stopped, an alert may have been inhibited, or the route may have changed. Muster notices that the Alert is Gone:

An Alert is Gone in an Alertmanager group when it has been missing from at least two consecutive Snapshots of that group, over at least processing.gone_min_absence (5 minutes), counted from the first Snapshot that missed it to the latest one.

A Snapshot that lists it ends the count. Both conditions are needed, for good reasons. A restarted Alertmanager starts with an empty memory, and its first Snapshots can leave out a firing alert twice, a minute apart, because Prometheus re-sends firing alerts only every 2 minutes. The minimum absence covers that. A restarted instance of an HA pair is covered by the duplicate window, because the other instance sends the full Snapshot 15 seconds later.

Snapshots of one group Result
Alert A missing at T and T+60 s, listed at T+120 s A stays firing; the count restarts
Alert A missing at T, T+60 s and T+6 min A is Gone at T+6 min
Alert A missing at T only A stays firing; one Snapshot never counts

An Alert that appears in several Alertmanager groups is resolved by absence only when it is Gone (or Stale) in every one of them.

Stale¶

If the whole group sends nothing at all, there is no Snapshot to miss the Alert from. This happens when the group is silenced, inhibited or muted as a whole in Alertmanager. Muster calls an Alert Stale when it has heard nothing about it for too long:

An Alert goes Stale when its Alertmanager group has sent no Snapshot for stale_after, which is processing.stale_after_factor (3) times the repeat interval that Muster has learned for the group's Alertmanager route.

Muster learns the interval from the gaps between Snapshots whose notification_reason is repeat interval elapsed or, from senders without that field such as Grafana, between identical Snapshots, and uses the median of the last 9 gaps (processing.repeat_samples). Until it has learned an interval, stale_after is processing.stale_after_unlearned, 25 hours. Alertmanager sends a repeat on the first group_interval tick after repeat_interval has elapsed, so the gaps are about repeat_interval + group_interval. With a 10-minute repeat_interval and Alertmanager's default group_interval of 5 minutes, Muster learns an interval of about 15 minutes, and a silenced group's Alerts go Stale after about 45 minutes. With a daily interval it takes more than three days, which is why the Integration page warns about an interval longer than processing.long_repeat_warning (1 hour). See Repeat intervals.

Gone and Stale both resolve the Alert with the reason "Alertmanager no longer reports this alert — it resolved without notice, was silenced or inhibited in Alertmanager, or the Alertmanager routing changed". If a silenced alert's mute ends within about 14 minutes, Alertmanager still reports it as resolved, so it resolves normally; after that it only goes Stale.

The Heartbeat¶

Stale resolution has a risk: if the path from Alertmanager to Muster breaks, Muster hears nothing, and every Alert would go Stale, although they may still fire. The Heartbeat removes the risk. It is a regular signal that proves the path works.

  • Stale applies only while the Integration's Heartbeat is live. An Integration without a Heartbeat, or still waiting for the first signal, never resolves an Alert as Stale. Gone still works.
  • While the Heartbeat is lost (no signal within integration.heartbeat_timeout, 5 minutes), the time does not count towards stale_after. From the last signal before the loss until the signal returns, nothing goes Stale. Muster's own downtime does not count either, beyond the timeout.
  • The Integration shows "No contact with Alertmanager since HH:MM", and Muster raises the Internal alert MusterHeartbeatLost.

So with a live Heartbeat and a learned 5-minute interval, an Alert whose group last arrived at T goes Stale at T+15 minutes. If the last Heartbeat signal also came at T, the Heartbeat was lost at T+5 minutes, and the signal returned at T+10 minutes, the Alert goes Stale at T+25 minutes: the 10 minutes from the last signal until its return did not count.

Truncation¶

An Alertmanager webhook configuration with max_alerts above 0, like Grafana's webhook contact point with Max alerts above 0, keeps only the first alerts of a group in each notification, and counts the rest in truncatedAlerts. A truncated Snapshot lists only part of the group, so it can never be evidence that an Alert is missing.

  • Muster does not count a truncated Snapshot as absence. While Snapshots of the group keep arriving truncated, the unlisted Alerts stay alive, which can keep an Alert open that has resolved in reality.
  • Muster raises the Internal alert MusterSnapshotTruncated for the Integration while any group is truncated, and resolves it when none is left. The Integration page shows a warning too.
  • Grafana computes the status of a Snapshot from the alerts it lists, not from the whole group, so a truncated Snapshot from Grafana can say resolved while alerts it left out still fire. Muster recognises a Snapshot from Grafana by its top-level orgId, which Alertmanager never sends. A truncated one with status: resolved resolves only the Alerts it lists as resolved, never its whole group.
  • The fix is max_alerts: 0 on the Alertmanager receiver, or Max alerts 0, the default, on Grafana's webhook contact point. See Send every alert of the group, Connect Grafana and the runbook.

Continuation¶

A Prometheus restart whose first evaluation falls exactly on the alerts' endsAt can make Alertmanager send them again with a new startsAt and no resolved in between. A longer stop gives a resolved and a new firing. That first case is a Continuation: the Alert stays the same, its start time is updated, and nothing is Loud and nothing reopens. Once an Alert has resolved by any route, the next firing of the same fingerprint is a new firing.

Why an Alert resolved¶

Every Alert resolved by Muster carries one of four reasons, which the Integration's Alerts view shows and the metric muster_alerts_resolved_total counts:

resolved
Alertmanager sent resolved, for the Alert or for its whole group.
gone
Missing from at least two consecutive Snapshots for the minimum absence.
stale
Its group sent nothing for three learned repeat intervals, with a live Heartbeat.
integration_deleted
Its Integration was deleted.

When the last Alert of an Alert Group resolves, the Alert Group is resolved by the system with the reason of the last one. See Alert Groups for what happens if the problem returns. If processing ever misbehaves, muster ingest replay reprocesses Stored Snapshots, and because processing is idempotent it changes nothing that was already right.
