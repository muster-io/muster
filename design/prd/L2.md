# Muster L2 — On-call parity

- Status: Draft — the 0.2.0 core is specified; the rest of L2 is specified later
- Date: 2026-10-26

L2 brings Muster to parity with a self-hosted on-call tool: Teams that own their Alerts and channels, who is On-call and
when, Escalation chains that Page more and more people while nobody reacts, calls through Notification gateways, and, in
later releases, more inputs, configuration as code and maintenance. This document is the index of L2: its scope and
releases, the map of the capabilities `C-25`…`C-32` of its core — each specified in its own file under [`l2/`](l2/) —
the conventions those files follow, the changes to the non-functional requirements, what is out of scope, what is still
open and how L2 revises L1. Terms follow [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md); the reasons behind the design are in the
[ADRs](../adr/README.md), above all [ADR-0018](../adr/0018-on-call-model-teams-schedules-and-escalation-chains.md); L1
behaviour is in [L1.md](L1.md).

## 1. Scope

### 1.1 Releases

L2 is `0.2.x` (NFR-11). **0.2.0** is its core, released at once: the Teams and the routing to Teams that installations
with several teams need first, together with Schedules, Escalation chains, Notification rules, Notification gateways,
Assignees, the new Mentions and buttons of Destinations and the compact message layout. A Team model released before
Schedules and chains would have to change again, so it does not come alone in a release of its own. The rest of L2
follows in `0.2.x` patch releases as additive features; breaking changes come only with a new minor version.

### 1.2 What the 0.2.0 core delivers

- **Teams** (C-25). A Team owns Routes, Destinations, Schedules and Escalation chains and gives no access. Alerts belong
  to Teams by the Team label or by Team matchers; people join by hand or through the groups of the identity provider,
  where every mapping entry, to a Role or to a Team, names its claim and may take a subtree `a/b/*` (issue #369). A Team
  has a Team mention per Connection and Working hours.
- **Routing to Teams and by Severity** (C-26, issue #363). Each Destination of a Route or a Team may be limited to
  critical or to below critical; a Route may also deliver to the Destinations of the Teams of its Alerts, with or
  without their Escalation chains; one Alert Group keeps one status and one acknowledgement in every Destination, and
  joins Destinations later with `destination_joined`. Urgent means critical, raised by the Route's Minimum severity; the
  Route's urgent mark and "critical is Urgent" go in expand and contract steps. The Route profile On-call is now Paging.
- **Schedules** (C-27). Rotation layers with Overrides that Responders of the Team make, several Schedules with one main
  Schedule per Team, gaps shown two weeks ahead and reported by an Internal alert, warnings about people who cannot be
  Paged, "On call now" and a personal iCal feed.
- **Mentions and buttons of a Destination** (C-28, issue #367). Mentions become one list of entries — everyone in the
  chat, people, messenger groups, Teams and the On-call of Teams, named or taken from the Alert Group — each with the
  Loud events it applies to, `storm_started` included, limited to what the messenger can mention. Each Destination
  chooses which buttons its Root messages show — in 0.2.0, as journal D567 decided.
- **Compact message layout** (C-29, issues #375 and #390). Title with the status as a word, a grid of fields, the
  summary and the description, the Route's "Labels in the message", the Alerts by their distinguishing label, the
  Assignees or the Owner, buttons and links, in every messenger; bridges get the same layout as structured, plain-text
  data.
- **Notification gateways** (C-30). Bridges in the role `gateway` call people or send SMS at a contact each person
  confirms with a code; an acknowledgement in a call applies only while nobody owns the Alert Group.
- **Notification rules** (C-31). A normal and an important rule per person, of Mentions in the Thread and calls through
  a gateway with waits between them.
- **Escalation chains and Assignees** (C-32). Chains named by Routes and Teams, with steps that Page by Mention in the
  Threads of their own Destinations or call through a gateway, started and stopped where the ack timeout is, replacing
  its notices where they run; undelivered Pages are recorded; the Assignees are the people the chains Paged last.

### 1.3 Adoption

An installation takes the core up in three stages; each is done when the sentence in its last column is observably true.
Every stage works without the next one.

| Stage | What runs | Capabilities | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Teams | Teams name the Alerts; Routes deliver to the operations channels and to the channels of the Teams | C-25, C-26, C-28 (its On-call entries from stage 2 on), C-29 | One Route per category of Alerts replaces the Routes per team, and each problem is one Alert Group with one status in the operations channel and in the channels of its Teams. |
| 2. On call | Schedules say who is On-call | C-27 | Every Team that wants to be Paged has a main Schedule without gaps over the next two weeks, or the Internal alert names the gap. |
| 3. Paging | Escalation chains Page the On-call; Notification gateways call | C-30, C-31, C-32 | An Alert Group nobody acknowledges Pages the On-call in the Threads of its Team's channels — and through a Notification gateway where there is one — and the first acknowledgement stops every chain. |

**Design kept for the `pull` and `agent` modes** — notes for their specification, not requirements. In `pull`, Muster
polls Alertmanager's API every 60 seconds with `GET /api/v2/alerts?receiver=<the Muster receiver>`; an Alert missing
from the API without a `resolved` webhook is resolved by the system only if it is missing from two consecutive polls
**and** Alertmanager has been up for more than 5 minutes (from `/api/v2/status`), so that an Alertmanager restart does
not resolve everything at once. For an HA pair the Integration holds a list of Alertmanager URLs; Muster polls the first
live one and fails over, and "missing" means missing from every live instance. In `agent`, a small `muster agent` runs
next to Alertmanager, polls it locally and pushes Snapshots to Muster over outbound HTTPS with an Integration token, for
networks that allow only outbound connections; it is also the transport of L4 data sources there. Both modes show
Alertmanager silences — "🔕 silenced in Alertmanager until …" — from the Alert's `suppressed` state. Polling is a Leader
task (ADR-0007), and ingestion stays idempotent on `fingerprint + status + startsAt`.

## 2. Conventions

The conventions of [L1 §2](L1.md#2-conventions) apply, with these additions.

**Files.** Each capability of L2 has one file `l2/C-NN-<slug>.md` with the sections of L1; its header names the release
that brings it instead of a rollout stage. Capability IDs continue those of L1: the core is `C-25` to `C-32`.

**Shared tables.** [`l1/reference.md`](l1/reference.md) and [`l1/defaults.md`](l1/defaults.md) stay the single lists of
the product, and the capabilities of L2 add their rows there. Where code checks a table of L1 against itself — the Roles
and Permissions, the metrics catalogue and the chart rules — the rows of L2 stand in a table of their own until the
story that implements them moves them in, so that the checks pass in between; the metrics of L2 are listed in
[Metrics added in L2](l1/reference.md#metrics-added-in-l2). A row of a shared table that L2 changes in place says what
changes "from L2" or "from 0.2.0" in the row itself.

**Provisional.** Items continue the numbering of L1, from `P-62` ([§5.3](#53-provisional-values-and-behaviours)).

**Revisions to L1.** A requirement of L1 that L2 changes keeps its ID and its text, which describes L1, and gets a
marker at its end — `_Revised for L2 (2026-10-26):_` — saying what changes and from which release, and naming the
requirement of L2 that carries the new behaviour; a new acceptance statement in an L1 file starts with `_Added for L2
(2026-10-26)._`. The text before the marker is no longer read alone. [Section 6](#6-revisions-to-l1) lists every revised
ID. A revision made later in L2 adds a marker with its own date.

**Expand and contract.** A change that removes or renames a field of the API or a column keeps the old field, read-only
or derived, until 0.3.0, which removes it (ADR-0006, NFR-11); the release notes of 0.2.0 say what changes and what to
do.

**Events and loudness.** The lifecycle events that L2 records are rows of the [L2 lifecycle event
table](l1/reference.md#lifecycle-events-added-in-l2), with the columns and meaning of C-09.FR-22.

**UI.** The approved mockups of the core (journal D544) are the reference for the pages, their sections and their texts;
the requirements quote the English texts, and the Russian ones follow the translations of the UI.

**Stories.** Stories follow the L1 conventions. `scripts/stories-sync` reads only `design/prd/l1/`; the first story of
L2 extends it to `l2/`.

## 3. Non-functional requirements

The non-functional requirements of [L1 §3](L1.md#3-non-functional-requirements) hold for L2, with these changes.

- **NFR-6 Security** gains: the contact of a person at a Notification gateway is set only by that person from their web
  session and confirmed by a code sent through the gateway; an acknowledgement through a gateway acts as the person
  Muster's signed action data names, never as the contact (ADR-0013); the token of an iCal feed is issued by Muster,
  hashed, shown once and masked wherever its URL would be logged (ADR-0011).
- **NFR-7 Privacy** becomes: Muster sends nothing to the project, except the opt-in telemetry of ADR-0019 — off until
  an Admin turns it on, category by category, and specified later in L2 — and never sends email; the outgoing
  heartbeat of C-19 goes only to the dead man's switch an Admin configures. Deleting a User
  pseudonymizes them and erases their contacts at Notification gateways, which are personal data that the Request log
  masks. Metrics never carry alert labels, users or Alert Group numbers; Teams, Schedules, chains and gateways appear by
  their ids.
- **NFR-11 Upgrades** becomes: during 0.x, `0.N` is layer N; `0.N.x` releases carry fixes and additive features; a
  breaking change comes only with a new minor version `0.N.0`, whose release notes say what to change, and a removal is
  done in expand and contract steps over two minor versions. 0.2.0 stops the Route's urgent mark and "critical is
  Urgent", renames the profile On-call to Paging, moves the Mention settings into entries and changes the default layout
  of messages (C-26.FR-10, C-26.FR-11, C-28.FR-5, C-29.FR-7); 0.3.0 removes the old fields. The rest of NFR-11 stands.
- **NFR-12 Compatibility** gains: Slack Connections may grant the scope `usergroups:read`, which only Team mentions and
  user groups need; bridges declare the roles `messenger` and `gateway`, group mentions and the mentions of everyone in
  the chat in `info`, and Root messages carry the structured `layout` — all additively in version 1 of the Bridge
  protocol.
- **NFR-2 Latency and NFR-4 Availability** cover Pages: a Page by Mention is a Thread reply of delivery, and Escalation
  steps are timer rows that any replica claims, so a lost Leader never stops a chain. The Leader adds only the scan for
  gaps of Schedules and the gauges of Broken gateways and of gaps; it polls the events of gateways in the poll mode with
  the Bridge polling of L1 (ADR-0007).
- **NFR-8 Internationalization**: the built-in texts of Pages, undelivered Pages and Mentions, Assignees and calls exist
  in English and Russian.
- **NFR-15 Phone width** includes the Assignees and the running Escalation chains on the Alert Group page.

## 4. Out of scope for L2

**Removed from the plans of L2:** backup Destinations — the Internal alert `MusterDestinationBroken` routed to another
messenger, and Notification gateways, already reach people (journal D495); rules that map label values to Mentions,
which Team mentions and the Mention entries replace (journal D509); exporting the Alert Group list as CSV, which the
API, the dashboard and the MCP server cover (journal D548); Paging by direct messages and messages about Shifts and
Overrides (journal D503, D504, D534); a bot command that says who is On-call (journal D515); importing Schedules from
iCal, and Overrides that the other person must approve (ADR-0018).

**Moved:** mirroring messenger Threads into the Timeline goes to the start of L3 (journal D522); an acknowledgement,
Owner and status per Team in a shared Alert Group is a change of the Alert Group's state machine planned for L3 (journal
D508, D535).

**Later, without a layer:** holidays in Working hours (journal D541); custom Roles and Teams that isolate access
(journal D464); hold hints, only if hold returns.

**Never:** an importer from Grafana OnCall (journal D489); email.

## 5. Open questions

### 5.1 Test-environment facts

Spikes on the test stands confirm these before the stories that rely on them; the answers become entries of [verified
facts](../facts.md), and their list is under [Pending](../facts.md#pending): whether a Mention in a Thread reply pushes
to a phone in Mattermost, Slack and Telegram (C-32); whether a Mattermost group mention notifies, and in which editions
groups exist (C-25, C-28); Slack user groups, `<!subteam^ID>` and `<!everyone>` from a bot (C-24, C-28); which token
carries GitLab's role claims and whether they list inherited memberships (C-03, C-25); and Telegram's muted discussion
groups and `getChatMember` (C-27, C-28). The Inbound webhook, Telegram groups and forum topics have spikes of their own
with the later part of L2.

### 5.2 Other questions

The questions of the first review of this PRD are decided (journal D567): an Override makes one person On-call over
every Rotation layer (C-27.FR-3); the profile edits the Notification rules with one switch that adds an immediate call
(C-31.FR-5); a bridge may declare everyone-in-the-chat tokens (ADR-0017, C-28.FR-2); the first call through a gateway
goes at once and the rest follow after the window (C-30.FR-4); saving a Team evaluates open Alert Groups again, only
adding (C-25.FR-13); a person who may not be Paged falls through to the Rotation layer below (C-27.FR-4); an
undelivered Mention of a Destination's list goes to the Timeline and the Destination's warnings, and "Page not
delivered" in the Thread is only for Escalation steps (C-28.FR-3, C-32.FR-9); the gap alert looks 7 days ahead and stays
raised while a gap lasts (C-27.FR-7); the compact message has both `summary` and `description` (C-29.FR-1); and the
buttons per Destination are in 0.2.0 (C-28.FR-8).

Where the PRD differs from the approved mockups, it follows ADR-0018, the journal or `CONTEXT.md`:

- the Team page offers the Team label and the Team matchers as one switch; a Team has both, and an Alert belongs by
  either (ADR-0018, C-25.FR-5);
- the lifecycle diagram starts a running chain over on any rise to critical; only a rise that ends an acknowledgement
  or a Snooze starts the stopped chains over, and a rise of a firing Alert Group that nobody owns starts only the chains
  it now meets (journal D555, C-32.FR-5);
- the Override form's "You can give only your own Shift" goes, since an Override names whoever takes the Shift
  (journal D567, C-27.FR-3);
- the message layout mockup has the field "Alerts firing/total" on by default and `category` among the default "Labels
  in the message"; C-29 keeps the four fields and the default labels of journal D543 and issue #375, and the visual
  editor that offers more fields comes later;
- its Telegram tab shows the status only as a word; C-29.FR-1 keeps the status emoji of L1 beside the word;
- it cuts the Alerts before the description when a message is too long; C-29.FR-6 cuts the description first (journal
  D567);
- the Destination and message mockups write "Page not delivered" into the Thread for a Mention entry; C-28.FR-3 writes
  nothing there and records it in the Timeline and the Destination's warnings (journal D567);
- the Home page's "Назначено мне" becomes "I am an Assignee" in English, since `CONTEXT.md` avoids "Assigned";
- the Route mockup chooses one Escalation chain and predates the sub-option "Run the Teams' Escalation chains"
  (journal D557); C-26.FR-2 and C-32.FR-2 let a Route name several chains, as ADR-0018 does;
- the Maintenance windows on the Team and Route pages and the theme switch in the profile are specified later (§1.5).

## Waiting for operating experience

These mechanisms are left out on purpose and come back only if operating Muster shows they are needed. What Muster does
instead is noted with each.

- **Flapping detection.** A flapping alert produces Reopens of one Root message and Thread replies, as long as each gap
  is shorter than the Reopen window.
- **Resolve delay.** The Reopen window already keeps one message per problem; revisit together with flapping.
- **Hold** (delaying publication). Design kept:
  - an attribute `publish_after`, not a status; a Route setting, off by default; never for Urgent Alert Groups;
  - presets 1, 2, 5, 10 and 15 minutes; 15 minutes is the maximum, and the UI explains that longer delays belong in the
    rule's `for:`;
  - a rise to Urgent during hold publishes at once;
  - an Alert Group that resolves during hold is never sent; its Timeline says "resolved by itself after N min", and the
    Route shows how many Alert Groups were held and resolved on their own in the last 24 hours;
  - Acknowledge or Snooze during hold publishes at once, Quietly, in the resulting status; Resolve during hold closes
    the Alert Group without publishing it, keeping its Timeline;
  - the ack timeout and the Escalation chains count from publication;
  - hold hints suggest values from statistics such as time to resolve, without AI, if hold returns.
- **Adaptive Thread batching window.** Stretch the window when limiters are under pressure.
- **CEL expressions for Matchers**, as an advanced mode with OR.
- **Maximum Alert Group age.** Options kept: a maximum age on the Route that re-issues a new Alert Group, or reposting
  the Root message without closing the Alert Group. An Alert Group lives as long as any of its Alerts fires, and
  Reminders keep its Owner aware.
- **A Note required on resolve.** A Route setting that makes Resolve require a Note. Resolve is one action everywhere
  and Notes are optional, from the UI and the API. If the setting returns, messengers need a rule for it: refuse the
  press privately with a link to the Alert Group in Muster, or hide the Resolve button on such Routes.

## Later, outside the layers

Row-level security as a second line of Organization isolation; a schema management tool for migrations; external key
providers (Vault transit, cloud KMS) behind the key provider interface; SCIM; custom Roles built from existing
Permissions; an external ingestion queue for very large installations; reading a repeat-interval hint from the
`routeLabels` that newer Alertmanager versions send; a Telegram Bot API server kind and a wizard that moves a bot
between Bot API servers, if asked for.

**Mobile companion app.** A phone app with push notifications about Alert Groups and their commands, like the Grafana
IRM mobile app, as a client of the API. Its push can become one more way to Page in the Notification rules, beside
Mentions in the Thread and calls through a Notification gateway (ADR-0018). Which layer it belongs to is still open;
until then, people act from messengers and from the web UI, which works at phone width.

Phone calls and SMS come from Notification gateways — bridges that a team writes or runs for its provider; Muster ships
none and never calls by itself. Not at the start: digests. Never: an importer from Grafana OnCall.
