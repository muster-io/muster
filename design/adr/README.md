# Architecture Decision Records

This directory records the architectural decisions behind Muster, a self-hosted alert grouping and on-call service that
sits after Alertmanager. Each record uses Michael Nygard's format — Context, Decision, Consequences — extended with an
Alternatives considered section, and carries a status and a date. Terms follow the glossary in
[`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md).

The records refer to Muster's layers: **L1** — the first release: grouping alerts into Alert Groups, acting on them in
messengers, delivery; **L2** — Teams, Schedules and Escalation chains, more sources and Connection modes, the Terraform
provider and an MCP server; **L3** — Incidents; **L4** — AI-assisted investigation.

Status values: Proposed, Accepted, Deprecated, Superseded by NNNN. A new decision gets a new file
`NNNN-<kebab-slug>.md`.

| ADR | Title | Status | Summary |
|---|---|---|---|
| [0001](0001-agpl-license-cla-and-dependency-policy.md) | AGPL-3.0-only with a CLA, and a dependency license policy | Accepted | AGPL-3.0-only plus a license-grant CLA via cla-assistant.io; strict license allowlist for shipped artifacts; any OSI license, plus CC-BY-4.0 for data-only packages, for tooling; no copied copyleft code; the specifications `api/bridge.yaml` and, from L2, `api/openapi.yaml` under MIT, the Terraform provider MIT in a repository of its own. |
| [0002](0002-webhook-only-ingestion-with-snapshot-semantics.md) | Sit after Alertmanager; webhook-only ingestion with Snapshot semantics | Accepted | Alertmanager webhooks are Snapshots: Gone after two missing Snapshots at least 5 minutes apart (near-duplicates within 45 seconds count once), a resolved Alertmanager group resolves all its Alerts, Stale after 3× the interval learned per Alertmanager route, counting only time with a live Heartbeat; truncation tracked per `groupKey`; a new `startsAt` without `resolved` is a continuation while the Alert is open; ingestion never answers `400` and stores the body as received; from L2, an Inbound webhook takes the body of Alertmanager's `POST /api/v2/alerts` as single Alert reports that turn Stale at their `endsAt`, measured against the real time of receipt. |
| [0003](0003-route-first-then-group-by-label-list.md) | Route first, then group by a list of labels | Accepted | First-match Routes with a Default route; Alert Groups formed inside a Route by a Group key that is a list of labels; Urgent means a critical Severity level, which a Route's Minimum severity can raise for its Alert Groups; delivery to the Route's Destinations and, where it opts in, those of the Teams of its Alerts, each optionally for some Severity levels, conditions combined with OR; Route edits never change a status. |
| [0004](0004-alert-group-state-machine.md) | Alert Group state machine | Accepted | Four statuses; Reopen restores the prior status; manual resolve with a Grace period; Quiet Unsnooze; only a rise in Severity level to Urgent, auto-unacknowledge after two unanswered Reminders where a Route turns it on and, from L2, new Alerts where a Route allows it remove an acknowledgement; Escalation chains start and stop where the ack timeout does and Unclaimed means all of them ran out; a Notification gateway acknowledges only what nobody owns; attached Alert Groups follow their main one; no flapping, hold or maximum age. |
| [0005](0005-delivery-as-desired-state-reconciliation.md) | Delivery as desired-state reconciliation | Accepted | One editable Root message per Alert Group and Destination, brought to the Desired state by the delivery worker with limiters per Destination and Connection; Loud events are always new messages; Storms per Route; a named interactive path for replies that takes limiter tokens first and fails fast; an exhausted retry budget makes the Destination Broken, and recovery sends only the current state; outgoing webhook events are ordered per Alert Group and delivered at least once; from L2, Pages as Mentions in Thread replies and no direct messages, a queue with retries for Notification gateways outside reconciliation, `destination_joined`, Storm summaries per Route and Destination, Mentions as entries with their Loud events, and the Loud/Quiet matrix on the Route. |
| [0006](0006-postgresql-only-storage-and-queues.md) | PostgreSQL as the only stateful dependency | Accepted | PostgreSQL 14 or newer; own thin mechanisms for ingestion, delivery and timers on `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, `LISTEN/NOTIFY` and advisory locks; daily Stored Snapshot and monthly event partitions; no Redis, no queue library. |
| [0007](0007-active-replicas-with-advisory-lock-leader.md) | High availability: all replicas active, one Leader for singleton work | Accepted | Every replica serves and runs all workers, timers included; a session advisory lock with a bounded, pinged lease elects the Leader for the few singleton tasks — polling Telegram and bridges and holding Slack's Socket Mode among them, and from L2 the Schedule gap scan, the refresh of Bridge `info`, the telemetry report and `pull` polling, each safe to run twice; a session-capable DSN; one replica by default. |
| [0008](0008-spec-first-openapi-and-api-conventions.md) | Spec-first OpenAPI and API conventions | Accepted | OpenAPI 3.1.2 subset with oapi-codegen on the standard `ServeMux`; RFC 9457 errors, cursor pagination, ETag/If-Match, `public_id` in URLs, oasdiff gate from 1.0; one repeated `label=<matcher>` filter, omitted-keeps and null-clears on update, ingestion outside request validation, catalogued problem codes. |
| [0009](0009-spa-embedded-in-the-binary.md) | Single-page UI embedded in the binary | Accepted | React SPA built into the Go binary with `go:embed`; TanStack, shadcn/ui on Base UI, orval-generated client, SSE hints for live updates. |
| [0010](0010-configuration-only-through-the-api.md) | Configuration only through the API | Accepted | The database is the only source of configuration, changed through the API; environment only for bootstrap, all `MUSTER_*`, with the database as a URL or as fields and the session connection defaulting to the main one; no provisioning; mandatory master keys never generated by Muster; first admin from env; `--actor` for state-changing CLI commands. |
| [0011](0011-application-level-secret-encryption.md) | Application-level encryption of secrets with a keyring | Accepted | AES-256-GCM with key ids; a Keyring from `MUSTER_SECRET_KEYS` with the active key chosen in the database; activation only when every replica holds the key; a random Signing secret per outgoing webhook Destination; a key canary at startup, re-encrypted on activation; from L2, Notification gateway contacts as personal data that is not secret, and the iCal feed token hashed like every token Muster issues. |
| [0012](0012-template-sandbox.md) | Template sandbox | Accepted | One Go template sandbox for messages, Link rules and outgoing webhooks, with explicitly registered sprout functions and Alertmanager function names; loop and output caps; dry run on save; Fallback template for messages at runtime; a failing outgoing webhook template leaves that delivery Not delivered. |
| [0013](0013-account-links-from-web-session-only.md) | Account links only from a signed-in web session | Accepted | Telegram `/start` token, or a DM code in Mattermost and Slack, all started from the Muster profile; no links from chat, no email matching; Mattermost via REST API and a bot account; from L2, Notification gateway contacts set only by their owner from the web session and confirmed by a code sent through the gateway. |
| [0014](0014-metrics-and-logging-policy.md) | Observability: metric labels, histograms, Internal alerts, an outgoing heartbeat and a closed log catalogue | Accepted | Entity labels by immutable id plus `*_info`, never alert labels or users; `le` histograms only; pull-only `/metrics`; Leader-only DB gauges; Internal alerts and log events as closed registries in code, one name per condition for Internal alerts and chart rules; an outgoing heartbeat from the Leader to an external dead man's switch. |
| [0015](0015-outbound-http-and-ssrf-policy.md) | Outbound HTTP and SSRF policy | Accepted | One outbound client package; no redirects; retry policies by client class; standard or strict outbound address policy checked after name resolution, loopback blocked unless allowed, metadata and link-local always blocked, the proxy's own address checked too; per-client proxies configured in the UI; a Telegram Bot API base URL checked by a dry probe, a stored token sent only to the saved address and a typed one only to the address typed with it, Slack's fixed addresses, WebSocket connections through the same package, tokens redacted from logs, errors and the Request log; from L2, a `telemetry` client class for the opt-in report, and Alertmanager polling and silences in the background and interactive classes. |
| [0016](0016-flat-domain-packages-command-layer-and-architecture-lints.md) | Flat domain packages, a command layer and architecture lints | Accepted | Flat `internal/<domain>` packages and flat CRUD; one dispatcher for the Alert Group lifecycle; CI lints for Organization filtering, write paths, messenger calls, outbound HTTP, secrets, contexts, logging and histograms; from L2, the commands Attach and Detach, system transitions for Escalation chains, Assignees, joining Teams, Maintenance windows and attached Alert Groups, and the Transports `gateway` and `mcp`. |
| [0017](0017-bridge-protocol-for-messengers-without-native-support.md) | Bridge protocol for messengers without native support | Accepted | A Bridge Connection talks to a separate service over an HTTP protocol (`api/bridge.yaml`, `/v1/`, `GET /v1/info` with `protocol_version` and optional features); a JSON envelope with a CommonMark body that the bridge renders; signed opaque button data with style hints; `Idempotency-Key` on every send; an opaque `thread_ref`; events by long polling or signed push, chosen per Connection; error classes the bridge decides; presses confirmed by the bridge and answered by a separate call; text commands in the Thread where there are no buttons; Account links by a token sent to the bot or a code by direct message; republication on a change of status once the edit window closes; the protocol under MIT, with bridge libraries for Go and Python and an example bridge, MIT without a CLA, in repositories of their own; from L2, a `gateway` role that makes a bridge a Notification gateway, group mentions declared in `info`, and `info` re-read by the Leader once its copy is older than `bridge.info_max_age`. |
| [0018](0018-on-call-model-teams-schedules-and-escalation-chains.md) | On-call model: Teams, Schedules and Escalation chains | Accepted | Teams own but do not isolate; membership by hand and from the identity provider, with a claim per mapping entry and `a/b/*` subtrees; Alerts belong to Teams by the Team label or Team matchers; where its Route opts in, an Alert Group goes to the Destinations of all its Teams with one status and one acknowledgement; Team mentions without a fallback; Schedules of Rotation layers and Overrides, gaps skipped; Escalation chains on Routes and Teams that Page by Mentions in the Threads of their own Destinations or through Notification gateways, never by direct messages; Notification rules; Assignees as a set; a Team's Maintenance window holds an Alert Group only while all its Teams are in one. |
| [0019](0019-opt-in-telemetry.md) | Opt-in telemetry | Accepted | Off by default; consent per category as an Organization setting; a preview without confirming each send; a fixed collector through the `telemetry` client class; a daily Leader task; never email; no consent dialog in development mode and tests. |
